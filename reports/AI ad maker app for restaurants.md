# Sell finished ads first, then build the app

Yes, someone has already built this, and much of it is free. Google's Pomelli, Meta's Ads Manager, TikTok's Symphony and your own supplier Higgsfield's Marketing Studio all turn a product photo or product page into finished ads, and food apps such as Lumera sell "dish photo to promo video" for $12.99 a month. So the general self-serve app you first described is the weak version; the answers below describe the strong one.

## Short answers to your 7 questions

1. **Has someone already built it?** Yes (above). Win by being narrower: food-only styles that animate the real dish and never invent it, every ad in English and Spanish, and you checking and delivering each one. A general app on a cheap plan fights free tools, and AI-native tools under $50 a month keep only about 23% of their revenue after a year.
2. **App or website?** A phone-friendly website that owners save to their home screen. Skip the App Store, which takes 15–30% of in-app sales.
3. **How does it make money?** Sell finished packs first ($49 Event Pack, $79 Launch Pack) and $249 a month for done-for-you. Add app plans at $29 and $79 a month later. Ads inside the app would earn only about $15–$75 a month.
4. **Credits, money down, and what Claude does?** Credits are prepaid tokens priced at about four times your AI cost. The hand-made test needs about $30 plus a P.O. box or virtual mailbox, then mostly card fees, on the Ultra plan you already pay for. The app costs under $250 to launch and about $45 a month on pay-as-you-go AI services, not your Ultra account, which Higgsfield's terms make unsuitable as an app engine. Claude Code writes the code; you open and own the accounts.
5. **Which styles first?** Before/after, crave close-up, new on the menu, offer banner, menu carousel, and where we are this week, each in English and Spanish, in 9:16 and 4:5.
6. **How will people find out?** Live demos at World Food Trucks in Kissimmee and the Sanford Food Truck Fiesta, plus three to five before/after demo posts a week. Test Meta ads at $10–$20 a day only once your posts show which style sells.
7. **What's the plan, and what could kill it?** Sell by hand for six to eight weeks as a "concierge" service (you do the work behind a simple order page), and build only after 10 paid orders and 3 repeat buyers. The main threats are free platform tools getting good at food, Higgsfield being both your supplier and your rival, a small customer base that cancels often, and diner backlash against fake-looking AI food.

## Free tools already make generic ads, so compete on food, Spanish and trust (Q1)

### Who has already built it

The closest copy of your idea belongs to your own supplier. Higgsfield says its yearly revenue pace passed **$1 billion** in September 2026 and that revenue from business contracts grew **10× since June**, mostly from online brands making AI ads. Those are the company's own figures ([Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/higgsfield-1b-annualized-revenue-run-rate)). Funded specialists crowd the same space. ### Five places you can beat them

You can't beat Google on price or Higgsfield on models, but you can beat all of them on five things they don't care about.

- **Styles built for food.** Most template galleries are generic e-commerce formats (UGC, unboxing, marketplace), and the few restaurant templates that exist, such as AdCreate's location-and-hours video templates, are add-ons to general tools ([AdCreate](https://adcreate.com/blog/restaurant-food-video-ads-ai-templates)).
- **Truth.** Diners are punishing fake food. Fortune covered "AI food slop" in restaurant ads in September 2026 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/06/ai-food-slop-restaurant-ads-menus/)), a San Francisco café took down its AI menu after graffiti and bad reviews ([NBC Bay Area](https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/local/restaurant-backlash-ai-menu-san-francisco/4115961/)), and Just Eat apologised and paused AI menu photos in October after one showed bacon floating in a soda ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/10/21/just-eat-food-delivery-app-apologizes-for-ai-generated-menu-photo-showing-bacon-floating-in-soda/)). A tool that only relights, animates and dresses up the owner's real photo, following the rule "the food must come from your kitchen; everything around the food can come from AI" ([Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/ai-video-for-restaurants-2026)), and asks "does this match what you serve?" before download turns that fear into a selling point.
- **Spanish-first service.** Osceola County is about **58% Hispanic** ([Census QuickFacts](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/osceolacountyflorida)), and Kissimmee's World Food Trucks park runs mainly in Spanish with 50+ vendors ([Orlando Weekly](https://www.orlandoweekly.com/food-drink/kissimmees-world-food-trucks-serves-up-a-global-showcase-with-a-distinctly-latin-edge-32883873/)). Apps like Lumera and PlateStudio support Spanish, but none found offers bilingual on-screen text, Caribbean and Latin American Spanish copy, and help over WhatsApp. Florida restaurant firms are 34% Hispanic-owned, slightly below the 38% for all Florida businesses, so this is a Florida advantage rather than a restaurant-only one ([NRA](https://www.restaurant.org/NRA/media/Research/Indicators/2026/NRA-Data-Brief-Restaurant-Owner-Demographics-April-2026.pdf)). If you aren't fully fluent, have a native speaker check each Spanish caption until you have a reviewed phrase list for menus, offers and locations.
- **You.** The most-documented owner pain point is time: in a Popmenu vendor survey, 58% of operators named creating marketing content as one of their most time-consuming tasks ([FSR / Popmenu](https://www.fsrmagazine.com/sponsored_content/artificial-intelligence-is-getting-real-results-for-restaurant-marketers)), and Toast now bundles a human marketer into its $499 plan ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/toast-debuts-toast-iq-grow-spring-release-2026)). The market wants it done *for* them.
- **Honest billing.** The category's loudest complaints are charges after cancelling and credits that vanish; AdCreative.ai sits around **3.2 stars from about 4,167 Trustpilot reviews**, with billing a recurring theme ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/adcreative.ai?page=3)). One-tap cancel, no setup fee, no charge for failed renders and an "export everything" button cost you almost nothing. The export button also protects customers if you ever shut down: Icon, a well-funded AI ad startup, reportedly closed in March 2026 without a company statement (secondary reports only) ([TechStartups](https://techstartups.com/2026/03/05/icon-the-ai-ad-startup-shuts-down-after-spending-12m-on-the-icon-com-domain/)).

## A phone-friendly web app beats an App Store app (Q2)

Build a website that works like an app on a phone. The technical name is a "progressive web app": a site the owner can save to the home screen, where it gets its own icon. A native iPhone or Android app is a bad first move. Apple requires credits bought inside an iPhone app to go through its in-app purchase system, which typically takes 15–30%, and says those credits may not expire; a US link to your own checkout is allowed only alongside Apple's system, not instead of it ([Superwall](https://superwall.com/blog/external-checkout-a-b-testing-and-trial-toggles-confirmed-apples-rules-for-ios)). As of mid-2026 Apple charged nothing on those US link-outs, but the district court was still deciding what, if anything, Apple may charge, and the Supreme Court agreed in June 2026 to hear part of Apple's appeal, so treat $0 as temporary ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/04/29/epic-games-wins-reversal-app-store-fee-battle/); [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/06/30/high-court-grants-cert-in-apples-challenge-to-ninth-circuit-contempt-ruling-in-app-store-dispute/)). Google Play's US terms reportedly charge a service fee of about 10% on the first $1M a year even when payment happens outside the app; sources differ on when that started ([Google Play help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15582165?hl=en)).

The web version has none of those costs. Owners can already snap and upload a dish photo from a phone browser, your updates go live instantly with no app review, you decide whether credits expire, and you keep each sale minus card fees. App-store discovery matters little here, because your customers will arrive from your posts, your visits and your ads, not from browsing the App Store. Consider a native app only after you have hundreds of paying users, and even then sell credits on the web.

## Charge for finished packs and done-for-you months, not in-app ads (Q3)

### In-app ads would earn about $15–$75 a month

Rough math: 500 active business users who each open about 30 pages a month produce 15,000 ad views. At display-ad rates of $1–$5 per 1,000 views (one vendor benchmark puts mobile display at $0.25–$5) ([Thrad.ai](https://www.thrad.ai/content/ai-app-rpm-benchmarks-2026)), that is $15–$75 a month, less than one subscription. The ads would also clutter a paid tool and could show your customers a competitor. Skip them.

### Cheap AI-native tools keep only 23% of revenue after a year

The data on cheap AI tools is harsh. ChartMogul's 2025 study found that AI-native tools priced **under $50 a month kept only 23%** of their revenue after a year, tools at **$50–$249 kept 45%**, and tools **over $250 kept 70%** ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-the-ai-churn-wave/)). The groups are small (slices of about 200 AI-native companies) and the link is a correlation, not proof that higher prices cause loyalty, but it matches the pattern of cheap tools being tried once and dropped. The same study found annual plans hold 10–20 points more revenue than monthly ones. Converted to months, 23% a year means losing about 11% of customers' revenue every month. To hold 100 customers at $29, you would need roughly 11–12 new paying customers a month just to stand still (assuming steady churn; in practice most cancellations come in the first three months). Ten done-for-you clients at $249 bring in **$2,490 a month**, the same as 86 self-serve users at $29, with far fewer people to find and keep (my arithmetic, not a published benchmark).

The price ladder you are entering: free tools at the bottom; $9–$25 a month for basic food-photo and video apps (Lumera, FoodShot, Creatify's entry plan); $39–$99 for richer photo tools (MenuPhotoAI, FoodShot's top tier); $249–$499 for restaurant platforms (Owner.com, Toast); and $300–$1,500 a month for freelancers doing restaurant social media ([Boomp](https://boomp.net/resources/questions/how-much-does-social-media-cost-for-a-restaurant)). One owner told Restaurant Business that social-media-only help shouldn't cost more than $500 a month per restaurant ([Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/financing/saving-money-social-media-services)). Food trucks pay **$150** just to work one Avalon Park Food Truck Night ([Eventeny](https://www.eventeny.com/events/food-truck-night-26749)), so a $49 pack to fill that night is easy maths for them. HeadshotPro, a solo-founder AI app, shows a one-time pack model can work for occasional buyers: it sells only one-time packages from $29, with no subscription ([HeadshotPro](https://headshotpro.com/pricing)). Its revenue figures are unverified and conflict.

## Under $250 starts it; Claude writes the code, you own the accounts (Q4)

### Credits are prepaid tokens priced at about four times cost

Think of credits as arcade tokens. A customer pays you $29 and gets 350 credits. Each action costs a set number: a picture 2, a short video 30. Your app keeps a running tally for each customer. Each time it makes something, it pays the real AI company from your prepaid balance and subtracts credits from the customer. Credits let one price cover very different costs, because a short video costs about 15 times what a picture does. If each action costs the customer about 3.5–4× what it costs you, your margin stays near 70% whatever they choose. And because customers prepay, you hold their cash before you spend anything on AI.

| Action | Your AI cost | Credits, at $0.10 each |
|---|---|---|
| Standard ad image (Nano Banana 2.1, 2K) | $0.05 | 2 ($0.20), about 75% margin |
| Image with sharp, readable prices (Nano Banana Pro) | $0.134 | 5 ($0.50), about 73% margin |
| Quick 8-second video (Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash, 720p) | About $0.81 | 30 ($3.00), about 73% margin |
| Premium 10-second video with sound (Kling 3.0 Pro on fal) | $1.68 | 70 ($7.00), about 76% margin |

The image prices come from Google's official price page, the Omni Flash figure is worked out from Google's per-token video price of about $0.10 a second at 720p, and the Kling figure is fal's listed $0.168 a second with sound ([Google AI Studio pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing); [fal](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/kling-video/v3/pro/image-to-video)). These are output prices (the uploaded dish photo adds a small input charge; whether Omni charges extra for audio wasn't checked), and the margins assume the $0.10 pay-as-you-go rate. Plan credits are cheaper ($0.083 on Starter, $0.072 on Growth), which puts plan margins at about 62–71% before card fees.

An app set of 10 images including a carousel, one quick video, a few redos, captions and music costs you about **$1.60** and uses about 60 credits ($6 at the pay-as-you-go rate). It costs less than a Launch Pack because it has one video instead of two, and pay-as-you-go video costs less per second than Ultra credits. A fully used $29 Starter plan costs you about $9–$10 in AI fees. After Stripe's 2.9% + 30¢ plus 0.7% for subscriptions ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/pricing)), you keep about $18, a **63% gross margin** (what's left after direct costs), and more when credits go unused. A fully used $79 Growth plan costs about $29 in AI and $3 in fees, leaving about $47 (about 59%).

Five rules keep credits from turning into the complaints that hurt competitors:

- Let plan credits roll over one month, capped at twice the monthly amount.
- Make top-up packs last 12 months.
- Refund credits automatically when a render fails. Higgsfield's API doesn't charge you for failures ([Higgsfield docs](https://docs.higgsfield.ai/docs/concepts/billing-and-retention.md)); fal's and Google's policies weren't checked, but either way customers shouldn't pay for a failed render.
- Show a preview before any big spend.
- Keep the smallest purchase at about $10, because Stripe's fees take 8.9% of a $5 charge but 4.4% of a $20 one.

Card disputes cost $15 each, plus another $15 if you contest one ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/pricing)), so refunding an unhappy customer fast is cheaper than fighting.

### What you need to put down

For the concierge test, almost nothing. A Carrd Pro Standard page with forms, Stripe buttons and your own domain costs $19 a year ([Carrd](https://carrd.com/pro)). Tally's free form plan accepts photo uploads up to 10 MB ([Tally](https://tally.so/pricing)). A domain runs about $10–$20 a year. Your Ultra plan covers the AI work; $129 a month for 3,000 credits is the only Ultra price every source agrees on ([Krea](https://www.krea.ai/blog/higgsfield-pricing-explained-2026-unlimited-credits-and-real-monthly-costs)). Your connected Higgsfield account showed about 3,900 credits on November 4.

A Launch Pack (6 images, two 5-second clips and re-dos) takes roughly 110–125 credits at about 2 credits per image and 35 per clip, so that balance covers about 30 packs at about $5 each. Those per-model rates come from a competitor's blog ([Creatify](https://creatify.ai/blog/higgsfield-pricing-%282026%29-plans-and-what-you-ll-actually-pay)), so check them against your own usage. The estimate also assumes you make the Spanish version and the extra sizes in your editor, by swapping overlaid text and reframing rather than generating new AI clips, so don't bake prices or words into AI images. Ten-second clips or separate Spanish renders could roughly double the cost, to about $9 a pack and 17–19 packs from your balance. Each month's 3,000 credits cover about 25 Launch Packs or 13 Menu Refresh clients, so ten monthly clients plus a few packs use almost all of it: budget for extra credits, or start phase 2, before you pass about 10 monthly clients.

For the app, expect under $250 to launch. A Florida LLC costs **$125** to form and **$138.75 a year** after that; the annual report is due by May 1, and filing late adds $400 ([Sunbiz](https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/forms/fees/llc-fees/)). Vercel's free hosting is for "personal, non-commercial use", so you need its Pro plan at $20 a month once you charge ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/pricing)). Supabase Pro, which handles logins, the database and file storage, is $25 a month ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/pricing)). Add $20–$50 of AI test credit; Higgsfield's API minimum top-up is $5 ([Higgsfield help center](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/integrations/what-is-the-higgsfield-api)). Stripe has no monthly fee. Fixed costs then run about $45 a month at launch, roughly $50–$150 at 100 users and $200–$500 at 1,000 (the research team's estimates), with AI costs paid by customers' credits. Florida generally doesn't tax pure software subscriptions, but that rests on a ruling for one taxpayer, so confirm with a CPA ([Florida DOR](https://floridarevenue.com/TaxLaw/Documents/16A-014.pdf)).

### Keep your Ultra plan out of the app

Three Higgsfield routes look tempting, and only one might work. Driving your Ultra account from an app runs into several clauses of Higgsfield's terms (updated October 23, 2026). Ultra includes no API access at all ([Higgsfield help center](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/integrations/what-is-the-higgsfield-api)). Credits are "non-transferable" (§9.4), API keys are "for your use only" (§11.3), account sharing is banned (§2.4), and Higgsfield may throttle or queue "automated" use on Unlimited plans (§10.6). Nothing in the terms says subscription credits can power an app for other people ([Higgsfield terms](https://higgsfield.ai/terms-of-use-agreement)). Higgsfield's own app builder can generate images and video, but every user signs in with their own Higgsfield account and "their generations are billed to their credits, not yours" ([Higgsfield help center](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/tools-and-workflows/what-are-higgsfield-apps)). You could never set prices or keep a margin, so it suits only a free demo. The third route is Higgsfield's pay-as-you-go API (an API is a paid connection your app uses to call an AI model). Higgsfield has sold it separately from Ultra since September 2026, and its terms allow "applications for your end users". The same terms, however, forbid being a "pass-through" with no value added and forbid building "products or services that compete with the Service" ([Higgsfield terms](https://higgsfield.ai/terms-of-use-agreement)). Higgsfield sells its own ad tools, so ask its support in writing before building on the API. Until you get a yes, make fal (a marketplace of AI models) and Google's Gemini API the main engines. Keep Ultra for your own hand-made client work, where the terms let you sell and sublicense what you make.

Whatever you choose, have Claude build the app so you can swap models in settings without code. OpenAI shut down its Sora video API on September 24, 2026 ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)). Google began retiring its Veo 3.1 preview models on October 22. Its recommended replacement, Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash, works out to about $0.10 a second at 720p, about twice the price of Veo 3.1 Lite, the cheapest model it replaces (my calculation from Google's price page) ([Google](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/deprecations); [Google AI Studio pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). Higgsfield's API lists lower "starting rates", such as Kling 2.5 from $0.042 a second, but the real price depends on resolution and sound ([Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/higgsfield-api)).

### What Claude can and can't do

Claude Code can write the whole app: a Next.js website on Vercel, Supabase for logins, photos and the credit tally, Stripe Checkout for packs and plans, a job queue so a one-to-five-minute video render never times out, and your styles stored as editable recipes (prompt plus model) that you can tune without touching code. Claude can't open accounts in your name, accept legal terms, use keys you haven't given it, or set your prices. You will create and own the GitHub (where your app's code is stored), Vercel, Supabase, Stripe, fal and Google accounts and the domain. You'll paste keys (secret passwords that let your app use each service) into settings, approve each change, test payments with Stripe's test cards, have your terms of service and privacy policy reviewed, and answer customers. The research team estimates 1–2 weeks of evenings for a one-style prototype and 3–6 weeks for a paid version with five styles, credits and Stripe; that's an estimate, not a benchmark. App builders like Lovable look friendlier, but Lovable's own Stripe guide says payment webhooks (the messages that tell your app "this customer paid") are off by default, and live keys in preview take real money ([Lovable docs](https://docs.lovable.dev/integrations/stripe.md)). Use one only to make a clickable mock-up to show owners.

## Six food styles fit what Meta and TikTok reward now (Q5)

### The algorithms reward different ideas, not more copies

Meta's ad system now finds the audience from the ad itself: Meta tells advertisers that variety in their ads has replaced narrow targeting as "the main way to find relevant audiences" ([Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/the-creative-advantage-unlocking-the-power-of-diversification-with-meta-andromeda)). The catch is that Meta "groups ads that look or feel alike as variations of one creative", and changing only the button text is "iteration, not diversification" ([Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/demystifying-creative-diversification)). Five colour versions of one ad count as one ad; five different styles count as five. TikTok asks for 3–5 clearly different ads per ad group (a set of ads sharing one budget), and at least six for Smart+ Web, its fully automatic campaigns ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en); [TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/best-practices-for-smart-plus-web-campaigns)). Meta's often-quoted "keep 20 ads running" advice is for large online-store sales campaigns ([Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/three-steps-to-optimize-your-performance-with-creative-diversification)). A single restaurant needs a handful of truly different ads live at once, refreshed every week or two, which is exactly what a weekly pack of different styles delivers.

The mix of formats matters too. On organic Instagram, carousels now match or beat Reels on engagement and saves (0.55% versus 0.52% engagement in Socialinsider's 2025 data). Reels still reach more people (29% more than carousels in Metricool's 2026 data), most Reels views come from accounts that don't follow the poster (Instagram head Adam Mosseri, 2023), and single photos are fading, with reach down about 22% in a year ([Showfolk summary of Socialinsider and Metricool](https://www.showfolk.ai/blog/instagram-carousels-vs-reels-reach)). In paid Meta data, promotional hooks lead: "newness", sale announcements and price anchors top Motion's hook list ([Motion](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-hook-tactics)). Its best format by spend is the "offer-first banner", with an 8.6% hit rate (the share of ads that became big spenders), though Motion warns the figure is inflated by the Black Friday season and comes mostly from online stores ([Motion](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-visual-formats)). A 2026 university study of Taboola ads found AI-made ads did as well as human ones as long as they didn't look AI-made; over-polish, heavy saturation and perfect symmetry were the giveaways ([WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/feed/genai-ads-perform-as-well-or-better-than-those-made-by-people/en-GB/11301); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-ads-perform-as-well-as-humans-if-they-dont-look-ai-made/)). Keep AI people out of food ads: **62% of Americans** find AI-created people or voices in ads unacceptable even when disclosed ([Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/713222/americans-aren-sold-businesses-using-ai-advertising.aspx)).

### The first six styles

| Style | What it looks like | What the owner sends | Length and sizes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before/after | The raw phone photo tagged "Before / Antes", a quick wipe to the same dish relit and styled with name and price, then an "Order / Pide" end card | 1 unedited dish photo, dish name, price | 9:16, 6–10 s, plus a 4:5 split-screen still |
| Crave close-up | The real dish fills the first frame, with gentle steam, sizzle or a slow push-in; caption with name and price; order end card | 1–2 dish photos or a 5–10 s phone clip, name, price, order link | 9:16, 6–15 s, plus a 4:5 cut |
| New on the menu / this week only | "NEW / NUEVO" in the first second, the hero dish, price, "until [date]" | Photo of the new item, name, price, dates | 9:16, 6–10 s, plus a 4:5 still |
| Offer banner | Big offer text over the hero dish ("2 tacos + drink $9.99 / 2 tacos + bebida $9.99") and the end date | Dish photo, exact offer, price, end date | 4:5 still plus a 9:16 story (still or 5–6 s) |
| Menu carousel | Card 1 the hero dish; cards 2–3 items with prices; card 4 a combo; card 5 how to order | 3–4 dish photos, names, prices, ordering info | 4:5 or 1:1, 3–5 cards |
| Where we are this week | Truck or dish photo plus a day, place and hours list, "Find us / Encuéntranos" | Weekly schedule, 1 truck or dish photo | 9:16 story plus 4:5 post, remade weekly |

Why these six: a before/after shows the change in one frame, and Before & After sits in Motion's 5–9% hit-rate band for online-store ads (not food data). Food on screen in the first 2 seconds, captions for muted viewers and no logo intro are the basics of restaurant video ads ([adlibrary](https://adlibrary.com/posts/meta-ads-for-restaurants)). "Newness" leads Motion's hooks, and 41% of operators plan more limited-time items in 2026 ([Popmenu via RTN](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/02/research-69-of-restaurants-are-adopting-ai-while-81-increase-digital-marketing-investment/)). The offer banner is the top format by spend in Motion's data, carousels lead on saves organically, and trucks need a where-we-are post every week, since event applications ask for their social handles ([Eventeny](https://www.eventeny.com/events/vendor/?id=31733)). The lengths are my suggestions, within the 6–15 seconds the research points to for food video.

Use the phone-photo-to-finished-ad before/after mainly as *your* ad to owners, since it shows what you do. For a restaurant's diners, offer a food version instead: raw ingredients to finished plate, or delivery bag to plated reveal. Showing diners "our photo vs the AI version" can trigger the AI-food backlash.

Each style should come out in English and Spanish, as a 9:16 vertical master plus a 4:5 version (and 1:1 for carousel cards). Keep text out of the top 14% and bottom 35% of the frame, where Meta's buttons sit, according to secondary spec guides ([AdsUploader](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones)). Use only cleared music: TikTok's Commercial Music Library for TikTok versions and Meta's Sound Collection for Instagram and Facebook versions. TikTok's licence covers TikTok only, so pick each platform's track from its own library, and never use trending sounds in ads ([Fohr](https://www.fohr.co/blog/how-to-find-royalty-free-sounds-music-for-sponsored-posts-on-tiktok)). A seventh style, a review-quote card, can come later, and only with a real Google or Yelp review pasted in. The FTC's fake-review rule covers AI-made testimonials, with penalties up to **$53,088 per violation** ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials); [Federal Register](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-18853.pdf)). Keep motion gentle on pours and cheese pulls, which AI tends to warp ([Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/ai-video-for-restaurants-2026)). Never make a patty look bigger than it is: Burger King was sued over exactly that, and the claims over its in-store menu-board pictures went ahead ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2023/08/29/738216.htm)). Federal deception law still covers social ads, and an advertising lawyer's rule fits every style: "Sell the product you depict, and depict the product you sell" ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/newsevents/news/2026/09/restaurants-have-been-serving-up-fake-food-ads-for-decades-ai-slop)).

### E-commerce styles, with stricter rules

For skincare, fitness and wellness brands, lead with a headline or offer still image (Motion's top asset types are text-only and product-image-with-text statics), a short product demo (8.1% hit rate) and an unboxing (9.8%, though few advertisers run it) ([Motion](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-visual-formats)). Run before/after results only where the brand can show typical results, which the FTC requires ([FTC Endorsement Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)). Text-message and sticky-note mockups are cheap to make and look less like ads. Any AI presenter must be labelled as AI and never passed off as a real customer. That is your own rule, and in New York it has been law since June 9, 2026 for ads shown there, with fines of $1,000 for a first violation and $5,000 after ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-29-new-york-enacts-synthetic-performer-disclosure-law-for-advertisements-including-those-using-generative-ai)). Arcads, Creatify and Higgsfield are strongest here, so treat brands as a later add-on, or serve them through your existing UGC service and your 10 Florida brand leads.

## Win the first customers at truck parks, then test Meta ads (Q6)

### 1,611 licensed trucks in Orange, Osceola and Polk

Your local market is small but concrete. Florida's licence records list **10,115 active food-truck licences** statewide as of November 2026, including **905 in Orange, 235 in Osceola and 471 in Polk**. Kissimmee lists 201, Haines City 100 and Davenport 31, and the core Four Corners ZIP codes 37 ([Florida DBPR public records](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/hotels-restaurants/public-records/), counted by the research team). DBPR's location field may be a truck's base or owner address rather than where it sells, and the counts are current licences only. Florida also has 48,907 restaurant and bar locations ([NRA](https://www.restaurant.org/research-and-media/research/industry-statistics/florida-state-fact-sheet)). That is plenty for a hands-on local service but not for a national software business, so your first channel is your feet. World Food Trucks in Kissimmee gathers 50+ mostly Latin vendors in one place and already posts short video profiles of its trucks on YouTube ([Orlando Weekly](https://www.orlandoweekly.com/food-drink/kissimmees-world-food-trucks-serves-up-a-global-showcase-with-a-distinctly-latin-edge-32883873/)); pitch the park a monthly promo reel for every resident truck. The Sanford Food Truck Fiesta brings 40+ trucks on the second Sunday of every month ([City of Sanford](https://sanfordfl.gov/food-truck-fiesta/)). Bring your phone, photograph a truck's real dish and show the finished before/after ad within minutes, labelled as AI-made.

Copy what worked for the winners. Arcads calls product-led growth (the product selling itself) its number one channel and runs ads made with its own tool ([Founderpath](https://founderpath.com/resources/romain-torres-arcads-10m-arr-ai-video-ads-sales)). Owner.com built its sales funnel on a free "Restaurant Website Grader" that collects emails ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/hubspotification-of-mom-pop-restaurants/)). Your version:

- **Organic posts.** Three to five before/after posts a week of your own demo dishes, in English and Spanish, on Instagram and TikTok, with "a free ad from one photo of your real dish" as the lead magnet (a free sample that collects contacts).
- **WhatsApp.** Offer it for onboarding and help, since 54% of Hispanic adults used it in Pew's 2023 survey ([Latin Times](https://www.latintimes.com/latinos-dominate-social-media-leading-usage-tiktok-instagram-whatsapp-across-us-551034)). No study shows owners prefer to *buy* there, so test it.
- **Cold email rules.** Your 25 restaurant leads and 10 brand leads in this repo can help, under your rules. Each email goes out only with your explicit OK, only to addresses the business published, at most 50 a day, with one follow-up, after checking the do-not-contact list, and with a postal address in every email. US anti-spam law (CAN-SPAM) requires that address, and you haven't provided one yet. Use a P.O. box or virtual mailbox, not your home, since this repo is public.
- **Landing-page rules.** Samples made for a business belong to that business, so the Chef Amanda reel can't appear there without her written OK, and every AI-made example must say so.

### Meta ads only after organic shows what sells

Paid ads should wait until your organic posts show which styles sell. Vendor blogs put Meta's cost for a software free-trial signup at about **$30–$80**, roughly doubling when you optimise for paying customers ([Benly](https://benly.ai/learn/meta-ads/meta-ads-saas-guide); [GrowthSpree](https://www.growthspreeofficial.com/blogs/meta-ads-plg-free-trial-saas-2026.md)). GrowthSpree also puts trial-to-paid at 15–20%, which implies roughly $150–$530 per paying customer. That is my arithmetic from vendor blogs about software in general; no published data shows what ads aimed at restaurant owners cost. A one-off $79 pack won't pay that back; a $249-a-month client can within one to three months. My suggested first test is about $10–$20 a day for two weeks ($140–$280 in total), and that spend is your real measurement. Use your best before/after demo as the ad, labelled as AI-made. Because Meta picks the audience from the ad itself, the ad must name its audience: put "Food truck & restaurant owners / Dueños de food trucks y restaurantes" on screen in the first second. Aim it within about 25 miles of Kissimmee and Davenport (my suggestion). In Ads Manager choose the "Leads" goal with Meta's built-in form (Instant Form), offering a free ad from one dish photo, not the "Boost Post" button, which defaults to engagement ([adlibrary](https://adlibrary.com/posts/meta-ads-for-restaurants)). Stop if a paying customer costs more than about two months of a monthly client's revenue, or more than one pack's margin for one-off buyers (my rule of thumb).

For ads you run for clients, two platform rules apply. Meta's new terms take effect November 20, 2026. They make advertisers responsible for anything Meta's AI creates or changes, even when a feature was switched on by default, and they forbid removing AI watermarks or metadata (hidden labels in the file saying it was AI-made) ([RelevantAudience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/meta/meta-commercial-terms-delayed-to-november-20-2026/)). So switch off automatic "creative enhancements" that could alter the food. On TikTok, tick "This ad contains AI-generated content" for AI-made ads; simple background removal doesn't need the label ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-ad-disclaimers-in-tiktok-ads-manager)). Mind the calendar too. Orlando tourism softened from June 2026 ([BlogMickey](https://blogmickey.com/2026/07/universal-confirms-orlando-tourism-summer-softness-attendance-below-expectations/)), summers bring heat and storms, and hurricane season runs June to November. Your best selling months are therefore probably January–May and October–November. Offer a summer pause instead of forcing cancellations, and keep an "Open after the storm / Abiertos después de la tormenta" template ready.

### What could kill it

- **Nobody pays.** The test exists to find this out cheaply. If six to eight weeks of honest effort can't land 10 paid orders, keep the service business and drop the app.
- **Free tools get good at food.** Pomelli, Meta and TikTok improve every quarter, and 42% of operators in Toast's 2026 survey experiment with AI only through vendors they already use ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/data/restaurant-ai-statistics)). Your defence is the work they won't do: local, Spanish, truthful and done for you.
- **Your supplier is your rival.** Higgsfield can change or stop developer access at any time under its terms ([Higgsfield terms](https://higgsfield.ai/terms-of-use-agreement)), it sells its own ad tools, and models disappear, as Sora and Veo 3.1 just showed. Build on two engines and keep styles swappable.
- **Customers cancel and close.** 42% of restaurant operators said they weren't profitable in 2025, and median margins in 2024 were 2.8% for full-service and 4.0% for limited-service restaurants ([NRA](https://wtop.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/SOI-2026-Report-Watermarked.pdf)). Census counts relayed by secondary sites show only about 12,500 US mobile-food establishments with paid staff in 2023 (a category that also includes carts and caterers), against IBISWorld's modelled 88,000–92,000 truck businesses. That suggests most trucks are one-person shops, likely with little spare cash ([StartBusinessByState](https://startbusinessbystate.com/food-truck-industry-statistics/); [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/food-trucks-in-the-us/4322/)).
- **AI food backlash or a misleading-ad complaint.** One fake-looking burger can cost a client reviews. An AI label "does not correct a false impression about what is actually being sold" ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/newsevents/news/2026/09/restaurants-have-been-serving-up-fake-food-ads-for-decades-ai-slop)), and DoorDash rejects AI-generated or non-representative food photos ([DoorDash](https://get.doordash.com/en-ca/learning-center/photo-rejection)). Make "real dish only" a hard rule in the product, not a setting.
- **Distrust of AI ads.** 49% of Americans view businesses' use of AI in ads negatively and only 19% positively ([Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/713222/americans-aren-sold-businesses-using-ai-advertising.aspx)); a natural, phone-shot look and no AI people reduce that.
- **Your own hours.** Hand-made work doesn't scale, so log hours per order and automate first whatever the log shows you repeat most.

## Conclusion

Owners aren't short of AI tools; they're short of time and trust, and every free tool still asks them to sit down and make the ad themselves. Software matters here first as your margin engine, the thing that lets one creator serve far more trucks than hand-editing allows; only later is it a product strangers find on their own. The concierge phase answers what no competitor data can: which styles owners pay for, what price they accept, and whether they want self-serve at all. If the answer is "just do it for me", you'll have a local service that can grow toward about $2,500 a month (10 monthly clients at $249), a tool that makes it faster, and the app as an option rather than a bet.
