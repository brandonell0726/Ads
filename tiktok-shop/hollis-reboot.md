# Account reboot: "Hollis, the Swamp Ape Next Door"

Researched 2026-11-09/10. The plan for rebooting @bigfootflvlogz as a TikTok Shop affiliate account.

**How it was made:**
- 9 research agents, each fact-checked by a second agent.
- 3 concept designers, scored by 2 judges.
- Both judges picked this concept. The fixes they asked for are already applied below.

**Status: proposal.** Nothing gets generated or bought until Brandon approves the steps in "Before spending anything".

---

## 1. What the research found

**AI Bigfoot vlogs are past their peak.**
- @bigfootvlogs, the account that started the trend: from a 2.2M median per video in May 2025 to 25.6K in Oct 2026 (−98.8%). It still has 1.1M followers.
- 15 of the 21 AI cryptid accounts found have stopped posting. Only @bigfootvlogs and @yetiboo0 still get a median above 10K.
- On YouTube, 44 of the 45 most-viewed AI Bigfoot vlog Shorts are from summer 2025.

**What fails is what we were doing.**
- Hood/chain styling, profanity, hype pitches with an AI-made product, and the same template every time.
- @realaitv got a median of about 800 views over 324 Bigfoot videos, then switched to a different character. @bigfootaintworried gets about 785. Our November median was 129.

**What still works in 2026:**
- **Calm ASMR** with real objects: @yetiboo0's top 2026 video has 873.9K views.
- **Story and continuity events:**
  - @bigfootvlogs' "back from hibernation" (660.6K)
  - "A fan visited me" (357.2K)
  - "Hunting Wendigo", where Bigfoot feeds the starving monster (265.6K)

**Products only beat a channel's normal views when the real product was the punchline to a visible problem.**
- Ice Blankets on @yetiboyfred: about 80× the account median, at the 2025 peak.
- The Bigfoot-themed brand that actually sells (@drsquatch, 1.9M followers) uses real hands on the real product with one joke, no mascot in frame (271.5K).

**Bigfoot as a topic is alive.**
- The *Capturing Bigfoot* documentary (Oct 20, 2026)
- A 1.8M-view "bring back Bigfoot vlogs" meme
- Real-gear Bigfoot hunts on YouTube (Moe Sargi, median about 143K)

**Open lane: the Florida Skunk Ape.**
- It's real Central Florida folklore. Davenport is in Polk County, next to the Green Swamp.
- No active AI Skunk Ape account was found on TikTok.
- The few AI Skunk Ape videos on YouTube are under 55K views and use the generic gorilla face.

**Why the Yeti and the old look are gone:**
- **YETI coolers trademark:** YETI holds a live registration (No. 7,795,592) for coolers and drinkware and enforces it. The US trademark office refused "Utah Yetis" as confusing.
- **Jack Link's duo:** Jack Link's has used a Sasquatch-and-Yeti duo since April 2025.
- **Racist-caricature pattern:** a durag and chain on a dark-faced ape matches the "AI primate" content that WIRED and others reported as racist caricature.
- **The shirt:** profane shirt text breaks TikTok's Oct 23, 2026 vulgarity rule.

## 2. The character: Hollis

**One line:** a silent, lanky Florida swamp ape keeps showing up at Brandon's house at night. Brandon (never seen, just his hands and his voice) fixes whatever Hollis causes, using real products.

**Name:** Hollis. Never "Yeti", "Sasquatch", "Squatch", "Skunkape" or "Bigfoot" in the name or handle:
- SKUNKAPE is a registered mark for soaps and supplements.
- Sasquatch is registered by Jack Link's maker.
- Squatch is a real grooming brand.

**Look** (base prompt):
- An original Florida swamp ape. Photoreal. Human-scale, lanky and long-limbed, with narrow sloping shoulders and a slight tired stoop. **Not** gorilla- or orangutan-shaped.
- **Fur:** damp, matted reddish-olive swamp brown, darker and wet-looking from the knees down. Strands of grey Spanish moss and a few dry oak leaves tangled in it.
- **Signature mark:** one pale grey-white "skunk stripe" from the crown of the head down the back of the neck.
- **Face:** long and narrow, weathered olive-grey leathery skin with dark freckle spots, a low heavy brow, small deep-set amber-hazel eyes with patient drooping lids, a broad flat nose, a long upper lip. Mouth closed. No teeth, no roar, no human face.
- **Hands:** long thin fingers, dark grey palms.
- **Identity anchor:** a faded teal-and-coral striped beach towel with no text, always over his **left** shoulder. Use a real towel Brandon owns.
- **Never:** clothes, chains, caps, name tags or printed text.

**Personality:** deadpan, shy, patient. He hates anything under about 60°F, loves the porch light, and stares at gadgets but never touches them. Never mean, crude or roasted.

**The rule that is also the compliance rule:** *"He doesn't touch my stuff. He just watches."* The AI never handles a product.

**Voice:**
- Hollis never speaks. His one sound is a long, low "hhhmm": Brandon's own exhale, pitched down. It costs nothing and isn't AI.
- All words are Brandon's natural voice: calm, dry, a tired neighbor narrating a nature documentary about his own backyard.
- No slang, no hype, no profanity.

**Lines he can use** (rotate them, never word for word every time):
- "He's back."
- "He doesn't touch my stuff. He just watches."
- "Anyway. Here's what actually fixed it." (cues the cut to real footage)
- "Leave the porch light on." (sign-off)

**Supporting cast** (keep it small, one AI character per frame):
- **Brandon:** never seen. Only his hands, his house and his voice.
- **The gator:** an old gator in the retention pond. Lore posts only, never with a product.
- **The HOA:** handwritten notices Brandon makes as props.

**Fixes from the judges, already applied:**
- Hollis's hook shots happen **inside the real rooms, at eye level, medium or close**: asleep on the dryer, in the driver's seat with the dome light on, at the sliding door. **He fills at least 40% of frame 0**, with his face and stripe readable. Bright porch light, plus on-screen hook text.
- The grainy "porch cam" look is **only for lore posts**, with no clock timestamps. **Never put a "cam" overlay** in a video that sells lights or anything camera-like, so it can't read as product footage.
- Measure Brandon's real height before setting Hollis's scale.

## 3. The formats (rotate them, never one template)

In every product video, real footage of the real product in Brandon's hands fills **75–90% of the runtime**. The product is on screen by **3 seconds at the latest**.

| Format | Length | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Case File** (problem → real fix) | 22–28s | 0–2s: Hollis causes or reveals a house problem (AI, labelled, no product). 2–3s: hard cut to the same problem in the real house, Brandon names the product. 3–16s: real demo at real speed. Optional AI cutaway of 2.5s or less, no product. Real CTA with "#ad" spoken and on screen. |
| **Quiet Hours** (night ASMR) | 24–30s | 0–2s: real macro of the product's most satisfying action. 2–4.5s: Hollis asleep on the lanai (AI). Then real, close, tactile handling with a whispered voiceover. No music. |
| **Gear Check** (lights people would use to find him) | 20–26s | Real night tests of headlamps, lanterns or flashlights on the real tree line, exposure locked. Hollis appears only in separate AI shots, with no beam effects. Running gag: "Found: zero Hollis." |
| **Eyeline Match** (micro) | 12–18s | 0–1.2s: Hollis stares at something just off frame. Hard cut on his eyeline to the real product in Brandon's hand. Fast real demo, 3–4 shots. |
| **HOA Notice** | 15–25s | Opens on a real handwritten paper notice ("Re: the large animal on your lanai") instead of the creature, then the real fix. |
| **Lore Drop** (no product, 2–3 a week) | 18–30s | AI-only mini stories with Brandon narrating: "Not a guy in a suit", "First cold front (54°F)", "The HOA letter", "Hollis missing / he's back", "Hollis and the gator". No product link and no #ad, but still labelled AI. |

**On every post:**
- A "Polk County sighting #N" counter.
- At the cut, a varied spoken line like "Hollis is AI. The vent kit is real."
- An on-screen badge, **"Real product. My hands. No AI on this part."**, used only when literally true. No Topaz or AI cleanup on those clips.
- "…says the listing" after every spec Brandon hasn't measured himself.
- Honesty lines like "I didn't have a dead battery, so I'm not pretending."

**First post: Episode 0, "Not a guy in a suit."** Hollis pats his own spine looking for a zipper. It rides the *Capturing Bigfoot* news, which is already three weeks old, so post it this week.

## 5. How we make it (production)

**The main path: film it for real, then swap in the character.**
1. **Script + shot list approved in writing.** 0 credits.
2. **Real product footage.** Locked camera or macro, Brandon's hands, his natural voice. 0 credits.
3. **Hollis takes.**
   - Brandon films himself on a locked tripod at 24 fps, acting Hollis's beat in the real room, with the towel on his left shoulder.
   - 3–6 seconds, one character per take, **no product in the take**.
4. **Genjutsu Object Swap on the higgsfield.ai website** replaces Brandon with Hollis.
   - On Nov 8–9 the website runs cost Brandon 0 credits.
   - Through Claude Code (MCP) they always cost credits, roughly 100+ per 15s.
   - The swap re-renders the **whole frame**, so a product in that shot would be AI-altered. That's why product moments are always separate real footage.
5. **Edit** in CapCut, or with Claude Code + ffmpeg. "AI character" label on every AI shot. "#ad" on screen.
6. **Upscale only the final export** if needed (Topaz, about 5 credits).
7. **Post as a draft**, then in the TikTok app:
   - add the product link
   - turn on the **AI-generated content** toggle
   - turn on the **commercial content** toggle
   - pick a **Commercial Music Library** sound (or no music)
   - run Video Pre-Check

**Fallback if the swap fails:** 1–2 short Hollis cutaways from an approved start frame. Test on the same model at the shortest length first. About 35–105 credits per video.

**One-time setup (about 30–80 credits):**
1. Design stills on the cheapest image model (GPT Image 2.6 costs about 0.25 credits per image).
2. Approve one design.
3. Make a multi-angle character sheet on a plain background, with mouth-closed close-ups.
4. Save it as one **Reference Element**.
5. Pass a 3-out-of-3 stills test before any video.

(Soul ID only works for humans, so a creature has to be an Element.)

**Credit rules ("right the first time"):**
- Run `get_cost` before every Claude Code job (it's free).
- Daily cap of about 200 credits, matched to the monthly grant.
- Per-video cap of 100 credits.
- Change one thing per retry. Stop after 3 tries on a shot, then cut it or replace it with real footage.
- No insults or profanity in prompts. That's what got the Nov 9 renders blocked as NSFW.
- Settle dialogue and jokes in text before rendering. One changed word has cost 70 credits.
- Never render native 1080p or 2K. Never upscale tests.

## 6. Before spending anything (checklist)

1. **Locations:** does the house have a porch or lanai, an outside dryer exhaust, a dark tree line or pond edge, and a driveway? If it's an apartment, use AI-only settings for Hollis instead of swaps into the real home.
2. **Swap test on higgsfield.ai:** 3 takes (night lanai, laundry room, car interior), including grainy low-light phone footage.
   - Confirm the website still charges 0 credits for Genjutsu on the live plan card.
   - If human-to-creature fails, use the fallback.
3. **Creator Center:**
   - commission and pilot eligibility for products #1–4
   - free-sample access
   - the real shoppable-video quota on the pilot page (TikTok pages disagree: 3 a day vs 5 a week)
   - current Creator Health Rating
4. **Names:** check that "Hollis" and the handle are free on TikTok and not a conflicting USPTO mark.
5. **Credits:** check the renewal date and the plan's monthly credits on the billing page. The balance was about 1,290 on Nov 9.

## 7. Rebrand

- **Display name:** "Hollis · Porch Cam" (or "The Swamp Ape Next Door")
- **Handle ideas** (check availability): @hollis.porchcam, @porchcamhollis, @greenswamphollis, @polkcountyporchcam. Drop "bigfoot" from the handle.
- **Bio (75 characters):** "AI swamp ape lives out back. I fix what he breaks. Real products, my hands."
- **Profile picture:** Hollis from behind on the lanai step, skunk stripe and towel showing. No text.
- **Old posts:** set the profane-shirt and hood-style Bigfoot/Yeti posts to private (vulgarity rule, brand safety).
- **Pinned:** Episode 0, then the best Case File, then the newest Lore Drop.
- **Hold off on extra accounts.** TikTok requires each account to post its own original content. One clean idea at a time.

---

## 8. Two-week test, with a kill rule agreed in advance

- **Plan:** about 8 shoppable posts and 4 Lore Drops.
- **Kill or pivot if:** the median is under about 500 views or there are zero orders after two weeks. Then change the format or drop the character before spending more credits or buying more products.
- **Honest expectation:** nothing in the research proves a creature-fronted format sells. The real demo is what sells, so most of the effort goes into the real footage.
- Also consider testing a 1–2 second face moment, since TikTok lists "show your face" as a requirement.
- Keep a view log per format and retire a format after 3 weak posts.
