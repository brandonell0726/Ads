# Digital products and light e-commerce as passive income (Etsy, Gumroad/Whop/Stan/Lemon Squeezy/Hotmart, courses and communities, KDP, print-on-demand, TikTok Shop, dropshipping, FBA), as of November 2026

Research date: 2026-11-04. About 31 web searches and 15 page fetches. Etsy's own pages (fees, creativity standards) returned HTTP 403, so the Etsy rules below come from trade press, Etsy's SEC filing and seller guides. Every number has a date next to it; where a date is missing, the source didn't give one.

## 1. Platform fees and rules in 2025-2026 (Etsy, Gumroad, Whop, Stan, Lemon Squeezy, Hotmart, Kajabi, Skool)

### Takeaway
Fees run from about 3% (Whop processing only) to 10-30% (Gumroad direct vs. Discover) to 10-25% on Etsy (25% once Offsite Ads apply). The bigger news is Etsy's October 22, 2026 AI policy update. It bans AI-generated patterns, recipes and instructions, requires AI disclosure and previews for digital items, and forbids "mass-automated creation of products or listings without individual human creative input". That rules out a "Claude lists products on Etsy day and night" model. For Spanish-language info products, Hotmart is the established platform: 9.9% + US$0.50 per sale over US$15.

### Cited Findings
**Etsy (fees)**
- US fees as summarized in 2026 guides: $0.20 listing fee, 6.5% transaction fee, 3% + $0.25 payment processing. That comes to about 10-11% of the sale price before ads — [Checkoutpage, 2026](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/etsy-fees); [Gelato](https://www.gelato.com/blog/how-much-does-etsy-take-from-a-sale)
- Offsite Ads: 15% of the sale (12% for shops above $10,000 in trailing-12-month sales), capped at $100 per order. Shops under $10k can opt out; shops over $10k are reported to be enrolled automatically with no opt-out. Total fees then reach about 22-25% — [Checkoutpage, 2026](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/etsy-fees); [Gelato](https://www.gelato.com/blog/how-much-does-etsy-take-from-a-sale). (Etsy's official fee page returned 403, so these were not checked against it.)
- Etsy scale (FY2025 10-K, year ended Dec 31, 2025): Etsy-marketplace GMS was $10,460.7 million, and consolidated GMS fell 5.3% year over year. The shareholder letter puts Etsy-marketplace active sellers at 5.6 million at Dec 31, 2025, which earnings coverage reported as down about 1.5% year over year — [Etsy FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm); [Etsy FY2025 ARS](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000114036126015380/ef20070789_ars.pdf)

**Etsy (AI and creativity rules)**
- On **October 22, 2026**, Etsy announced an AI-policy update that:
  - "explicitly prohibits AI-generated functional instructions" (knitting, crochet and sewing patterns, recipes, woodworking plans, instruction manuals); "the instructions themselves must be the seller's original work";
  - requires "Disclosure ... when AI is used to create the product itself";
  - requires "most digital downloads [to] include visual previews or excerpts showing what buyers will receive";
  - requires at least one unedited photo of the real item for most listings;
  - prohibits "mass-automated creation of products or listings without individual human creative input and oversight."
  - On enforcement: Etsy warns sellers when AI images are detected at upload, plans to extend this to existing listings, will expand removals "over the coming months", and repeated violations can lead to shop removal. No grace-period deadline was given — [Value Added Resource, Oct 22, 2026 (updated Nov 4, 2026)](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/etsy-bans-ai-patterns/); [Retail Dive](https://retaildive.com/news/etsy-updates-ai-use-policies-emphasize-craftsmanship/833838); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/etsy-cracks-down-on-ai-listings-rebuild-buyer-seller-trust)
- AI-made digital downloads (ebooks, journals, planners, AI art) are still allowed if the seller plays a clear creative role and discloses AI use — [Inkfluence AI, Oct 2026 (seller blog)](https://www.inkfluenceai.com/blog/etsy-ai-rules-update-october-2026)
- An earlier Creativity Standards update is dated June 10, 2025 by a third-party compliance guide. Most AI-assisted digital items fall under the "designed by" / AI-disclosure framework — [lineart-ink guide, 2026](https://lineart-ink-core.pages.dev/blog/etsy-ai-compliance-guide). (Not verified against etsy.com/legal/creativity, which returned 403.)
- POD partners must be disclosed on Etsy ("A member of my shop or production partner") — [InsightAgent POD rules guide, 2026](https://www.insightagent.app/guides/print-on-demand-etsy-rules) (third-party)

**Gumroad**
- Official pricing page (fetched 2026-11-04): **10% + $0.50** per sale through your profile or direct links, and **30%** on sales to new customers who find you through Gumroad Discover. No monthly fee. Gumroad says it handles sales-tax collection and remittance — [Gumroad pricing](https://gumroad.com/pricing)
- Third-party guides say Gumroad has acted as merchant of record since January 2025. One competitor (Creem) disputes that — [Dodo Payments, 2026](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/gumroad-fees-explained); [Checkoutpage, 2026](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/gumroad-fees)
- Gumroad's refund policy: sellers set their own policy, but Gumroad "reserves the right to issue refunds within 90 days of purchase, at its discretion, to prevent chargebacks" — [Gumroad Help](https://gumroad.com/help/article/51-what-is-gumroads-refund-policy)

**Whop**
- Official fee page (fetched 2026-11-04, no effective date; "applies to new merchants that sign up today"):
  - Processing: 2.7% + $0.30 per domestic card sale, +1.5% for international cards, +1% for currency conversion, ACH 1.5% (max $5).
  - Payouts: next-day ACH $2.50; instant 4% + $2.50; Venmo 5% + $2.50; crypto 5% + $1; wire $23.
  - Disputes: **$15 per chargeback**, $29 per early dispute alert.
  - Optional add-ons: tax and remittance 2% (only when tax is collected), billing 0.5%, orchestration 0.8%. "With all enabled and tax collected", a domestic card sale costs 6% + $0.30.
  - The page lists no platform fee and no Discover/marketplace fee — [Whop docs: fees](https://docs.whop.com/fees)
- Whop removed its 30% marketplace (Discover) fee in May 2025 — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/whop-at-142m-revenue). Some reviews still mention a ~3% platform fee, and sources conflict on whether it still exists — [Ruzuku, verified Mar 26, 2026](https://www.ruzuku.com/learn/articles/whop-pricing); [Make Influence, May 2026](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/whop-and-the-rise-of-creator-owned-digital-commerce-what-it-means-for-brand-deals)
- Whop scale: about $80M monthly GMV by Dec 2024, nearing $100M a month by Mar-Apr 2025. More than $845M in cumulative creator earnings by Oct 2025, with an **average** (not median) of $8,413 per creator per month. About $142M annualized company revenue in Oct 2025 — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/whop-at-142m-revenue); [Sacra overview](https://sacra.com/c/whop). One estimate counts 211,000+ sellers (modeled from public data) — [InsightRaider](https://insightraider.com/pt/answers/how-much-money-can-you-make-on-whop)

**Stan, Kajabi, Skool**
- Stan Store: Creator plan $29 a month and Creator Pro $99 a month ($300 / $948 a year), 0% platform fee, as checked in September 2026 by third-party compilations — [Ruzuku pricing comparison](https://www.ruzuku.com/compare/pricing) (not verified on stan.store)
- Kajabi: Basic $179, Growth $249, Pro $499 a month billed monthly, or $143 / $199 / $399 billed annually. Kajabi Payments charges 2.9% + $0.30 on Basic (US) — [Elite Content Marketer, Skool vs Kajabi](https://www.elitecontentmarketer.com/skool-vs-kajabi/) (third-party)
- Skool: Hobby $9 a month with a 10% transaction fee; Pro $99 a month with a 2.9% fee. One page reports a 3.9% rate above $899, which is unconfirmed — [SkillScouter, 2026](https://skillscouter.com/skool-review/); [Schoolmaker, 2026](https://schoolmaker.com/blog/skool-pricing)

**Lemon Squeezy**
- Stripe bought Lemon Squeezy in 2024. Stripe's help center now names the legal seller "Sold through Link, LLC", formerly Lemon Squeezy LLC — [Stripe Support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/why-is-sold-through-link-llc-the-legal-entity-on-my-invoice-for-stripe-managed-payments-fees)
- The fee is still described as 5% + $0.50 per sale with no monthly fee — [Dodo Payments review, 2026 (competitor)](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/lemonsqueezy-review). Stripe Managed Payments (merchant of record) went into public preview in Feb 2026 with a migration path for Lemon Squeezy merchants. One estimate puts it at about 2.9% + $0.30 plus about 3.5%, near 6.4% + $0.30 in total. It lacks Lemon Squeezy's storefront, affiliate and download-delivery features — [Passive Income Tools, 2026](https://passive-income-tools.pages.dev/blog/lemon-squeezy-stripe-managed-payments-2026); [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/lemon-squeezy-vs-stripe/)

**Hotmart (Spanish/Portuguese info-product platform)**
- Fees:
  - **9.9% + US$0.50** per approved sale for products over US$15. Hotmart's own example: a US$30 product pays US$3.47 in fees and the producer keeps US$26.53.
  - Products of US$15 or less: 9.9% + US$0.10 — [Hotmart blog (es), precios](https://hotmart.com/es/blog/precios); [Panda Video](https://www.pandavideo.com/blog/hotmart-fees)
  - Brazil (BRL): the fixed part rose from R$1.00 to R$2.49 on 2026-09-21. Up to 8.9% applies above R$500k in yearly sales — [Tactus (pt)](https://tactus.com.br/taxas-da-hotmart-para-produtor/)
  - Withdrawals: US$1 per withdrawal, US$50 minimum. Payout time was cut from 15 days to 2 business days in 2025 — [Bit4Learn](https://bit4learn.com/es/lms/hotmart/) (third-party)

### Inferences
- For a low-ticket Brandon product ($15-$49), Whop (about 3% + $0.30) or Stan (a flat $29 a month) keeps the most. Gumroad direct (10% + $0.50) is simplest and handles tax, but Discover sales lose 30%. Etsy brings buyers, but at 10-25% in fees and with the strictest AI rules.
- Etsy's ban on mass-automated listings makes "Claude runs an Etsy shop day and night" a policy violation. Any Etsy shop has to have Brandon's real creative input on each listing.
- Whop's $8,413 a month is an average across 200k+ sellers and is very likely skewed by a small number of top sellers. It says nothing about a typical seller's income.
- Etsy marketplace GMS divided by active sellers is about $1,870 per seller per year in 2025 (my calculation from the 10-K: $10.46B / 5.6M). That figure is gross sales, not profit, and a mean; the median shop is likely far lower.

### Gaps
- Etsy's official fee page and creativity standards returned 403. I couldn't confirm the Offsite Ads auto-enrollment rule, any new identity or "shop setup" fee, or the official wording of the Oct 2026 policy.
- One guide claims 17,000+ Etsy listings were removed in early 2025 for AI-disclosure violations. That is unverified, single-source, and left out of the findings.
- Gumroad: whether card processing is charged on top of 10% + $0.50 (one third-party guide says the effective rate is about 12.9% + $0.80), and a reported March 2026 change to a $100 minimum payout for unverified accounts. Neither appears on the official pricing page.
- Stan's and Kajabi's prices weren't checked on their own sites. Whop's Latin America rates sit on a separate page I didn't fetch.

## 2. Amazon KDP (AI disclosure, title cap, low-content saturation, royalties, real earnings) and print-on-demand (Merch on Demand tiers, Printify/Printful margins, IP risk)

### Takeaway
KDP allows AI content but requires disclosure for AI-generated text, images and translations. It has capped new titles at 3 a day since 2023. The typical author earns very little: in Written Word Media's 2025 survey, 44% of authors made under $100 a month, and 80% of those with 1-4 books did. Merch on Demand reportedly moved on June 1, 2026 to royalty tiers that pay organic-only sellers about half as much ($2.44 vs $4.88 on a $19.99 tee). POD margins on Etsy+Printify are thin (often 15-30%), and trademark takedowns are the main account risk.

### Cited Findings
**KDP AI and content rules**
- KDP definition: "AI-generated content [is] text, images, or translations created by an AI-based tool", and it stays AI-generated "even if you applied substantial edits afterwards." Content you wrote and only edited or refined with AI counts as "AI-assisted" and needs no disclosure. "We require you to inform us of AI-generated content ... when you publish a new book" or edit and republish one — [KDP Content Guidelines (fetched 2026-11-04)](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672390)
- KDP bans "content that's typically disappointing to customers", "descriptive content meant to mislead customers", and undifferentiated public-domain copies. Summaries and study guides of copyrighted works can't be published outside the US without the rights holder's permission — [KDP Content Guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672390)
- **Title cap:** In September 2023, KDP limited publishers to three new titles per day, citing AI concerns, and told the Authors Guild the number may be adjusted. Exceptions can be requested — [Authors Guild, Sept 2023](https://authorsguild.org/news/amazon-adds-to-kdp-generative-ai-policy-caps-daily-self-publishing-uploads/); [Ebook Friendly](https://ebookfriendly.com/amazon-kdp-publishers-publish-more-than-3-books-a-day/). The current content-guidelines page doesn't mention the cap, so its 2026 status is unconfirmed.

**KDP royalties**
- Paperback royalty on Amazon.com is **50%** for list prices at or below $9.98 and **60%** at or above $9.99, minus printing cost. Expanded Distribution pays 40%. KDP's example: a $15 book × 0.60, minus $5.00 printing, earns $4.00 — [KDP Paperback Royalty (fetched 2026-11-04)](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834330). (The page gives no effective date for the 50% tier.)

**KDP earnings and saturation**
- Written Word Media 2025 Author Survey (published Dec 2, 2025; 1,346 respondents, 96% published, self-reported):
  - Monthly income: 44% of authors under $100; 28% $100-$1,000; 15% $1,000-$5,000; 13% over $5,000.
  - By catalog size: 80% of authors with 1-4 books earn under $100 a month; 5-9 books, median about $50 a month; 25+ books, median about $3,000 a month, with 40%+ over $5,000.
  - More than half of low earners spend under $100, or nothing, on covers — [Written Word Media](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2025-author-survey/)
- ALLi 2025 Indie Author Income Survey: median about $13,500 a year, but only among authors who spend at least half their time on writing or earn at least half their income from it, so casual authors are excluded — [ALLi](https://selfpublishingadvice.org/2025-author-income-survey/); summarized by [Vappingo](https://www.vappingo.com/word-blog/how-much-do-kdp-authors-earn/)
- US ISBN-registered titles rose 32.5% in 2025 to more than 4 million (Bowker data, cited secondhand) — [BestWriting self-publishing statistics](https://bestwriting.com/self-publishing-statistics)
- Low-content books (journals, logbooks, planners) are described as saturated since the early "gold rush". Commentators point to medium-content, tightly niched books (math workbooks, sight-word activity books, cryptograms, pixel-art coloring, large-print books for seniors) as what still sells — [Naomi Jane, Substack](https://naomijane.substack.com/p/low-and-medium-content-books-on-amazon); [The Write Moves](https://the-write-moves.beehiiv.com/p/low-content-burnout-are-publishers-quitting-kdp-in-droves) (opinion, not data)

**Merch on Demand (Amazon)**
- Reported change effective **June 1, 2026**: flat royalties replaced by three tiers based on the share of sales from external (non-organic) traffic over trailing 60 days, recalculated monthly, US only.
  - Royalty on a $19.99 tee: **Creator (default, under 15% external traffic) $2.44**; Plus (15-34%, 10+ units a month) $4.88; Premium (35%+, 10+ units) $5.27.
  - External traffic means Sponsored Products and Brands ads, off-Amazon ads, social posts and Amazon Attribution links. Organic Amazon search doesn't count — [Merch Titans (cites "Amazon Merch on Demand Royalty Update, April 2026"; publisher sells Merch tools)](https://merchtitans.com/blog/amazon-merch-royalty-changes-june-2026); [AMZ Prep](https://amzprep.com/amazon-merch-on-demand/)
- AMZ Prep says the Creator rate "dropped from approximately $5.78" and that listings with no sales in 18 months are removed automatically (no date given). It also says 2026 application approval rates are about 30-40% after Amazon tightened screening against AI spam accounts. None of these claims cite Amazon — [AMZ Prep](https://amzprep.com/amazon-merch-on-demand/). This conflicts with Merch Titans, which says Plus ($4.88) is the same as the old rate.
- Printify's advice: treat each Merch tier "like a temporary lease, not a permanent upgrade" — [Printify knowledge hub](https://printify.com/knowledge-hub/is-amazon-merch-on-demand-worth-it/)

**Printify / Printful POD on Etsy**
- Printify says POD margins usually run about 30-50%, with its worked example landing near 31% net after fees — [Printify blog](https://printify.com/blog/is-print-on-demand-profitable/). A third-party guide says Etsy sellers using Printify often see about 15-20% — [Outfy](https://www.outfy.com/blog/top-selling-digital-products-on-etsy/)
- IP risk: Disney and Marvel characters, sports logos and trademarked phrases are described as "instant takedown and suspension". Third-party guides describe an escalation of temporary suspension on the second violation and permanent closure on the third — [InsightAgent POD rules, 2026](https://www.insightagent.app/guides/print-on-demand-etsy-rules); [Cohn Legal Group](https://www.cohnlg.com/how-to-avoid-etsy-copyright-infringement/)
- Rights holders also sue POD platforms directly. Atari sued Printify and won an ex parte TRO without first sending takedown notices; the case later fizzled — [Eric Goldman blog, Jan 2024](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2024/01/ataris-lawsuit-against-a-print-on-demand-service-fizzles-out-atari-v-printify.htm)

### Inferences
- KDP fits a slow catalog build, not quick passive money. Income tracks catalog size (25+ books), the 3-a-day cap limits mass AI publishing, and in the 2025 survey the median author with fewer than 10 books earns about $50 a month or less.
- If the June 2026 Merch tiers are accurate, organic-only Merch sellers (the classic "upload designs and wait" model) earn about $2.44 a shirt. Higher tiers need paid ads or social traffic, so the model is no longer passive.
- Etsy+Printify at 15-30% net on a $25-30 shirt leaves about $4-8 before ads, with suspension risk from any trend design that touches a trademark. Riding a trending phrase is the main way POD sellers get banned.

### Gaps
- KDP doesn't publish earnings data. All distributions are self-reported surveys of motivated authors, so survivorship and selection bias apply.
- I couldn't confirm the date the 50% royalty tier for paperbacks under $9.99 took effect (I recall it from 2025, but no source was fetched for the date), or whether the 3-a-day cap is unchanged in 2026.
- Amazon's official announcement of the Merch June 2026 tiers wasn't viewable. The two secondary sources disagree on the old baseline ($4.88 vs. about $5.78), and the design-slot tier numbers conflict across sources.
- No 2025-2026 primary data on Printify/Printful suspension rates or KDP rejection rates for AI books.

## 3. Courses, templates and communities for creators, incl. teaching AI video/ad creation (demand, pricing, earnings evidence, Spanish-language/Hotmart)

### Takeaway
There is real demand for AI video and ad skills, and the market is crowded. Higgsfield runs its own free Academy. Paid Higgsfield bootcamps already run on Maven, prompt packs sell for $29 up to $99-$348, and Spanish-language AI-ad courses already exist on Gumroad. Hotmart says AI education is booming in Latin America (Brazil AI courses grew 700% in 2025). There is no verified data on typical seller earnings. Community platforms (Skool) are highly concentrated: estimates suggest about 70% of groups are free and most paid groups earn low thousands a month at most.

### Cited Findings
- Higgsfield runs an official **Higgsfield Academy** with free courses and lessons, such as a 3-step AI ad course — [Higgsfield Academy](https://higgsfield.ai/academy); [lesson example](https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/ai-ad-3-step/lessons/the-props)
- Paid cohort courses on Higgsfield already exist on Maven ("Build Your AI Content Engine with Higgsfield", "Scroll-Stopping AI Video with Higgsfield") — [Maven (TAI Labs)](https://maven.com/tailabs/build-your-ai-content-engine-with-higgsfield); [Maven](https://maven.com/p/e6e5fb/scroll-stopping-ai-video-with-higgsfield). So do bootcamps elsewhere — [Hackceleration](https://hackceleration.com/training/higgsfield)
- Prompt and template pack prices seen: a Gumroad "AI Ad Prompt Library" (200+ templates for Higgsfield, Sora 2, Veo 3) at $29 — [Gumroad listing](https://aitechtools.gumroad.com/l/AIAdPromptLibrary); a Higgsfield prompt pack at a $99 launch price, then $348 — [Hooked](https://www.hooked.so/prompts/higgsfield); a Whop "AI Content Starter Kit" covering Kling Motion Control — [Whop listing](https://whop.com/masteracademy-ai/products/ai-content-starter-kit/)
- A seller guide reports that showing example outputs is often the difference between a $15 and a $39 prompt pack, and recommends three tiers (starter, pro, complete library) — [Dodo Payments guide, 2026](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/sell-ai-prompts-online) (vendor blog)
- Spanish-language AI ad and video courses are already sold on Gumroad. Search snippets showed one at about $149 and a 22-module, 30+ hour course at about €200 — e.g., [Gumroad (es) 1](https://bernardobasso.gumroad.com/l/cursoiaventas), [Gumroad (es) 2](https://alandodominguez.gumroad.com/l/rknidi), [Gumroad (es) 3](https://ingeniela.gumroad.com/l/ia-que-trabaja-por-ti). These are sales pages with no sales data, and the price-to-listing mapping comes from snippets.
- Hotmart (company-reported, relayed by press, 2025-2026):
  - More than 250,000 active producers globally in 2025.
  - 74% of its Latin American transactions cross borders.
  - Women are 47% of producers and 60% of buyers.
  - Buyers in Argentina grew about 30% from 2024 to 2025 while supply grew more slowly.
  - In Brazil, AI education through courses and digital products made more than R$140 million in 2025, up 700%.
  - Hotmart cites Grand View Research: Latin America's creator economy was about US$10.8B in 2024, projected at about US$98B by 2033 (27.8% CAGR) — [Que.es, Jun 19, 2026](https://www.que.es/2026/06/19/economia-creadores-latam-hotmart/); [DPL News](https://dplnews.com/colombia-lidera-la-economia-de-creadores-en-hispanoamerica-en-un-mercado-que-entra-en-fase-de-escala/); [Semana, 2026](https://www.semana.com/economia/macroeconomia/articulo/colombia-lidera-la-economia-de-creadores-en-la-region/202612/)
- Hotmart's own AI course-outline tool is available in Spanish, English and Portuguese. 35,000+ course structures had been created as of a 2023 post (stale) — [Hotmart blog](https://hotmart.com/es/blog/hotmart-ai)
- Skool economics (all modeled from public listings, i.e., price × members, an upper bound):
  - About 70% of communities are free.
  - Among trending communities, the median price is $27 a month and 84.6% charge.
  - Most paid communities sit in the low thousands of dollars a month; one estimate says the median paid community makes under $5k a month.
  - The 10 biggest groups hold 42.3% of tracked memberships, and the top community runs at about $5M a year (May 2026) — [Profitable.app Skool stats](https://profitable.app/skool/stats); [Revenue Geeks Skool statistics](https://revenuegeeks.com/software/skool/statistics); [Skool paid report](https://skool-paid-report.pages.dev/data/executive_synthesis.md)
- Gumroad's most recent public earnings distribution is old. October 2020: 19,480 creators made something, 6,682 made over $100, 1,657 over $1K, 192 over $10K and 10 over $100K. The tweet doesn't state the period, though it's probably monthly — [Sacra Gumroad / secondary](https://sacra.com/research/gumroad-android-creator-economy/); [Latka interview (2019 snapshot)](https://getlatka.com/companies/gumroad/interviews/gumroad-sahil-lavingia-2019)
- General benchmark: over half of creators earn under $15k a year (Influencer Marketing Hub, cited secondhand) — [Hopp](https://www.hopp.co/post/guide-selling-digital-products)

### Inferences
- A generic "learn Higgsfield / AI video" course competes with Higgsfield's own free Academy and with established Maven cohorts. Brandon's edge would be narrower and proof-based: a bilingual "AI ads for food trucks and restaurants" kit (prompt pack, Canva caption templates, shot lists) backed by his own client results. That means real, documented results, not income claims.
- Content tied to specific models goes stale fast (models get added and removed often), so an AI prompt pack needs regular updates. It is not set-and-forget.
- Spanish-language demand is real on Hotmart in Latin America. Evidence that Spanish versions face less competition for US Hispanic buyers is missing. Spanish AI-ad courses already exist, so "less competition" is unproven.
- Paid communities are the opposite of passive: members expect live calls and posts. The typical Skool group earns little, and a few top groups take most of the revenue.

### Gaps
- No verified revenue data (Stripe-verified or platform-published) for AI video, prompt-pack or template sellers on Whop, Gumroad or Hotmart. All earnings evidence found is sales pages or modeled estimates.
- No Hotmart data split for US Hispanic buyers, and no Hotmart best-seller ranking for AI courses in Spanish.
- A search snippet claimed OpenAI shut down the Sora API on September 24, 2026 and that course and prompt vendors removed Sora 2. I couldn't attribute this to a specific fetched source, so it's left unverified.

## 4. TikTok Shop selling, dropshipping and Amazon FBA in 2025-2026 (capital, margins, tariffs, failure rates, time) vs. affiliate

### Takeaway
None of these are passive. TikTok Shop charges about 6% referral (reportedly 8% from Aug 4, 2026, unconfirmed) plus creator commissions and mandatory TikTok fulfillment. China-sourced dropshipping was hit when the US de minimis exemption ended (May 2, 2025 for China and Hong Kong; Aug 29, 2025 for all countries). Private-label FBA needs about $3,000-$10,000+ to start, and sellers report rising shipping, goods and ad costs. Failure rates are only estimated (blogs say 80-90% of dropshipping stores fail). Affiliate selling (TikTok Shop affiliate or Amazon Associates) needs no inventory or capital, which is why it suits a <$500 budget.

### Cited Findings
**TikTok Shop**
- US referral fee: 6% for most categories and 5% for jewelry (cited in mid-2026 guides). ## 6. FTC rules if Brandon ever sells a course or "how to make money" product (earnings claims, Business Opportunity Rule, 2024-2026 enforcement against AI e-commerce coaching)

### Takeaway
The FTC's position: any earnings claim must reflect what a typical buyer achieves and must be backed by data in hand. "Results not typical" disclaimers aren't enough, and lifestyle imagery counts as an earnings claim. The Business Opportunity Rule (16 CFR 437) requires a one-page disclosure 7 days before payment, plus a formal Earnings Claim Statement if any earnings claim is made. In January 2025 the FTC proposed expanding it to business coaching ("Business and Money-Making Opportunity Rule"); I found no final rule as of Nov 2026. Enforcement in 2023-2025 hit AI e-commerce "passive income" schemes hard: Click Profit, Ganadores (Florida-based, aimed at Spanish speakers), Growth Cave, Ecommerce Empire Builders, Automators AI, FBA Machine and Air AI.

### Cited Findings
**FTC guidance and rules**
- FTC guidance:
  - "Any earnings claim should reflect what the typical person to whom the representation is directed is likely to achieve."
  - Claims need "reliable, empirical evidence that supports the claim, not subjective beliefs or personal anecdotes."
  - Images or statements of wealth are implied earnings claims.
  - "A 'results not typical,' 'results are not guaranteed,' or similar disclosure is not enough"; the FTC calls for a "clear, prominent, and unavoidable presentation of the typical participant's revenue and expenses" — [FTC Business Guidance (MLM page; principles apply to income claims generally)](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guidance-concerning-multi-level-marketing)
- Business Opportunity Rule:
  - Sellers must give a one-page Disclosure Document, in the standard form, at least seven days before a buyer signs or pays.
  - Any earnings claim requires a separate "EARNINGS CLAIM STATEMENT REQUIRED BY LAW". It must give the claim, the dates the earnings were achieved, and the number and percentage of buyers who got those results or better.
  - Sellers must provide 10 references, disclose legal actions from the past 10 years, update quarterly and keep substantiation — [FTC: Selling a Work-at-Home or Other Business Opportunity?](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/selling-work-home-or-other-business-opportunity-revised-rule-may-apply-you-1); [FTC standard disclosure form](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files?file=attachments%2Fdisclosure-important-information-about-business-opportunity%2F111122bizoppappendixa.pdf)
- **January 2025 proposals:** the FTC issued two NPRMs and one ANPRM.
  - Expand the Business Opportunity Rule to "money-making opportunities", including business coaching and investment opportunities requiring payment, and rename it the "Business and Money-Making Opportunity Rule".
  - Replace the ban on misrepresenting income with a broader ban on "false or misleading earnings claim[s]".
  - A separate Earnings Claim Rule for MLMs.
  - Republican commissioners dissented, and the rules faced uncertainty under the new chair — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/ftc-issues-three-notices-changes-business-opportunity-rule-and-new-rule-multi-level); [Lathrop GPM](https://www.lathropgpm.com/insights/ftc-proposes-changes-to-deter-deceptive-earnings-claims-by-multilevel-marketers-and-money-making-opportunity-sellers/); [Hunton](https://hunton.com/hunton-retail-law-resource/ftc-issues-three-notices-for-changes-to-the-business-opportunity-rule-and-a-new-rule-on-multi-level-marketing-mlm-practices)

**Enforcement cases**
- **Click Profit:**
  - The FTC sued in March 2025, with a TRO entered Mar 5, 2025. It alleged false promises of "passive income" from AI-powered e-commerce stores on Amazon, Walmart and TikTok. Consumers lost at least $14M; some paid "management fees" of at least $45,000; about 95% of stores were blocked, suspended or terminated by Amazon — [FTC, Mar 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/03/ftc-acts-stop-click-profit-online-business-opportunity-has-cost-consumers-least-14-million)
  - August 2025 settlement: permanent industry ban and asset turnover for redress — [FTC, Aug 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/08/ftc-case-against-e-commerce-business-opportunity-scheme-its-operators-results-permanent-ban-industry); [case page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/click-profit-llc)
- **Ganadores** (a Florida-based e-commerce and real-estate coaching scheme):
  - The FTC acted in June 2023. Marketing was largely in Spanish while contracts and disclosures were often in English — [FTC, June 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/06/ftc-acts-stop-real-estate-online-commerce-coaching-scheme-ganadores-targeting-spanish-speaking)
  - January 2024: bans for the companies, their owners and managers — [FTC, Jan 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/01/ftc-action-leads-ban-ganadores-real-estate-income-scam-its-owner-managers)
  - June 20, 2025: lifetime ban for operator Robert Shemin from e-commerce and real-estate coaching. He must back up earnings claims and turn over refund funds — [FTC, June 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/06/operator-ganadores-ecommerce-real-estate-business-opportunity-scam-faces-lifetime-ban-result-ftc)
- Growth Cave (passive-income program; about $50M taken from consumers, per the FTC), Ecommerce Empire Builders ("AI-powered ecommerce empire" coaching, sued fall 2024), Automators AI (an "AI-integrated" e-commerce opportunity), FBA Machine / Passive Scaling (FBA Stores LLC permanently banned) and Ascend (stipulated permanent ban on business opportunities and coaching, including bars on AI performance claims) — [American Bar Association newsletter](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/antitrust_law/resources/newsletters/ftc-continues-crackdown-ai-deceit-e-commerce/); [Frankfurt Kurnit ad law blog](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102kymz/ftc-shuts-down-ai-driven-business-opportunity-scheme-continues-sweep-of-deceptiv); [Entrepreneur](https://entrepreneur.com/business-news/ftc-sues-click-profit-alleges-passive-income-amazon-ai-scam/488697); [Midpage, FTC v. Ascend](https://app.midpage.ai/case/federal-trade-commission-v-ascend-1000448869941)
- **Air AI (2025):** the FTC sued over claims about business growth, earnings potential and refund guarantees, citing the Telemarketing Sales Rule and the Business Opportunity Rule (including failure to give earnings claim statements) — [GRC Report](https://www.grcreport.com/post/ftc-sues-air-ai-over-deceptive-business-claims-seeks-to-halt-scheme-targeting-small-businesses)

### Inferences
- If Brandon sells a "how I make AI ads for restaurants" course, it should teach a skill and make no income promises: no screenshots of earnings, no "make $5k a month" headlines, no lifestyle imagery. The FTC treats all of these as earnings claims that need typical-buyer data, which a new seller won't have.
- A plain skills course without earnings claims, or without promises to supply clients or accounts, generally isn't a "business opportunity" under today's rule. The January 2025 proposal would pull coaching in, so the line could move. Bundling "we'll find you clients" or "done-for-you store" claims is exactly what got Click Profit and others banned.
- Ganadores matters for Brandon because of the pattern: a Florida operator selling Spanish-language income coaching. Any Spanish-language offer from him should carry all terms in Spanish too.

### Gaps
- Status of the January 2025 Business Opportunity Rule NPRM as of November 2026 (final, withdrawn or stalled) wasn't found.
- I found no FTC action filed in 2026 against AI e-commerce coaching. The cases found are 2023-2025, and newer filings may exist that search didn't surface.
- Florida-specific rules (e.g., the Florida Sale of Business Opportunities Act, Fla. Stat. ch. 559 part VIII) weren't researched.
