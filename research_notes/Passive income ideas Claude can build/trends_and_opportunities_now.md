## 1. What is trending now in US searches, products and social (holiday 2026 forecasts, viral product categories)

### Takeaway
Holiday 2026 is forecast as a record but modest-growth season: Adobe projects $275.1B online (+6.7%), NRF $1.013T total (+4.2%) and Deloitte about $1.7T. Shoppers are value-focused and K-shaped. Growth is fastest in grocery, toys, cosmetics and furniture online, and in AI-referred and social/affiliate traffic. The products actually "popping" in 2026 cluster in K-beauty skincare, supplements (magnesium, collagen, ACV), trending toys (KPop Demon Hunters, Jellycat, NeeDoh, Pokémon), wearables and smart glasses, and the GTA VI launch on Nov 19, 2026. No live Amazon Movers & Shakers or TikTok Creative Center data could be read.

### Inferences
- "Popping" products are mostly low-ticket beauty and supplements sold through creator video, not through search pages. Adobe's channel data points the same way: affiliates/influencers and social are gaining revenue share fastest, paid search is not. This fits Brandon's video skill better than a search-first Amazon site.
- The safest durable categories in this data are skincare, supplements and wellness (repeat purchase), home/cleaning basics and big franchises (Pokémon, GTA, Nintendo, KPop Demon Hunters). Single SKUs inside them churn quickly (see Q3).
- AI shopping traffic (+130% Adobe; ~20% of visits from agents per Salesforce) means some "best X" research that used to land on affiliate blogs now ends inside AI assistants. That is platform risk for new affiliate content sites.

## 2. Emerging problems people and small businesses are paying to solve in 2026 (with demand evidence)

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced 2026 "problems" are these. First, small businesses (restaurants included) adopting AI but not knowing how to use it well: most experiment, few have it embedded. Second, cost-of-living pressure: value-seeking, K-shaped spending, and Florida ACA premium payments more than doubling for subsidized enrollees. Third, GLP-1 lifestyle change: 12% of adults currently on GLP-1s, and 23% of households have a user. Fourth, Florida home costs: insurance, hurricanes, and a property-tax amendment voted on Nov 3, 2026. Fifth, Orlando tourism at record levels (76.7M visitors in 2025). Spanish-speaking audiences are measurably under-served by digital marketers. That is the clearest edge for Brandon.

### Inferences
- The problem with the best demand evidence that Brandon is uniquely placed to solve is local small businesses that want AI and video marketing but lack skills, especially Spanish-speaking owners. That is his main business. A passive asset that collects demand for it (a guide, tool or templates) beats an unrelated trend.
- GLP-1 eating is a multi-year behavior shift (doubling in 18 months, 2030 projections), not a fad. A food-focused, Spanish/Latin-cuisine angle on it is plausible, but health claims are YMYL and regulated.
- Florida money problems (insurance, ACA, property tax, hurricanes) have real demand but are YMYL. Big institutions and news outlets dominate them, and ACA/insurance lead generation is regulated. Expect low odds for a new solo site unless it's a narrow tool or local Spanish-language explainer.
- Peptide/"weight-loss peptide" demand is real (Exploding Topics +99x) but sits in FDA-enforcement territory with hospitalizations. Treat it as a "do not touch".

## 3. Fads vs evergreen: how fast trend-based assets decay, and which trends last long enough to build on

### Takeaway
Most social trends die within days. Publicis found 48% of TikTok trends fade within five days. Viral SKUs lose ranking within months, and even huge fads like Labubu saw resale prices fall 50%+ within months of their peak. Trends that lasted are tied to durable franchises (Pokémon, KPop Demon Hunters still a top toy a year after peaking in search), behavior changes (GLP-1, AI at work) or places (Orlando tourism). Assets built on fads have little resale value: content-site sales fell in 2025 and buyers pay on proven profit, not spikes.

### Cited Findings
- **TikTok trend lifespan:** Publicis Groupe (reported Apr 2026): 48% of TikTok trends fade within five days, and only a fraction last beyond two weeks. — [Pulse: Publicis on TikTok trends](https://www.pulse.bot/media/news/only-a-fraction-of-tiktok-trends-last-beyond-two-weeks-publicis-groupe-finds-7f651586-7dc4-4082-9a25-efea55509b06/)
- **Viral SKU decay on TikTok Shop (Q2 2026):** NeoCell collagen fell ~33% from Q1, and the Dr.Melaxin set declined every month, even while both were in the top 10. The pressure washer (+173% MoM into June) and the fan were seasonal spikes. — [FastMoss](https://www.fastmoss.com/blog/best-selling-tiktok-shop-products-us-q2-2026/)
- **Labubu (a fad case):** it was #3 on Google's 2025 US trending list. — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-year-in-search-2025-3621989). By Sept 2025 resale prices were down 50%+ in some cases, sometimes below retail. Pop Mart shares fell ~25% from Aug 2025 highs. Pop Mart raised supply roughly tenfold to ~30M units a month, and analysts disagree whether supply or demand drove the fall. — [Inside Retail, Sept 2025](https://insideretail.co.nz/2025/09/23/has-the-labubu-craze-reached-its-peak-trend-experts-discuss-the-drop-in-demand/); [The Standard HK](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/china/article/314826/Labubu-resale-price-falls-may-be-more-about-supply-than-demand); [Hypebeast, Sept 2025](https://hypebeast.com/2025/9/pop-mart-stock-declines-labubu-craze-fading-reports). Labubu was not on Adobe's 2026 hot-toy list. — [Adobe PDF](https://news.adobe.com/news/downloads/pdfs/2026/09/adi-holiday-forecast-release-9-28.pdf)
- **KPop Demon Hunters (a lasting case so far):** #2 on Google's 2025 US trending list, and its toys are still on Adobe's Sept 2026 hot-sellers list. — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-year-in-search-2025-3621989); [Adobe PDF](https://news.adobe.com/news/downloads/pdfs/2026/09/adi-holiday-forecast-release-9-28.pdf)
- **Behavior-change trend example:** current GLP-1 use doubled from 6% (May 2024) to 12% (Nov 2025). — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/kff-health-tracking-poll-prescription-drug-costs-views-on-trump-administration-actions-and-glp-1-use/)
- **Shape heuristic (low-rigor sources):** fads show rapid, unsustainable spikes in Google Trends, while trends show steady, sustained growth. — [Accio](https://www.accio.com/business/trends-vs-fads); [Spate](https://www.spate.nyc/blog/how-to-interpret-trend-signals). Academic work fits fad models to Google Trends meme time series (abstract only seen). — [Fraunhofer publication](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/handle/publica/383171)
- **Exploding Topics labels topics "Exploding", "Regular" or "Peaked"** and shows search volume with multi-year growth (e.g., Oct 17, 2026 science page). — [Exploding Topics](https://explodingtopics.com/science-topics-last-10-years)
- **Resale value of sites:** Flippa's 2025 recap says content-site sales fell 33.5% (volume −37%, tied to Google updates), while SaaS rose 73.5% and YouTube channels 155%. Content sites sell for ~25–40× monthly profit (Flippa). — [Flippa](https://flippa.com/blog/how-much-can-you-sell-your-website-for/)
- **Small software tools also decay:** a blog citing TrustMRR data says the median indie hacker makes under $1K MRR and "generic AI tools" on TrustMRR are losing ~6% a month (secondary, unverified). TrustMRR verifies revenue by Stripe API key. — [Mean CEO blog](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=11175); [TrustMRR FAQ](https://www.trustmrr.com/faq)

### Inferences
- Rule of thumb from this evidence: build assets (sites, tools, products) only on trends that have shown 12+ months of steady or rising interest, or that are tied to a durable franchise, place or behavior. Use fast social video, which is cheap and disposable, to ride short spikes, and point that traffic to the durable asset.
- A "page for a product that's popping right now" (the YouTuber model) lives or dies on social traffic, not Google, because new pages take months to rank and most spikes are over in days. It is a content job, not passive income (also concluded in `content_sites_ads_affiliate.md` §6).
- Event spikes with known dates (GTA VI on Nov 19, Black Friday/Cyber Monday, hurricane season every June–Nov, ACA open enrollment, Florida tax bills each November) are predictable. Content for them can be prepared in advance and reused each year. That is the one kind of "trend" a part-time creator can plan around.

### Gaps
- No rigorous quantified study of Google-search fad half-lives was found. The Fraunhofer paper's full text wasn't read.
- No case data on how trend-jacking pages perform after the spike (traffic retained, revenue) in 2025–2026.

## 4. A validation checklist and free or cheap tools (search volume, competition, monetization fit, AI Overview exposure, platform risk)

### Takeaway
Validate in this order: is the demand durable, can you reach people without Google, can a solo creator compete, does it monetize, and how exposed is it to AI answers and platform rules. Free tools cover most of it: Google Trends, Google autocomplete/"People also ask", TikTok and YouTube search, Amazon Best Sellers/Movers & Shakers, and Reddit. Exploding Topics (from ~$39/mo) and Glimpse (reported $30–99/mo, small free tier) are optional. Long, question-style informational queries are most exposed to AI Overviews. Local, real-time and tool-type queries are least exposed.

### Cited Findings
- **Exploding Topics pricing (third-party directories, 2026):** paid from ~$39/mo (range $39–249). API access only on the ~$99/mo "Investor" tier. Free tier is limited browsing. — [TrendsMCP pricing comparison](https://trendsmcp.ai/trends-api-pricing-comparison); [Treendly comparison](https://vc.treendly.com/vs/exploding-topics)
- **Glimpse pricing conflicts (2026):** reports range from a $30/mo starter to $49 Pro / $99 Team. The free tier is reported as 5–10 searches or "no meaningful free tier", and Glimpse posts no public pricing link. — [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/glimpse/pricing); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/glimpse-pricing/); [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/glimpse/pricing)

Users clicked a result on 8% of visits with a summary vs 15% without. — [Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)

- **AI shopping agents (2026):** Adobe forecasts AI-referred retail traffic +130%, and Salesforce predicts ~1 in 5 holiday e-commerce visits from AI agents. Both increase the risk that product research skips affiliate sites. — [Adobe PDF](https://news.adobe.com/news/downloads/pdfs/2026/09/adi-holiday-forecast-release-9-28.pdf); [Chain Store Age](https://chainstoreage.com/five-2026-holiday-predictions-salesforce)
- **Monetization signals:** affiliates/creators and social are gaining revenue share fastest in holiday 2026 (Adobe +12%/+17% vs paid search +4%). — [Adobe PDF](https://news.adobe.com/news/downloads/pdfs/2026/09/adi-holiday-forecast-release-9-28.pdf). AdSense RPM by niche (third-party estimate, unverified, from a vendor selling an alternative): finance/B2B $8–20, health/wellness $5–12, lifestyle $3–8. — [Newor Media](https://newormedia.com/blog/best-adsense-alternatives/). Spanish-language digital ad budgets are under 1% of online retailers' spend (Q1 2025). — [Nielsen](https://www.nielsen.com/news-center/2025/hispanic-consumers-overindex-on-streaming-consumption-versus-rest-of-u-s-new-nielsen-report-finds/)
- **Amazon Associates rules (Apr 2026 policy page):** no bidding on Amazon trademarks, and purchases via links shown on search engines are disqualified. Program content may not be used in advertising outside your site. — [Amazon Associates Program Policies](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/policies)
- **Trend-list caveat:** Google Year in Search lists measure spikes relative to the prior period, not total volume. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-reveals-the-top-searches-of-2025/563738/)

### Inferences
Repeatable method (my synthesis; the tool steps are standard practice and were not each separately verified in this research):

**Step 1: Find candidates (weekly, ~30 minutes, free).** Claude can run steps 1–3 on a schedule.
- Google Trends: Trending Now (US and Florida), then "Year in Search" lists for context.
- Amazon Best Sellers and Movers & Shakers in 3–4 categories (beauty, health, home, toys). A browser-enabled session is needed because direct fetch was blocked.
- TikTok Creative Center (trending hashtags, songs, products) and TikTok Shop top products. FastMoss's free blog posts give quarterly revenue lists.
- Exploding Topics free pages, Reddit (rising posts in r/FoodTrucks, r/orlando, r/smallbusiness), YouTube search autocomplete.
- Calendar events: Black Friday/Cyber Monday, hurricane season (June 1–Nov 30), GTA VI-style launches, open enrollment, tax-bill season, Hispanic Heritage Month, theme-park openings.

**Step 2: Durability test (kill most ideas here).**
- 5-year Google Trends chart: steady or rising for at least 12 months? Or a spike? A spike means video only, no asset.
- Is it tied to a durable franchise, place, behavior or recurring date?
- Check for a seasonal pattern (same bump every year = plannable).

**Step 3: Demand and competition.**
- Keyword volume via Google Keyword Planner (free with a Google Ads account, gives ranges) or a free keyword generator. Look for long-tail phrases with real volume.
- Google the top 5 terms. If page 1 is all big brands, government sites or large publishers (Disney blogs, insurers, Healthline), skip. If it's forums, Reddit and thin sites, there's an opening.
- Check the same terms in Spanish. Thin or machine-translated results suggest a Spanish gap.
- On TikTok and YouTube, count how many recent videos have strong views from small accounts. If small accounts get views, there's room.

**Step 4: Monetization fit.** Pick the model before building:
- Ads need high volume (AdSense $3–8 RPM lifestyle; see `content_sites_ads_affiliate.md`).
- Affiliate needs buying intent and a product with a program. Amazon pays low rates and has strict rules.
- A digital product or service needs a clear problem and a buyer who pays.
- Leads/sponsorships need local businesses who'd pay (Brandon's strongest path).

**Step 5: AI and platform risk.**
- Does an AI Overview already appear for the main queries? Question-style "how/what/why" queries are most exposed. Local, real-time and tool-style queries are less exposed.
- Does the plan depend on one platform's algorithm or policy (TikTok, YouTube inauthentic-content rules, Amazon Associates terms)?
- Is it YMYL (health, money, insurance)? If yes, require credentials or avoid.

**Step 6: Brandon fit and a 30-day test.**
- Does it use AI video, Spanish, Central Florida or food/local-business knowledge? Does it feed the main ad-pack business?
- Set a kill rule before starting, for example: if 20 short videos or 15 pages get fewer than X views or clicks in 30 days, stop.

**Paid tools only if needed:** Exploding Topics (~$39/mo) or Glimpse (~$30–99/mo). Neither is necessary at under $500 total budget.

### Gaps
- Google Keyword Planner, Ahrefs free tools, AnswerThePublic and TikTok Creative Center current features and limits were not verified (sites not fetched).
- No study quantifies AI Overview exposure for local or "near me" queries or for calculator/tool queries specifically. The "less exposed" claim is an inference from news/real-time being least affected (sibling notes).
