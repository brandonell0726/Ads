# Verification: content sites, video/social income, and trends notes

Fact-check date: 2026-11-04. Method: 32 load-bearing claims taken from `content_sites_ads_affiliate.md`, `video_social_creator_income.md` and `trends_and_opportunities_now.md`. I tried to refute each one with fresh sources, primary where possible: official policy, help and pricing pages, YouTube's own blog, Amazon's Operating Agreement and its "What's Changed" page, Pew, Adobe's PDF, KFF, Visit Orlando and Nielsen. That took 18 web searches and about 22 page fetches. "Accessed" means read on 2026-11-04. A claim I couldn't support is marked UNVERIFIED. The notes files were not edited.

Verdict key: **CONFIRMED** means a source supports the claim as stated (small nuances are in the note column). **CORRECTED** means the claim is wrong or its scope is misleading, and the note gives the right version. **CONFLICTING** means sources disagree. **UNVERIFIED** means I found no adequate support.

## Most important corrections (in order of how much they change Brandon's decisions)

1. **Amazon now pays nothing on purchases from ads you bought that link to Amazon (Operating Agreement, Apr 14, 2026).** "Any advertisement that you purchased" that refers a customer to Amazon disqualifies the purchase. Amazon's change log says this includes boosted posts. The only carve-out is paid search that sends people to *your own site* first. The same update adds an "original content" test (significant commentary, analysis or transformation) and tightens the session and onsite rules. This settles the open gap in the notes: the YouTube "trending Amazon product page" model can't be fed by boosted TikTok or Instagram posts or paid ads linking to Amazon. It only works with organic traffic, or with paid traffic to a page that adds real original content. That makes the idea weaker than the notes already said.
2. **PA-API 5 is already off: calls return HTTP 403.** Any page Claude builds that shows Amazon prices or product data must use the Creators API from day one. Don't plan around a "May 15" date.
3. **The YouTube 2027 rules are now confirmed by YouTube's own blog** (Aug 10, 2026): 8,000 hours or 20M Shorts views for new applicants, and a 10M per 90-day floor for Shorts revenue. So the "Shorts-only channel is not viable" conclusion stands on a primary source. But the **"45% direct payout for ads targeted to 5 or fewer channels" isn't on YouTube's post and should be removed**, and "checked monthly" is secondary only.
4. **The "typical outcome" anchors are weaker than stated.** "57% of affiliates earn under $10K/yr" can't be traced to a primary page (the year and sample conflict). The Chartbeat "small publishers −60%" figure covers about two years, not one, and comes from an Axios exclusive. The direction (small content sites are losing) holds. Flippa's H1 2026 data (content-business sales −39%) shows the decline is **continuing into 2026**.
5. **The Amazon "cuts up to 50%" story is a press report for some publishers, not a rate-card change.** Amazon disputes the scope. A new small Associate is paid from the public card (3–4.5% for most home and kitchen goods, 1% for health and grocery). That card is already low, so the economics don't improve, but the report shouldn't say every affiliate's rates were halved.
6. **The KFF "+114%, $888 to $1,904" figure is a national projection from Sept 2025, not a measured Florida result.** It matters only for candidate #14 (Florida money explainers), but stated as Florida fact it would be wrong.
7. **The details of the Jan 2026 YouTube removals differ from the notes.** The figures come from Kapwing. 11 channels were terminated and 5 wiped, and YouTube cited spam policies. The lesson (mass-produced AI channels get removed) still holds. The policy attribution should change.
8. **The GTA VI date is re-confirmed for early Nov 2026** (pre-load Nov 10, pre-orders live). Candidate #12's timing holds, but launch is 15 days away, which leaves almost no lead time.
9. Smaller corrections. The AIR Gaming and Entertainment long-form RPMs are within-channel comparisons, not niche medians. The TikTok Shop 2026 forecast is now $23.41B. The TikTok Shop AI-voice and still-image rules apply to Shop content, with effective dates between May 23 and June 22, 2026. Meta's "15 Reels in 30 days" rule isn't in Meta's post. Raptive's tier-one share rule isn't on Raptive's page.

## Claims the report should not state as fact

- That Amazon cut Associates rates "by up to 50%" for everyone, or that the cuts apply to new or small Associates. The source is a press report from publisher interviews, and Amazon disputes the scope.
- "PA-API retires May 15, 2026". Say "PA-API 5 is already deprecated (403 errors); use the Creators API."
- "57% of affiliates earn under $10K a year" and "average affiliate earns $8,038/mo". If used at all, present them as survey figures with a sampling bias.
- YouTube "pays 45% directly for ads targeted to 5 or fewer channels", and that the Shorts floor is "checked monthly".
- Any RPM as a fact: AdSense niche RPMs ($3–8 / $5–12 / $8–20 come from a competing ad network), Mediavine and Raptive RPMs ($15–35 and similar), TikTok CRP $0.40–$1.00, Spanish-language YouTube RPM $1.50–$4, and TikTok Shop "13.02% average commission". All of these are vendor or creator-reported.
- "Florida subsidized enrollees' payments rose 114%". It's a national projection.
- "71% of affiliate sites were hit by the March 2026 core update", and DealNews "−44%" in May 2026. These come from low-rigor trackers.
- The AI Mode "−18.8 percentage points" field experiment and the AOP "guide/how-to −20%" split. I saw both only through a TechWyse summary and didn't verify them.
- Raptive's 50%/40% tier-one traffic requirement, Meta's "15 Reels in 30 days", Higgsfield's "4.5M videos a day", Epic Universe's "double-digit lift" and "9.2M visitors".
- That Spanish-language content is "less saturated" or "under-served" by creators. Nielsen measures advertiser spend, not creator competition. Kapwing shows large Spanish-language AI channels already exist.
- That "AI narration is prohibited for all TikTok affiliate promotions". The documented ban covers TikTok Shop LIVEs and shoppable-video formats, and outlets differ on how far it reaches.
- That YouTube Data API uploads from unverified projects are public by default, until a test upload confirms it.
- Exploding Topics search volumes (e.g., hypochlorous acid 201K a month) as measured search data. They are that tool's own estimates.
