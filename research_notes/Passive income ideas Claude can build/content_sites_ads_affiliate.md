# Niche content websites earning from display ads and affiliate links (incl. "trending Amazon product" pages), as of late 2026

Research date: 2026-11-04. About 32 web searches plus about 20 page fetches. Most RPM figures come from vendors or blogs. Primary sources fetched: Mediavine requirements page, Raptive blog, Ezoic requirements page, AdSense eligibility help, Google spam policies and gen-AI guidance pages, Amazon commission table and Program Policies, Pew Research, Press Gazette (Chartbeat/Reuters Institute data), Cloudflare Pages limits. A number marked "secondary" was not checked against its primary source.

## 1. Ad network entry requirements and payouts (AdSense, Ezoic, Mediavine/Journey, Raptive; RPMs; Spanish vs English; pageviews needed for $100 / $1,000 / $5,000 a month)

### Takeaway
Getting into a network is easier in late 2026 than in 2024: Mediavine Journey needs 1,000 Tier-1 sessions a month and Raptive needs 25,000 pageviews. Ezoic went the other way and now wants 250,000 monthly users. Earning is still a volume game. At AdSense-level RPMs ($3–8 lifestyle, third-party estimate), $1,000 a month takes roughly 125,000–333,000 pageviews. At premium-network RPMs it takes about 30,000–70,000. No reliable 2025–2026 data compares Spanish-language and English RPMs.

### Cited Findings
- **AdSense eligibility (current help page, undated):** applicant must be 18 or older; "Your content must be high-quality, original, and attract an audience"; applicant must be able to access the site's HTML source; site must comply with AdSense Program policies before applying. The page sets **no minimum traffic and no minimum site age**. — [Google AdSense Help: eligibility](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9724)
- **AdSense privacy policy requirement:** Google's help page lists "Google advertising cookies" as required content in a publisher's privacy policy, and third-party vendor cookies must also be disclosed. Google says it can't supply specific wording because sites and laws vary. — [AdSense Help: required content / privacy](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/1348695?hl=en). Vendor guides add that EEA/UK visitors need consent before ad cookies fire (secondary). — [Termly guide](https://termly.io/resources/articles/privacy-policy-for-google-adsense)
- **Mediavine full program (fetched 2026):** "Generate a minimum of $5,000 in annual ad revenue", original audience-first content, "clean, human, brand-safe traffic", good standing with AdSense/AdExchange. The page sets no session minimum for the full program. — [Mediavine requirements](https://www.mediavine.com/mediavine-requirements/)
- **Journey by Mediavine (fetched 2026):** "1,000 sessions from Tier 1 countries" in a 30-day period. Tier 1 = US, Canada, UK, Australia. No revenue minimum. — [Mediavine requirements](https://www.mediavine.com/mediavine-requirements/). Third-party sources date the cut from 10,000 to 1,000 sessions to about **January 15, 2026**. — [Productive Blogging on Journey (2026)](https://www.productiveblogging.com/everything-you-need-to-know-about-journey-by-mediavine/); [Jupiter: Mediavine requirements 2026](https://www.jupiter.co/blog/mediavine-requirements-2026-how-to-qualify)
- **Raptive (blog last updated Oct 16, 2025):** "a minimum of 25,000 monthly pageviews to join Raptive", replacing 100,000. The Rise pilot (50k–100k) was retired into the "Insider" level. — [Raptive blog](https://raptive.com/blog/opening-the-door-to-more-creators-who-meet-raptive-quality-standards/). Secondary coverage says sites at 25k–99,999 PV need about 50% of traffic from tier-one markets, and sites at 100k+ need 40%. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/raptive-drops-traffic-requirement-by-75-to-25000-views/558780/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/raptive-drops-pageview-requirement-to-25-000-monthly-visits/)
- **Ezoic (support page fetched 2026):** "250,000+ monthly active users" for new publishers. Sites that monetized before **Feb 19, 2026** are grandfathered as long as they stay continuously on Ezoic. Sites below that size may qualify for an "Incubator Program". Content must be original and in an AdSense-supported language. — [Ezoic requirements](https://support.ezoic.com/kb/article/getting-started-ezoics-requirements); [Ezoic Incubator](https://www.ezoic.com/incubator). Older pages saying Ezoic has "no minimum" (2023) are now out of date. — [Newor Media on the 250K threshold](https://newormedia.com/blog/?p=1430)
- **AdSense RPM by niche (third-party estimates, 2026, unverified; the source sells an alternative ad network):** Finance/B2B $8–20, Health/Wellness $5–12, Lifestyle $3–8. — [Newor Media: AdSense alternatives 2026](https://newormedia.com/blog/best-adsense-alternatives/). US traffic pays the most, and India/SEA traffic pays under $1 RPM (calculator site). — [MonetizeMore 2026](https://www.monetizemore.com/blog/how-much-should-my-website-be-earning/)
- **Mediavine/Raptive RPM:** Mediavine publishes no average and says RPMs "are cyclical" and that "mileage will vary". Its income reports are voluntary. — [Mediavine RPM page](https://www.mediavine.com/?p=1738); [Mediavine income reports](https://www.mediavine.com/mediavine-income-reports/). Third-party estimates conflict: Mediavine $15–35 vs $25–52, Raptive $25–50 vs $12–32, with no methodology given. — [Hakaru blog revenue guide](https://hakaru.io/guides/blog-ad-revenue-calculator-guide); [EarnifyHub 2026 RPM data](https://earnifyhub.com/blog/blogging/mediavine-vs-raptive-vs-ezoic-rpm). Raptive claims travel creators who switched in 2025 averaged a 103% RPM lift (vendor claim, unaudited). — [Raptive TBEX page](https://raptive.com/tbex/)
- **Example of established-site earnings (anecdotal, early 2026):** one blogger's income reports show Mediavine-monetized sites at about $1,300–$1,600 a month. — [Niche Safari income report](https://nichesafari.kit.com/posts/february-income-report)
- **Spanish vs English:** I found no systematic 2025–2026 data. Old anecdotes only: a 2008 Google AdSense blog case study of a Spanish music site, and forum posts from 2007 and 2016 that disagree (one says Spanish earns about a third more than English, another says Spanish eCPM is "somewhat low"). — [AdSense blog 2008](https://adsense.googleblog.com/2008/01/sounds-of-success-for-buenamusica.html); [WebmasterWorld 2016](https://www.webmasterworld.com/google_adsense/4816991.htm). An undated ad-network page lists country RPMs of Spain $4.60 and Chile $3.62. — [Adcash](https://adcash.com/?p=6959)

### Inferences
- **Pageviews needed** (calculated as revenue = pageviews ÷ 1,000 × RPM, using the cited third-party RPM ranges; not measured):
  - AdSense at $3–8 RPM (lifestyle): **$100/mo ≈ 12,500–33,000 PV; $1,000/mo ≈ 125,000–333,000 PV; $5,000/mo ≈ 625,000–1.67M PV.**
  - AdSense at $8–20 RPM (finance/B2B): $100 ≈ 5,000–12,500 PV; $1,000 ≈ 50,000–125,000; $5,000 ≈ 250,000–625,000.
  - Mediavine/Raptive at roughly $15–35 RPM: $1,000 ≈ 29,000–67,000 PV; $5,000 ≈ 143,000–333,000 PV. A new, small site will probably sit at the low end.
- At Mediavine's $5,000 annual ad-revenue gate (~$417/mo), a site at $20 RPM would need roughly 21,000 PV a month.
- Ad networks group traffic by country, not language (Mediavine "Tier 1 countries", Raptive "tier-one markets"). So US Spanish-speaking readers presumably count as US/Tier-1 traffic. Spanish-language traffic from Latin America would not. This is my reading of the requirement pages and is not tested.
- Ezoic's 250k threshold removes the usual "AdSense first, then Ezoic" step for small sites. The path is now AdSense → Journey (1k sessions) → Raptive (25k PV) or Mediavine ($5k/yr).

### Gaps
- No official AdSense RPM data by niche exists. All niche figures are vendor or blog estimates.
- AdSense payment threshold ($100) and the "site must be X months old" rule for some countries were not confirmed on the fetched page.
- Mediavine/Raptive revenue-share percentages were not found on the fetched pages.
- No credible current Spanish-vs-English RPM comparison for US Hispanic audiences.

## 2. Amazon Associates 2025–2026 (commissions, 24-hour cookie, 3 sales/180 days, key rules, Influencer program) and better-paying networks

### Takeaway
Amazon's public rate card is low: 3% for Home, Furniture, Toys, Beauty, Pets, Sports; 4.5% Kitchen; 1% Grocery and Health & Personal Care; 10% only for Luxury Beauty. In 2026 Amazon also quietly cut negotiated and bonus rates by up to 50% for some publishers (US from about March 9, 2026). The rules ban trademark bidding, redirect links, cloaking, offline/ebook links, and using Amazon content in ads outside your own site. Prices may only be shown with API data or a timestamp, and PA-API was replaced by the Creators API in 2026. An Amazon page lives on thin margins and depends on Amazon's rules.

### Cited Findings
- **Public US commission table (fetched Nov 2026, no effective date shown):** 10% Luxury Beauty / Luxury Stores Beauty / Amazon Explore; 5% Digital & Physical Music, Handmade, Digital Videos; 4.5% Physical Books, Kitchen, Automotive; 4% Amazon devices, Apparel, Watches, Jewelry, Luggage, Shoes, Handbags, and "All Other Categories"; **3% Toys, Furniture, Home, Home Improvement, Lawn & Garden, Pets, Headphones, Beauty, Musical Instruments, Business/Industrial, Outdoors, Tools, Sports, Baby**; 2.5% PC/components; 2% TVs, digital video games; **1% Amazon Fresh, physical video games/consoles, Grocery, Health & Personal Care**; 0% gift cards, alcohol, restaurant food, etc. Bounties: Prime free trial/paid/sign-up $12, Audible monthly $20, Amazon Business $15, Prime Video trial $2. — [Amazon Associates commission schedule](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ)
- **2026 unannounced cuts:** Amazon cut commission rates "by as much as 50%", removed milestone-based bonuses, and degraded reporting tools. The rollout began in Asia-Pacific in late 2025 and reached the US "around March 9" (2026). Publishers heard of it one-on-one from account managers, and the change was never publicly announced (Adweek, seven unnamed publishers; paywalled). — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/amazon-associates-affiliate-rate-cuts-publishers/). Shopifreaks (May 21, 2026): some rates fell from a "premium 10%" to 4–5%; escalating bonus tiers "largely disappeared"; **publishers relying on paid media were hit hardest**, while those with long editorial ties kept better terms; one deal site halved its 2026 Amazon revenue projection. — [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/amazon-quietly-slashed-associates-affiliate-commission-rates-by-up-to-50-across-publisher-ecosystem-and-gutted-reporting-tools/). One secondary source says same-category attribution was also removed (unconfirmed). — [NovaData](https://novadata.io/resources/news/amazon-associates-commission-cut-may-2026)
- **2025 category history (secondary blog):** Furniture, Home, Lawn & Garden and Outdoors fell from 8%/6% (2019-era) to 3%. This matches the current public table. — [Voluum blog](https://voluum.com/blog/?p=4661)
- **24-hour window:** the Operating Agreement's Commission Income Statement counts qualifying purchases in a session that starts with the click and ends when "24 hours elapse from that click". — [Amazon Program Policies / Operating Agreement (updated Apr 14, 2026)](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/policies)
- **3 qualifying sales in 180 days:** Amazon's help pages say an applicant has 180 days to refer 3 qualifying sales before the application is reviewed. The application can be withdrawn if that doesn't happen, and a rejected account/ID can't be reinstated. Secondary sources say own orders don't count and each sale must be a separate order. — [Amazon Associates help (Account Dormancy)](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/G7MJTPEP9NC3YKMG); [TechTactician](https://techtactician.com/amazon-affiliate-3-sales-in-180-days)
- **Rules (Program Policies "Updated: April 14, 2026"):**
  - No promotion "in any offline manner"; banned formats include "any printed material, ebook, mailing, or any oral solicitation".
  - Special Links are allowed in emails, SMS and social DMs only if the recipient opted in.
  - Prices and availability may be shown only if the link serves them or you use PA API / Creators API data, with a date/time stamp if you refresh less than hourly. Non-image content can be cached up to 24 hours. Product images may not be stored or cached.
  - "You will not bid on or purchase keywords… covering Amazon trademarks". Purchases via a link "generated or displayed on a search engine" are disqualified, as are prohibited paid search placements.
  - Redirect links that "send users indirectly to an Amazon Site via an intermediate site" are disqualifying. No cloaking or obscuring of the Site's URL, and shorteners may not hide that the link goes to Amazon.
  - Program Content may not be used "within advertising outside of your Site". The page does not name Meta/TikTok paid ads specifically.
  - Influencers should use "#Ad"/"#Sponsored".
  — [Amazon Associates Program Policies](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/policies)
- **PA-API → Creators API:** Amazon's PA-API documentation gives a retirement date of **May 15, 2026** and directs developers to the Creators API (OAuth 2.0, new credentials created in Associates Central by the primary account owner). Plugin vendors cite April 30, 2026 or earlier dates, which conflict. — [Amazon PA-API docs](https://webservices.amazon.com/paapi5/documentation/read-la.html); [Create plugin release notes](https://create.studio/releases/create-plugin-2-4-0)
- **Amazon Influencer program (secondary only):** open to YouTube/Instagram/Facebook/TikTok creators with no strict follower minimum. "Onsite commissions" for videos placed on Amazon product pages need a separate approval. Amazon doubled influencer commissions temporarily for Prime Day 2025. — [Helium 10 (2025)](https://www.helium10.com/blog/your-roadmap-to-make-money-with-the-amazon-influencer-program-in-2025/); [Camphouse: Prime Day 2025](https://camphouse.io/news/amazon-influencer-commissions-prime-day-2025)
- **Other networks:** ShareASale was folded into Awin. User migration was completed about **Aug 15, 2025**, and the ShareASale platform closed **Oct 6, 2025**. — [Awin announcement](https://www.awin.com/us/news-and-events/awin-news/shareasale-to-awin-upgrade); [Hello Partner](https://hellopartner.com/2025/08/19/shareasale-to-sunset-in-us-as-awin-migration-wraps-up/)
- **Affiliate commission context:** Commission Factory and Shopifreaks frame the Amazon cuts as a reason to diversify, and Shopifreaks suggests TikTok Shop could gain. — [Commission Factory](https://www.commissionfactory.com/market-insights/affiliate-marketing/amazon-commission-changes-how-affiliates-can-reduce-their-losses); [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/amazon-quietly-slashed-associates-affiliate-commission-rates-by-up-to-50-across-publisher-ecosystem-and-gutted-reporting-tools/)

### Inferences
- Illustrative math, with my own assumptions and no cited conversion data: a $30 Home/Kitchen item at 3–4.5% earns about $0.90–$1.35 per sale. $1,000 a month would need roughly 740–1,100 sales a month. The 24-hour cookie helps a little, because other items bought in the same session also count.
- A site that shows live Amazon prices needs Creators API access, which is tied to an approved Associates account. A static page without prices avoids the price/timestamp rule.
- "Publishers relying on paid media were hit hardest", together with the paid-search bans, suggests Amazon is squeezing paid-traffic arbitrage. This is directly relevant to a "trending product page" fed by ads.
- Brand programs and networks (Awin, Impact, CJ) often pay more than 3–4%, but this was not researched in depth here.

### Gaps
- No primary-source details on the 2026 cuts (which categories, which publisher types, whether the public rate card for small/new Associates changed). The public table shows no effective date.
- Whether Creators API access has a minimum-sales requirement (PA-API historically did) — not found.
- Impact.com and CJ terms, and typical brand-program rates in beauty/fitness/kitchen, were not researched.
- Official Amazon Influencer program requirements were not fetched.

## 3. Google Search changes and enforcement 2024–2026, Google's stance on AI content, and what still grows

### Takeaway
Google does not ban AI content. It does ban "using generative AI tools… to generate many pages without adding value for users" (scaled content abuse), copied-merchant "thin affiliation", site reputation abuse and expired-domain abuse, and it applies manual actions that can remove whole sections from the index. Affiliate and AI-heavy roundup sites were reportedly hit hardest in the March 2026 core update; that figure comes from third-party trackers only. Sites that still grow are narrowly focused, show real expertise, and add something original such as testing, photos, data or local knowledge.

### Cited Findings
- **Spam policies (Google Search Central, fetched Nov 2026):**
  - Scaled content abuse = "many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users". Example: "Using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users".
  - Site reputation abuse = "third-party content is published on a host site mainly because of that host's already-established ranking signals".
  - Expired domain abuse = an expired domain "repurposed primarily to manipulate search rankings".
  - Thin affiliation = "product descriptions and reviews are copied directly from the original merchant". Good affiliate pages add "original reviews, testing, price information, or product comparisons".
  — [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **Origin:** these policies (expired domain, scaled content, site reputation abuse) were announced with the March 2024 core update. In the same post Google said the helpful content system would become part of core ranking systems; I did not re-fetch the post this session to confirm that wording. — [Google Search Central blog, March 2024](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies); [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-march-2024-spam-updates-37002.html)
- **Google's gen-AI guidance (fetched, undated):** AI "can be particularly useful when researching a topic". Generating many pages without added value may violate spam policy. Publishers should "manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content for accuracy and trustworthiness before publishing", including titles, meta descriptions, structured data and alt text. Merchant Center requires AI-generated product data to be labeled, and AI images should carry IPTC DigitalSourceType metadata. Google suggests disclosing how content was created. — [Google: Using generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content). Secondary sources report an update to this page on **Oct 1, 2026** (unverified). — [Originality.ai study page](https://originality.ai/blog/ai-content-seo-study)
- **Quality Rater Guidelines (Jan 2025):** raters are told to give the lowest rating to pages whose main content is created with automated/generative AI tools with little effort or originality. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-quality-raters-content-ai-generated-lowest-quality-454161)
- **March 2024 deindexing wave:** Originality.ai examined about 79,000 sites on certain ad networks and found that roughly 1,146–1,446 were deindexed by manual actions, about 1.5–1.9% (Niche Pursuits' page and transcript give conflicting counts). 100% of the deindexed sites showed AI-content signs, and 50% had 90–100% AI-generated posts. — [Niche Pursuits (Mar 15, 2024)](https://www.nichepursuits.com/?p=133767); [MarTech Series on Originality.ai study](https://martechseries.com/predictive-ai/ai-platforms-machine-learning/100-of-websites-deindexed-by-googles-update-showed-signs-of-using-ai-new-study-by-originality-ai-finds/)
- **2026 manual action example:** NationalToday.com published 850K+ AI-generated "local news" URLs under /us/. The section surged in the March 2026 core update, then received a "Scaled content abuse" manual action and was quickly removed from the index. It then also dropped out of AI Overviews/AI Mode and mostly out of ChatGPT citations (Glenn Gabe, Apr 20, 2026). — [GSQI: When "Mt. AI" crumbles](https://www.gsqi.com/marketing-blog/when-mt-ai-crumbles-chatgpt-follows/)
- **2025 scaled-content enforcement:** secondary sources describe a 2025 wave of manual actions (dated about June 2025 by some, early 2025 by others) with Search Console notices citing "aggressive spam techniques, such as large-scale content abuse". Dates conflict. — [Blck Alpaca knowledge base](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/seo-geo/technical-seo/ai-content-and-google-enforcement-and-eu-ai-act)
- **March 2026 core update (third-party trackers, low rigor):** affiliate sites were the hardest-hit category, with about 71% of tracked affiliate sites negative (JetDigitalPro data relayed by Pulse). Losses were concentrated on templated "best X under $Y" roundups and AI-heavy sites with no editorial review, and focused niche sites with demonstrated expertise fared better. Reported rollout dates range from Mar 10 to Mar 27. — [Pulse: March 2026 core update hit affiliates](https://www.pulse.bot/marketing/news/googles-march-2026-core-update-hit-affiliate-sites-harder-than-any-other-category-97b2f86f-68a0-4268-82d2-138396e7fb55/); [On-Page.ai](https://blog.on-page.ai/googles-march-2026-core-update-winners-losers-and-what-changed)
- **May 2026 core update:** one tracker reports DealNews (an affiliate deals site) lost 44% of traffic. — [Rank Math tracker](https://rankmath.com/google-update/may-2026-core-update/)
- **February 2026 Discover update:** favored locally based publishers in the user's country. — [Over The Top SEO](https://www.overthetopseo.com/?p=46492)
- **August 2026:** a spam update ran **Aug 18–21, 2026** and did not target site reputation abuse. On **Aug 28, 2026** Google announced that Site Reputation Policy manual actions would no longer affect results shown to EEA users from Aug 30, 2026. The policy stays in force elsewhere, including the US. — [Google Search Central blog: Update to the Site Reputation Policy](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/08/update-site-reputation-policy); [Relevant Audience on the Aug 2026 spam update](https://www.relevantaudience.com/seo/google-august-2026-spam-update/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-updates-site-reputation-abuse-policy-removes-penalties-in-eea/587429/)
- **September 2026 spam update:** reported by a single outlet (Daily Pakistan) as targeting scaled content. Unconfirmed. — [Daily Pakistan](https://en.dailypakistan.com.pk/?p=342520)
- **AI-assisted success case (old):** Casey Botticello grew an AI-assisted tech-niche site to about $21,700/month (Mediavine ads, about 550k sessions projected) within about 12 months. The first 29 posts were human-written; later posts were drafted with Koala Writer/GPT-3.5 and then edited heavily (1–3 hours each), at about $2–3 per AI article. Page last updated **Feb 5, 2024**, so this predates the March 2024 policies and the 2025–2026 updates. — [Niche Pursuits podcast](https://www.nichepursuits.com/?p=128551)

### Inferences
- A Claude-run "publish lots of pages automatically" site is the pattern Google names as scaled content abuse. It can work for a while ("Mt. AI" surge) and then lose whole sections overnight, and since Gabe's case the loss can spread to AI search citations.
- For Brandon, a site that adds original value and couldn't be mass-produced is safer: real local photos and video (Central Florida food trucks/restaurants), bilingual first-hand content, actual product testing with his own footage. Human review of every page fits Google's own guidance.
- Affiliate roundup formats ("best X under $Y") look like the most exposed format in 2026.

### Gaps
- Google publishes no manual-action counts. Category-level impact figures for 2025–2026 updates come from SEO vendors with unclear methods.
- The exact rollout dates and Google announcements for the 2025 core/spam updates (e.g., March, June, December 2025) were not verified this session.

## 4. AI Overviews and AI Mode: measured impact on publisher clicks, and which query types lost most

### Takeaway
Clicks from Google fall by roughly half when an AI Overview appears: Pew 15% → 8% (March 2025), Ahrefs −58% for position 1 (Dec 2025), Seer −61% organic CTR (Sept 2025). Google search referrals to publishers fell about 38% year on year in the US to November 2025 (Chartbeat), and small publishers were reportedly hit hardest. Long, question-style informational queries and guide/how-to content are the most exposed. News, sports and real-time queries are the least affected.

### Cited Findings
- **Pew Research (published July 22, 2025):** 900 US adults, browsing data from March 1–31, 2025. 68,879 unique Google searches; 18% produced an AI summary. Users clicked a traditional result on **8%** of visits with an AI summary vs **15%** without. Only **1%** clicked a link inside the summary. 26% of AI-summary pages ended the browsing session vs 16% without. AI summaries appeared for 8% of 1–2 word searches, 53% of 10+ word searches, and 60% of queries starting with question words. Wikipedia, YouTube and Reddit were the top-cited sources. — [Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/). Google disputes the findings. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/07/24/googles-ai-overviews-eating-internet-search-traffic/)
- **Ahrefs (published Feb 4, 2026, data to Dec 2025):** AI Overviews reduce position-1 CTR by **58%** (300,000 keywords; position-1 CTR for AIO keywords fell from 0.073 in Dec 2023 to 0.016 in Dec 2025). Drops of about −50.8% at position 2 and −19.4% at position 10. The April 2025 version measured −34.5%. Ahrefs calls this a correlation. — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update)
- **Seer Interactive (Sept 2025 update):** 3,119 informational queries across 42 organizations, June 2024–Sept 2025. Organic CTR on AIO queries fell from 1.76% to 0.61% (−61%), and paid CTR fell from 19.7% to 6.34% (−68%). Queries without AIOs also fell (organic −41%). Brands cited in the AIO saw 35% higher organic CTR (correlation). A later Seer study (Jan 2025–Feb 2026) reportedly supersedes this one but was not checked. — [Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-september-2025-update)
- **Similarweb (July 2025):** zero-click share of news searches rose from 56% (May 2024) to 69% (May 2025). Organic visits to news sites fell from more than 2.3B a month (mid-2024) to under 1.7B (May 2025). — [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/similarweb-google-zero-click-search-growth-39706.html)
- **Chartbeat via Reuters Institute (Press Gazette, Jan 12, 2026; year to Nov 2025):** Google search referrals were down **33% globally and 38% in the US** year on year. Google Discover was down 21% globally and 29% US. Facebook referrals rose 9% and X 15% globally. ChatGPT was 0.02% of referrals and Perplexity 0.002%. Media leaders expect search traffic to fall a further 43% on average over three years. — [Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/digital-journalism/google-traffic-down-2025-trends-report-2026/)
- **By publisher size (Chartbeat, March 2026, secondary):** search referrals fell 60% for small publishers, 47% for medium and 22% for large. — [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/googles-ai-search-features-cut-publisher-traffic-data-from-s-d1c19012)
- **AI Mode (preregistered field experiment, 1,100 participants, 2026):** assignment to AI Mode-only cut publisher click-through by **18.8 percentage points** compared with current Search. — [TechWyse summary](https://www.techwyse.com/news/search-seo/google-ai-mode-reduces-publisher-clicks-study-2026)
- **Query and content types:**
  - An AOP study of 8 UK publishers (July 2026) found Google referral declines above 20% for guide/how-to content, 18% for consumer/B2B, about 5% for news, and 4% for reportage. — [TechWyse / AOP coverage](https://www.techwyse.com/news/search-seo/google-ai-mode-reduces-publisher-clicks-study-2026)
  - Semrush/Datos found real-time categories (news, sports) the least affected by AIOs. — [same](https://www.techwyse.com/news/search-seo/google-ai-mode-reduces-publisher-clicks-study-2026)
  - Cloudflare data cited by the NYT shows human traffic to software, IT, retail and finance sites down as much as 40% in under a year. — [AI Weekly on NYT](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/nyt-googles-ai-search-is-choking-open-web-referrals)

### Inferences
- "How to…", "what is…" and long informational queries, the classic AdSense-blog content, are the most exposed. A new informational site would be starting where clicks are shrinking fastest. The Pew finding that 60% of question-word queries trigger AI summaries supports this.
- Small publishers are losing proportionally more (−60% vs −22% for large), which works against a new solo site.
- Content tied to time, place, or a real person (local events, local food trucks, a creator's own reviews and video) is less replaceable by an AI summary. This is inferred from news/real-time being least affected.

### Gaps
- No credible 2025–2026 data broken out specifically for recipes, product reviews or local queries.
- Google has published no CTR data for AI Overviews or AI Mode.

## 5. Real outcomes: income distribution, AI-site experiments, time to first $100 / $1,000 a month, and site resale values

### Takeaway
Most affiliate/niche site owners earn little. Surveys report a high average that is skewed by a few winners. New sites in anecdotal 2025–2026 reports take about 6–18 months to reach $1,000 a month, and many never do. Content sites became harder to sell: Flippa content-site sales fell 33.5% in 2025, and multiples are about 24–40× monthly profit. A site is a sellable asset only if its traffic is stable.

## 6. The "trending Amazon product page" model: organic vs paid/social traffic, Amazon paid-traffic rules, trend lifespan, post-trend performance

### Takeaway
A one-product page for a viral item is unlikely to get Google traffic in time. Most TikTok trends peak within about 72 hours and 48% fade within five days, while new pages take months to rank. Traffic would have to come from TikTok/Pinterest/paid ads. Amazon bans bidding on its trademarks and using its content in outside ads, and at 3–4% commissions paid traffic rarely pays back. After the trend fades, such pages are left with little traffic and a thin-affiliate profile.

### Cited Findings
- **Trend lifespan:** Publicis Groupe (reported April 2026): 48% of TikTok trends fade within five days. — [Pulse: Publicis on TikTok trends](https://www.pulse.bot/media/news/only-a-fraction-of-tiktok-trends-last-beyond-two-weeks-publicis-groupe-finds-7f651586-7dc4-4082-9a25-efea55509b06/). A Tubular Labs figure (secondary) says the average trend peaks within 72 hours and declines sharply after five days. — [Alibaba product insights](https://www.alibaba.com/product-insights/why-do-some-tiktok-trends-disappear-overnight-while-others-last.html). One source claims branded search spikes within 2–6 hours and returns to baseline within 48–72 hours (unsourced). — [Measure Protocol](https://www.measureprotocol.com/insights/tiktok-virality-brand-search-72-hour-tracking). Interior-design trends reportedly last about 10 months (low-rigor source). — [Best Colorful Socks stats](https://bestcolorfulsocks.com/blogs/news/tiktok-product-trend-lifespan-statistics)
- **Amazon rules that matter here (Apr 14, 2026 policies):**
  - No bidding on Amazon trademark keywords. Purchases via links displayed on a search engine are disqualified, and so are prohibited paid search placements.
  - Redirect links via an intermediate site are disqualified, and cloaking is banned.
  - Program Content may not be used "within advertising outside of your Site".
  - Special Links "must be accessed directly from your Site".
  — [Amazon Program Policies](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/policies)
- **2026 cuts hit paid-media publishers hardest** (Adweek via Shopifreaks). — [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/amazon-quietly-slashed-associates-affiliate-commission-rates-by-up-to-50-across-publisher-ecosystem-and-gutted-reporting-tools/)
- **How creators actually do it (vendor guides, 2026):** TikTok creators use link-in-bio mini landing pages (Carrd and similar) holding several Amazon links, rather than a single product site, and use tracking IDs per traffic source. — [JoinOtto: Amazon links on TikTok (2026)](https://joinotto.com/blog/how-to-add-amazon-link-to-tiktok); [Repricer: TikTok for Amazon sellers 2026](https://www.repricer.com/?p=46421)
- **Google policy risk:** thin affiliation = "product descriptions and reviews are copied directly from the original merchant". Cookie-cutter templates replicated across domains are thin. — [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)

### Inferences
- The YouTube "make a page for a hot Amazon product" approach is really social-traffic affiliate marketing. The page only works if Brandon (or a channel) makes viral short videos that send people to it. That plays to his video and Higgsfield skills, but it's an active content job, not passive income.
- The Amazon Influencer storefront or TikTok Shop affiliate route probably beats a standalone page for this use: no site needed, and no Google indexing delay. TikTok Shop's terms were not researched here.
- Evergreen "problem → product" pages (e.g., a category with lasting demand) are a better fit for search than one-off viral SKUs.
- Paid-ads math (illustrative, my own assumptions): at 3% on a $25 item ($0.75 per sale), even a $0.30 CPC with a 5% sale rate costs $6 per sale. That loses money.

### Gaps
- No case studies with numbers for single-product trending pages in 2025–2026.
- Whether Amazon allows paid social (Meta/TikTok) ads sending traffic to the Associate's own site: the policy page doesn't name these platforms, so this needs Amazon's FAQ or legal review.
- TikTok Shop affiliate commission rates and rules were not researched.
