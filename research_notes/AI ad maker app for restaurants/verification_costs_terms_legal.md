- **CONFIRMED**: a primary source, or several independent sources, support the claim as written.
- **CORRECTED**: the claim is wrong or out of date, and the right figure is given.
- **CONFLICTING**: credible sources disagree.
- **UNVERIFIED**: no support was found this session.

## Most important corrections (ordered by how much they change Brandon's decisions)

1. **Veo 3.1 is being retired, and the cost model built on it is out of date.** Google's Gemini API retired the Veo 3.1 Standard, Fast and Lite preview models on 2026-10-22; the replacement is Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash. fal's Veo 3.1 Lite endpoint will be deprecated on 2026-11-17. Omni Flash costs about $0.10/s at 720p, about twice Veo 3.1 Lite. So the budget pack's "8 s video for $0.40" and the "Quick video = 15 credits" price need redoing. At about $0.81 per 8 s, 15 credits gives roughly a 46% margin, not 73%, so the quick video should cost about 30 credits, or use a cheaper model such as Kling 2.5 on the Higgsfield API or Hailuo. Veo 3.1 is still sold through Runway's API ($0.40/s with audio), but it could be retired there too. Design the app so the video model can be swapped without code changes.
2. **Higgsfield's Terms (updated 2026-10-23) change the backend decision.** The Terms now explicitly allow building apps for end users through the API, MCP or CLI, so "commercially exploiting the Service" is not the operative bar. Two other clauses are. §11.5 bars acting as a "pass-through or service bureau" with no independent value added. §5.1(iii) bars using the Service or Outputs to build "products or services that compete with the Service", and Higgsfield sells its own ad tools. Using Ultra as the app's backend is still a bad idea: credits are non-transferable, automated use on Unlimited plans can be throttled, account sharing is banned, and the Terms never say subscription credits may fund Developer Access. **Before building on the Higgsfield API, ask Higgsfield in writing whether an AI ad-maker for restaurants counts as a competing product.** Until then, fal plus Google (or Runway) is the safer primary backend.
3. **Kling 3.0 Pro on fal costs less than the notes say:** $0.112/s without audio and $0.168/s with audio, not $0.224/$0.28. A 10 s premium clip with audio costs $1.68, not $2.80. This makes Kling 3.0 a good premium video choice now that Veo is going away.
4. **Florida's AI-likeness bills died.** SB 482 and HB 1395 (the notes' "SB 1395" is the House bill) both died on 2026-03-13. Florida has no new AI likeness law, but § 540.08 already bars unauthorized commercial use of a person's likeness. New York's synthetic-performer law (in force since 2026-06-09) applies to ads shown in New York.
5. **Vercel Hobby is for non-commercial use only.** The app needs Vercel Pro (from $20/month) once it charges customers. The $550/TB overage figure is wrong: overage starts at $0.15/GB.
6. **The FTC fake-review penalty is $53,088 per violation**, not $51,744. That is the 2025 amount, and the FTC kept it unchanged for 2026.
7. **The Stripe Tax fee depends on how the app connects to Stripe.** It is 0.5% through Checkout or Billing, but a custom API integration costs $0.50 per transaction. Stripe Managed Payments (merchant of record) costs another 3.5%. Responding to a dispute costs a further $15.
