# Competitor map: general self-serve AI ad generators (as of 2026-11-04)

Research notes for Brandon (elier.visuals), who is planning a self-serve app: a business uploads a product such as a burger, picks one of about 5 ad styles shown with example references, and gets several finished AI ads on a subscription/credits plan. Scope: general AI ad tools plus the ad platforms' own free tools, US focus. Restaurant-specific marketing tools are covered by another researcher and only mentioned in passing here.

How these notes were made, and how far to trust them: WebSearch was the only tool that reliably reached sources, and most vendor pricing pages could not be fetched. Most pricing figures below therefore come from third-party pricing trackers and review blogs, and several of those are run by competitors (Creatify, Shhots, Superscale, Novoads, AdMakeAI publish "X pricing" pages about rivals). Prices are dated to the source's own check date where one was given. Treat every price as "reported", and check the vendor's live page before quoting it.

## 1. Which general AI ad generators exist, what do they do, and what do they cost?

### Takeaway
The market is crowded and well funded at the "e-commerce/DTC UGC video" end: Arcads, Creatify, MakeUGC, HeyGen, Captions/Mirage, Topview, Pippit and Higgsfield. Most charge about $20–$130/month on a monthly credit allowance where unused credits expire, with agency/pro tiers at $250–$550+. Static-image ad generators (AdCreative.ai, Predis, Quickads, Pencil, Omneky) are older and cheaper per asset but face "generic output" complaints. Prices moved up sharply during 2026: Creatify's Pro price doubled, and Arcads moved to per-minute billing.

### Inferences
- The going rate for a self-serve "AI video ad" seat is about $25–$130/month. Real per-ad cost on avatar tools runs roughly $4–$15 per finished video ad: Arcads works out to about $11 per minute-long actor ad at list price, and HeyGen product placement to about $3–$6 per ad on a $29 plan. On image tools it is under $1–$2 per downloaded static.
- Nearly every tool uses monthly credits that expire, and 2026 saw price rises (Creatify Pro doubled, Arcads went per-minute, AdCreative reshuffled credits). That suggests vendors' compute costs and margins are under pressure, and it leaves room for simpler, more predictable pricing.
- Almost all of these products target e-commerce/DTC, apps and agencies, not local restaurants or food trucks. The "product URL → ad" workflow assumes a Shopify-style product page that a food truck doesn't have.

### Gaps
- No official pricing page could be fetched for any vendor. All prices are third-party, and several come from competitor-run pricing pages.
- Could not confirm whether Arcads actors can currently hold real products; sources conflict.
- Did not find a 2026 public price list for Mirage/Captions "AI Ads" specifically. A 2025 report mentioned a $399/mo business tier, but no primary source was found.
- Could not find official Zeely or Topview credit-cost-per-ad tables.

## 2. What free generative ad tools do the ad platforms themselves offer, how good are they, and how much do they threaten a paid app?

### Takeaway
By late 2026 every major platform gives advertisers free photo-to-ad and photo-to-video generation inside its ads manager: Meta Advantage+ creative and Ads Creative Studio, Google Asset Studio with Veo, and Google Labs Pomelli (free, aimed at SMBs, website → on-brand campaigns, product "Photoshoot", "Animate" video and Instagram publishing). TikTok Symphony Creative Studio uses Seedance, and Amazon's Video Generator is unlimited and free. Meta says nearly 10 million advertisers use its generative creative tools. Quality and brand-safety complaints about Meta's auto-generated creative are widespread, though. That is the main opening a curated paid tool still has.

### Inferences
- "Upload a product photo → get an ad image or video" is now a free commodity for anyone who opens Meta Ads Manager, Google Ads, TikTok Ads or Pomelli. Pomelli is the most direct free threat to a small-business-facing app, because it is website-driven, brand-aware, makes product photos and short video, and posts to Instagram.
- Meta's publicly documented failures (off-brand swaps, toggles that re-enable themselves, the advertiser held legally responsible) show that owners still want control, review and taste. A paid app can still win on reliability and curation, on not misrepresenting the food, and on making the result feel local and human. It loses if it only sells "generation".
- The platform tools are built for people already running paid ads. Many food trucks and small restaurants mainly post organically, and the platform tools don't serve that well (Pomelli's Instagram publishing is the exception).

## 3. Which tools offer "style templates" / "ad formats" the user picks from, and how do they show examples?

### Takeaway
Picking a format or template is standard. Usually it is a gallery of example thumbnails or videos by category: UGC/testimonial, product shot, before/after, unboxing, marketplace, hook-led, funnel stage. ## 7. What gaps remain for an indie entrant?

### Takeaway
The gaps an indie entrant can realistically use are a *vertical* (local restaurants and food trucks), *language/locale* (bilingual English/Spanish for Central Florida), *truthfulness* (improving real food footage and photos rather than inventing food), *trust* (simple pricing, easy cancel, no expiring credits), and *done-with-you posting* (organic Reels/TikTok/Google Business Profile rather than only paid ads). Raw "photo → ad" generation is not a gap: the platforms give it away free.

### Cited Findings
- Nearly all of the paid leaders position around e-commerce/DTC product URLs and AI actors, not local food businesses. Examples: Arcads (6,000+ clients, DTC/app marketers) — [Arcads blog](https://www.arcads.ai/blog/arcads-raises-16m-seed); Creatify's "end-to-end AI ad agent" — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250528506486/en/Creatify-Crosses-$9M-ARR-Raises-$15.5M-Series-A-to-Launch-the-First-End-to-End-AI-Ad-Agent-for-Video); Higgsfield's "product URL → ad" — [Higgsfield help center](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/tools-and-workflows/how-do-i-use-marketing-studio-to-create-video-ads)

### Inferences
These are my analysis, not cited facts.

**Niche gaps**
- *Local restaurants and food trucks.* Styles built around food, and inputs that fit how these businesses work: a phone photo or a 10-second clip of the real dish, today's location or hours, the menu price, a "new item" drop. Generic tools assume a product URL and an AI actor holding a bottle.
- *Bilingual English/Spanish ads for Central Florida (Kissimmee/Four Corners).* Not just TTS translation, but local-sounding Spanish copy and on-screen text in both languages in one ad, which none of the reviewed tools markets as a core feature.
- *Local SMB video versus DTC.* Local businesses mostly need organic Reels/TikTok/Stories, Google Business Profile posts and the odd boosted post, not 50 variants for paid testing.

**Workflow gaps**
- Posting and scheduling to IG/FB/TikTok/Google Business Profile.
- A weekly "content pack" cadence.
- Simple results (views, calls, direction clicks) rather than ROAS dashboards.
- Canva Grow, Zeely and Pomelli are moving into posting and running ads, so this gap is narrowing for paid ads, but less so for organic local posting.

**Trust and pricing gaps**
- Flat, predictable pricing (for example, a fixed number of finished ads a month).
- Credits that don't expire.
- No charge for failed renders.
- One-tap cancel.
- An export-everything guarantee.
- Category norms run about $25–$130/mo for credits that expire. A food truck owner probably compares against "free" (Meta, Pomelli, CapCut) or "$X per video from a local creator". Hypothesis to test: price per ready-to-post pack, or a low monthly fee with a human-reviewed premium tier.

**Truthfulness as positioning**
- Improve the *real* dish (relighting, motion, steam/sizzle overlays, kinetic text, music, layout) rather than generating food that doesn't exist.
- Offer a built-in AI-disclosure line. This also matches Brandon's CLAUDE.md rule about telling businesses that samples are AI-generated.

**Hybrid moat**
- Brandon is a working local video creator. An "AI draft in minutes + optional human polish/shoot" tier is something pure-SaaS competitors and free platform tools can't offer, and it echoes Icon's reported move to human UGC packages.

**Biggest threats**
- Google Pomelli (free, SMB-focused, website/brand-aware, product photoshoot, Animate video, Instagram publishing).
- Meta's free in-Ads-Manager generation.
- Higgsfield itself, which is the supplier and already offers Marketing Studio.

### Gaps
- No data found on local restaurants' or food trucks' willingness to pay for AI ad tools, or on their adoption of Meta/Pomelli generative tools.
- No benchmark of AI video tools rendering food specifically (burger structure, cheese, steam), and no study comparing engagement for AI-enhanced versus fully AI-generated food ads.
- Restaurant-specific competitors (e.g. restaurant marketing platforms with AI content) were deliberately not covered here. See the other researcher's notes.
- Could not verify Google Pomelli's post-beta pricing, or its generation caps beyond "a few hundred during beta".
