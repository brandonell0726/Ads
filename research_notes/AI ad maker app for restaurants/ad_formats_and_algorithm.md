# Ad creative formats and platform algorithms (Meta, TikTok, YouTube Shorts), 2025-2026: what to build into a self-serve AI ad app for restaurants and small e-commerce brands

Research date: 2026-11-04. US focus. Note for the report writer: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through this task, so a few threads (YouTube Shorts official specs, the NPR Chili's article itself, food-vertical leaderboards) were only partly checked. Those are listed under Gaps. Motion's creative library (motionapp.com) now redirects to runneth.com. The runneth.com URLs below are the same Motion "Creative Benchmarks 2026" pages.

## 1. How Meta (Andromeda, Advantage+, GEM, Generative Recommender) and TikTok (Smart+) use creative, and what they say about diversification, volume, ads per ad set and fatigue

### Takeaway
Meta's delivery stack now picks the audience from the creative itself. Andromeda retrieves candidate ads for each person, and GEM and the 2026 LLM-based "Generative Recommender" rank them, so ads that are actually different from each other (different visuals, message, storyline, format) reach different people, and near-duplicates are grouped together as one creative. Meta's one hard official number is "at least 20 diversified ads" in Advantage+ sales/shopping campaigns, with 20-30% of budget spent on testing and new ads launched weekly. TikTok officially asks for 3-5 distinct creatives per ad group, or at least 6 assets for a Smart+ Web campaign. Winning ads are rare (about 5% of creatives), so volume of distinct concepts is what produces winners.

### Cited Findings

**Meta: system architecture and what it does with creative**
- Andromeda (Dec 2, 2024) is the first, retrieval stage of Meta's ads pipeline. It narrows "tens of millions of ad candidates" to "a few thousand", and larger ranking models then pick the ad shown. Meta reported +6% retrieval recall and +8% ads quality on selected segments. — [Engineering at Meta, Dec 2024](https://engineering.fb.com/2024/12/02/production-engineering/meta-andromeda-advantage-automation-next-gen-personalized-ads-retrieval-engine/) (figures via secondary summaries; primary page not opened)
- Meta for Business (Apr 22, 2025): advertisers "have shifted from narrow demographic, interest, and behavior targeting toward creative variety as the main way to find relevant audiences". Creative diversification means "a wide range of ad creatives with different themes, messages, and visuals for different audience segments". Andromeda "has led to an 8% improvement in ad quality". Advantage+ sales campaigns with the streamlined setup show on average 9% lower cost per action. Quote: "The biggest constraint you can put on your Meta strategy is the constraints you place on your creative process." Case: Dribbleup kept steady performance while going from "3-4 new creatives a week to almost 50 on average". — [Meta for Business: The creative advantage / Andromeda](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/the-creative-advantage-unlocking-the-power-of-diversification-with-meta-andromeda)
- Meta for Business (Nov 19, 2024): "We recommend maintaining at least 20 diversified ads in Advantage+ shopping campaigns", "dedicating 20-30% of your overall ad budget towards testing new ads", and launching new ads weekly. It advises building variants for personas with different needs and purchase blockers. Example: younger shoppers may respond to deals and value, older shoppers to reviews and demos, and Gen Z to creator-led ads. Partnership (creator) ads reduced CPA by 19% on average. — [Meta for Business: Three steps to optimize with creative diversification](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/three-steps-to-optimize-your-performance-with-creative-diversification)
- Meta for Business (Dec 16, 2025): diversity means "unique campaign assets tailored to different potential customer personas or use cases". Ads should differ in visuals, messaging, storyline and format, and changing only the CTA text on the same visual is "iteration, not diversification". Meta's system "groups ads that look or feel alike as variations of one creative", and visually similar ads can cause fatigue. Advertisers should mix image, video and text. Ads Manager creative insights tag about 150 themes (e.g., humor, nostalgia). — [Meta for Business: Demystifying creative diversification](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/demystifying-creative-diversification)
- Meta small-business page: "When creative is too similar, our system may not recognize it as different", which can trigger a "Creative limited" or "Creative fatigue" status in the Ads Manager Delivery column. Meta's suggested Reels concepts: Listicle, "Types of", Transition, Q&A, Product Demo, POV. Its suggested tools for more variety: image-to-video animation, background generation, image expansion for other aspect ratios. — [Meta for Business: Creative differentiation](https://www.facebook.com/business/m/small-business/creative-differentiation)
- Meta also pushes consolidation and broad audiences ("Combining similar ad sets may help you spend your budget more efficiently"; "By targeting the broadest possible audience, you enable our system to find more potential customers"). It removed detailed-targeting exclusions between Jan and Apr 2025. — [Sum Digital, quoting Meta pages](https://sumdigital.com/blog/give-meta-what-it-wants); [Meta developers blog, Jan 21 2025](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2025/01/21/removal-of-detailed-targeting-exclusions/)
- "Creative is the new targeting" paraphrases Meta's direction fairly, but it is not Meta's own wording. — [Sum Digital](https://sumdigital.com/blog/give-meta-what-it-wants)
- GEM (Generative Ads Recommendation Model; engineering post Nov 10, 2025) is a ranking/recommendation foundation model. It delivered +5% ad conversions on Instagram and +3% on Facebook Feed in Q2 2025, and is about 4x more efficient at turning data and compute into performance than earlier ranking models. — [Engineering at Meta: GEM](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/11/10/ml-applications/metas-generative-ads-model-gem-the-central-brain-accelerating-ads-recommendation-ai-innovation/) (via search summaries)
- Later 2026 claims that GEM lifted conversions 18.9% and CTR 12% come from trade coverage. Analysts note Meta gave no dataset or comparison, so the figures can't be checked. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-says-its-gem-ads-model-lifted-conversions-18-9-across-its-apps/); [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/meta/meta-gem-ads-model-conversion-claims/)
- Meta "Generative Recommender" (announced Jul 29, 2026) is an LLM-based ranking system that "evaluates ad creative and user preferences together instead of scoring each ad in isolation". It runs automatically underneath Advantage+. Early trials: +15.7% conversions on Facebook, +1% app-event conversions on Instagram. 9 million small businesses use at least one Meta AI creative tool. Common Thread Collective's (CTC) advice: keep at least 5 active variants, test multiple angles and hooks, and mix video, carousel and image, because single-image statics "give the system much less to work with". — [Common Thread Collective, Jul 31 2026](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/meta-generative-recommender-q2-2026-ecommerce)
- Andromeda v2 (Oct 28, 2026) "scores every eligible ad", at over 1 billion ads/second. Meta claims +2.4% Facebook conversions "since 2025" (no baseline given). Meta says generative AI tools "have helped advertisers create hundreds of millions of new ads". Nearly 10 million advertisers used AI creative tools in Q3 2026. — [PPC Land, Oct 2026](https://ppc.land/meta-says-andromeda-lifted-facebook-conversions-2-4-since-2025/)

**Meta: ads per ad set**
- Meta has published no per-ad-set number beyond the 20-ads guidance for Advantage+ sales campaigns. Agency numbers conflict: AdScale data says 4-6 active creatives per ad set; others say 6-8 minimum and 15-25 optimal; one guide says it found no Meta source for such ranges. A Meta "six ads per ad set" cap reportedly dropped in early 2025, which I could not verify. — [AdScale](https://adscale.com/blog/how-many-ad-creatives-per-ad-set/); [Daumenstopp](https://daumenstopp.de/en/guides/meta-creatives-andromeda.html); [Karb.ai](https://karb.ai/blog/meta-ads-creative-diversification-2026)

**Practitioner data on volume, hit rate and fatigue**
- Motion Creative Benchmarks 2026: $1.29B Meta spend, 578,750 creatives and 6,015 accounts, Sep 1 2025 to Jan 1 2026. A "winner" (spend ≥10x the account median and ≥$500) is about 5% of creatives. Winners take 55% of spend, rising from 23% for micro accounts to 64% for enterprise accounts. Enterprise advertisers launch 18.8 creatives per week. Ranking is by spend, not ROAS. — [Motion/Runneth Creative Benchmarks 2026](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/)
- Hit rate by spend tier: about 3.8% for under $10K/month and about 8.2% for over $1M/month (Motion endpoints). Middle tiers are the author's estimates. Volume rule of thumb (Curtis Howland): about 1 new ad per $3,000 of monthly Meta spend. Motion puts accounts under $10K/month at about 2.8 new ads/week. Statics are 55.6% of ads overall and 64.8% for DTC (Howland, 67,852 ads, 106 brands). A static takes 30-60 minutes to make versus 4-8 hours for a video. Fatigue signals: CTR under ~1%, CTR down 20%+ week over week, prospecting frequency over 3.0. Refresh every 7-14 days at scale (operator practice). Meta publishes no official creative benchmarks. — [Taylor Sicard, Meta creative benchmarks 2026](https://taylorsicard.com/blog/meta-ads-creative-benchmarks-2026)
- Hook rate (3-second views ÷ impressions) thresholds attributed to Motion's 2025 data: under 25% weak, 25-35% solid, over 35% strong. Other guides put good at 30-40% and elite at 40%+. TikTok uses a 2-second view, so the numbers aren't comparable. — [Sepia Lab](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/hook-rate-benchmarks); [Motion glossary](https://motionapp.com/library/glossary/thumbstop-rate-hook-rate); [Taylor Sicard](https://taylorsicard.com/blog/meta-ads-creative-benchmarks-2026)
- Secondary sources put fatigue at anywhere from 10-14 days to 14-21 days. One cites a "40% faster" Reels fatigue figure from a Meta handbook that couldn't be verified. — [Sum Digital](https://sumdigital.com/blog/give-meta-what-it-wants)
- Adoption of Meta's own creative AI: more than 1M businesses in Nov 2024 ([Meta](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/three-steps-to-optimize-your-performance-with-creative-diversification)), about 8M in Q1 2026, nearly 10M in Q3 2026 ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-says-andromeda-lifted-facebook-conversions-2-4-since-2025/)).

**TikTok (official)**
- General performance ads (updated Jun 2025): 3-5 creatives per ad group, 3-5 diversified ad groups per campaign, "creatives with big differences, especially during testing". Refresh when delivery shows a consistently declining trend, and add new creatives to the existing ad group rather than making a new one. No fixed refresh cadence is given. — [TikTok Ads: Creative best practices for performance ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en)
- Smart+ Web (Apr 2025): "Add at least six creative assets during campaign creation", then "allow Smart+ to handle creative optimization". Run at least 7 days, and edits in the first 7 days can disrupt learning. — [TikTok Ads: Best practices Smart+ Web](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/best-practices-for-smart-plus-web-campaigns)
- Smart+ App: 4-6 assets at setup, then add 2-5 at a time. Smart+ Lead Gen (Jul 2025): add 2-5 new creatives without deleting old ones, and no more than 10 at once. Smart Performance (Feb 2025): at least 3. — [TikTok Smart+ App](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/best-practices-for-smart-plus-app-campaigns/); [TikTok Smart+ Lead Gen](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/best-practices-for-smart-lead-generation-campaigns); [TikTok Smart Performance](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/smart-performance-campaign-best-practices?lang=en)
- TikTok Brand Basics ties brand lift to creative count: 10+ creatives in North America (5+ APAC/LATAM, 7+ Europe). — [TikTok Ads: Brand Basics](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/best-practices-for-optimizing-brand-awareness-and-ad-recall-with-tiktoks?lang=en)

### Inferences
- For the app, a "style menu" maps directly onto what Meta and TikTok reward: each style is a different concept (visual + message + storyline + format), not a variant. Generating 5 styles from one product photo gives the algorithm 5 things to match to different people. Generating 5 colour or text variants of one style mostly counts as one creative.
- A small restaurant or brand (under $10K/month) realistically needs about 2-4 new distinct ads a week. On Meta it should keep enough live (aim for 20 in an Advantage+ sales campaign; small local budgets will run fewer) to get past "creative limited". On TikTok it needs 3-5 per ad group or 6+ for Smart+. A "weekly refresh pack" (new styles every week) fits this cadence and could be a subscription hook.
- The app should spread output across formats (9:16 video, 4:5 static, carousel), because Meta (Dec 2025) and CTC (Jul 2026) both say format mix is part of diversity.
- Since only about 5% of creatives win, the value proposition is cheap, fast, varied volume plus the ability to see which style wins. Showing per-style results back to the user would be a strong feature.

### Gaps
- Meta's "ad volume" help page reportedly advises fewer ads per ad set, with up to 10 creative assets per ad. I could not open it.
- No official Meta fatigue timeline or refresh cadence exists. The 7-14-day and 14-21-day figures are practitioner opinion.
- TikTok publishes no hit-rate or fatigue benchmarks. I found no TikTok equivalent of Motion's dataset.
- YouTube Shorts / Google Demand Gen creative-volume guidance was not researched (search budget exhausted).

## 2. Which formats perform for food and restaurants, and what drives orders and visits

### Takeaway
The evidence base for restaurants is thin and mostly agency or vendor material. It points consistently to: (a) short vertical "crave" video of food in motion (prep, plating, sizzle, pour, cheese pull), opening on the food with no logo intro; (b) offer-led and "new on the menu" creative, which matches Motion's finding that promotional and "newness" hooks have the highest hit rates across Meta; (c) menu carousels for delivery and ordering; and (d) phone-shot, behind-the-counter authenticity over studio polish. The single hard sales data point is Chili's, where social cheese-pull videos were credited with lifting the Triple Dipper from 10% of sales (2024) to 15% (2025).

### Cited Findings
- Chili's Triple Dipper (with its big mozzarella-stick cheese pull) was 10% of sales in 2024 and 15% in 2025. NPR (Dec 17, 2025) credited the rise to social-media videos posted by Chili's CMO, and also credits cheese-pull content with helping Raising Cane's, Olive Garden and The Cheesecake Factory. — [Wikipedia: Cheese pull (citing NPR, Dec 17 2025)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_pull) (secondary; NPR article itself not opened)
- Restaurant Meta playbook (May 17, 2026): a "crave" Reel of 10-20 s that opens on the food within 2 s with no logo or brand intro and shows prep or plating rather than only the finished dish. Use smartphone-style footage, on-screen captions (most watch muted) and ambient kitchen sound rather than stock music. Carousel for the menu: 2-10 cards (hero dish → supporting dishes → offer card), headlines under 40 characters naming the dish. Four-asset stack: crave video; social-proof static (dish photo + a real review quote); offer carousel of 3-5 cards built on a real time-limited offer; event/occasion ad made 3-4 weeks ahead. Refresh the crave video every 4-6 weeks or when frequency reaches 3+ in 7 days. Radius 3-4 km dense urban, 5-8 km suburban, 10-15 km destination/delivery. Avoid "Boost Post", which defaults to engagement. Most benchmarks in the piece are unsourced. — [adlibrary: Meta Ads for Restaurants 2026](https://adlibrary.com/posts/meta-ads-for-restaurants)
- The same publisher's 10 restaurant ad archetypes (May 17, 2026): ~10 s plated-dish Reel → "Reserve"; ~15 s three-scene ambience Reel for retargeting; 4-card delivery carousel (priced hero, second item, bundle, CTA); bold offer text over hero dish ("Free delivery this weekend"); clean dish photo with no text; seasonal 5-card ingredient carousel; new-location split layout with address and a "first 50 tables" offer; chef-on-camera 20 s Reel with subtitles; ~12 s delivery bag → container → plated reveal; "lunch" daypart static with price and hours, shown only 10:30-1:30. Its industry-range CTRs (Europe, 2026, unsourced; North American CPMs said to run 15-30% higher): Reels for foot traffic 1.8-2.6%, Reels for delivery 1.6-2.8%, delivery carousel 1.2-2.0% (lowest cost per order, €1.80-4.50), single-image delivery LTO 0.9-1.5%. It claims food-prep hook rates run 40-60% higher than finished-dish shots, citing an undated "Meta Creative Performance research", which is unverified. — [adlibrary: Best Restaurant Meta Ads Examples 2026](https://adlibrary.com/posts/best-restaurant-meta-ads-examples)
- Motion's 2026 Meta hook-tactic leaderboard is led by a "promotional cluster": Newness, Sale Announcement, Price Anchor, Urgency, Announcement, Offer Only, FOMO, New Product Announcement. This band hits 6-11% versus 4-8% tier averages. Giveaway, Price Anchor, Announcement, Event Announcement and Offer Only lead on spend use ratio (band ~0.9-2.2). Caveat: the window (Sep 2025-Jan 2026) includes BFCM, so promotional hooks may be inflated. — [Motion/Runneth: Top hook tactics 2026](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-hook-tactics)
- Motion's top visual format overall is the "Offer-First Banner" (prominent discount banner): 8.6% hit rate, 21.9% of creatives, 29.3% of spend. "Behind the Scene", "POV", "Before & After", "Listicle", "Montage" and "Cinematic B-Roll" all sit in the 5-9% hit-rate band. — [Motion/Runneth: Top visual formats](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-visual-formats)
- Motion lists "Food & Nutrition" as one of its 16 verticals but publishes no public leaderboard for it. Motion notes that Travel & Hospitality "expect high-production baseline quality". — [Motion/Runneth: Visual formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical); [Motion/Runneth: Top asset types](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types)
- Restaurant guides repeatedly name prep, plating, sizzling, pouring and cheese pulls as strong Reels. They say a 15-second phone video of the chef plating beats a scripted 4K crew shoot, and that TikTok Spark Ads (boosting organic posts that already have engagement) offer good ROI. A TouchBistro 2025 report says 48% of restaurant operators use TikTok, up from 26% in 2023. — restaurant marketing blogs from one search, incl. [Tablein](https://www.tablein.com/blog/restaurant-social-media-marketing-trends), [Awakened Films](https://awakenedfilms.com/restaurant-social-media-marketing/), [Brew](https://www.wearebrew.com/digital-marketing-hospitality-blog/restaurant-content-2026/) (search summaries; I did not confirm which blog carries each claim)
- TikTok's official creative guidance favours featuring "creators, employees, or customers", a "DIY or not overly polished style", and trends, memes or challenges. — [TikTok Ads: Creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en)
- In the 2026 Taboola/Columbia/Harvard/TUM/CMU study of AI vs human ads, "food and drink" was one of the sectors where AI creative performed strongly. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-ads-perform-as-well-as-humans-if-they-dont-look-ai-made/)
- Store visits: the most-cited Meta restaurant case is old. Church's Chicken (Feb-Oct 2017) used the Store Traffic objective with a "Get Directions" CTA instead of an order link. Rigorous 2025-2026 Meta restaurant store-visit case studies were not found. Dine-in footfall is hard to attribute, so guides suggest offer redemptions and promo codes. — [HubSpot Facebook case studies](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/facebook-case-study); [Jolí app hospitality guide](https://joliapp.com/blog/meta-ads-hospitality-guide/)
- One article claims "+47% CTR with visible price vs without price (Meta Ads, 2025)" with no link. Unverified. — [contenidorestaurante.com](https://contenidorestaurante.com/EN/food-reels-in-restaurants-myth-vs-reality-2026.html)

### Inferences
Ranked restaurant/food styles for the app. Ranking weighs strength of evidence, fit to the algorithm, and how feasible each is from one uploaded photo.

1. **"Crave" close-up video (sizzle / pull / pour / steam, slow-motion or ASMR-style).** 9:16, 6-15 s (10-20 s per the playbook). Food on screen in frame 1, product in the first 2-3 s, no logo intro, dish name and price as a caption, end card with the CTA (Order / Directions / Reserve). Placements: Reels, TikTok, Shorts, Stories. Evidence: Chili's sales lift, the playbook, TikTok's "key message early" research. AI-generated motion of real food must still look like the dish actually served (see Q5 on misrepresentation).
2. **Offer-first / LTO price banner.** Static 4:5 for feed plus a 9:16 story version. Big offer text ("2 tacos + drink $9.99 – this week only") over the hero dish. Evidence: Motion's #1 format by spend share and its promotional hook cluster. The BFCM caveat applies.
3. **"New on the menu" / launch announcement.** Video or static with a "NEW" hook, using Motion's top hook tactic, Newness. Good for LTOs and seasonal items.
4. **Menu carousel.** 4:5 or 1:1, 3-5 cards: hero dish → 2 supporting items with prices → bundle or offer card → CTA. Good for delivery and ordering, and organic carousels get high saves (Q6). Caveat: Motion ranks carousel last among asset types for e-commerce winners.
5. **Behind-the-counter / chef POV** ("POV: you work the line at…"; prep and plating). This one needs real footage or careful AI. AI chefs or staff trigger the NY synthetic-performer law and consumer distrust (Q5). It is best as a template where the business uploads phone clips.
6. **Before/after: plain phone photo → styled ad** (Brandon's existing style). The opening "before" frame acts as a pattern interrupt, and Before & After sits in Motion's 5-9% band. It doubles as a demo of the app itself. Risk: the "after" must not misrepresent portion or ingredients.
7. **Real-review quote card.** Dish photo plus a verbatim Google or Yelp review and star count. Text-heavy statics over-index in Motion's asset data (Q3). The quote must be real (FTC rule, Q5).
8. **Delivery "unboxing" reveal** (bag → container → plated). Motion's Unboxing has a 9.8% hit rate (e-commerce data). Suited to restaurants that rely on DoorDash or Uber Eats.
9. **Local listicle** ("3 things you have to order at ___ in Kissimmee", "Best birria in Four Corners"). Listicle is in Motion's band and is one of Meta's suggested concepts.
10. **Food truck "where we are this week" schedule card.** Weekly-refresh static or story. No performance data exists, but it is pure utility and guarantees weekly new creative.
11. **Stop-motion / "types of" grid of menu items.** Stop motion is in Motion's Health & Wellness top-10 by hit rate. There is no food-specific data.

### Gaps
- No public food/restaurant vertical leaderboard: Motion's Food & Nutrition data sits in a PDF (pages 15-20) that was too large to fetch.
- No rigorous 2025-2026 data on which creative drives orders or visits (as opposed to CTR). Most restaurant benchmarks are unsourced agency ranges, many from European markets.
- The NPR Chili's article itself was not opened. The 10%→15% figure comes via Wikipedia.
- No data was found on "POV you're hungry" specifically, taste-test or customer-reaction formats for restaurants, or ASMR food ads.
- TikTok's restaurant-specific ad products and benchmarks were not researched.

## 3. Which formats perform for e-commerce / DTC brands (UGC, demo, unboxing, before/after, comparison, statics), with hit-rate, ROAS, CTR and hook data

### Takeaway
Motion's 2026 Meta dataset is the best large-scale evidence. Offer-first banners, demos, testimonials and unboxings capture the most spend and have the highest hit rates. Before & After, Listicle, Split Screen, Us vs Them, Montage, Founder and Review sit just behind (5-9% hit rate). Simple text-forward statics ("text only", "product image with text") are the best asset types by hit rate and spend share, while carousel and animation are the worst. Vertical matters: Stitch and reaction videos lead Health & Wellness, and Post-it notes, quizzes and stylized product shots lead apparel. UGC-style creative beats studio polish on CTR and hook rate, though most UGC numbers come from vendors.

### Cited Findings
- **Motion 2026 visual-format leaderboard (Meta, Sep 2025-Jan 2026):** Offer-First Banner 8.6% hit rate / 21.9% of creatives / 29.3% of spend / spend use ratio 1.3. Demo 8.1% / 12.6% / 12.9% / 1.0. Testimonial 6.5% / 13.3% / 13.3% / 1.0. Unboxing 9.8% / 2.1% / 2.8% / 1.3. Celebrity 5.9% / 0.8% / 1.8% / 2.1. Next band (5-9% hit rate, no per-format numbers): Headline, Montage, Before & After, Listicle, Split Screen, Us vs Them, Feature Benefit Point, Cinematic B-Roll, Grid Swap, Screen Recording, Problem Agitation, Review, How-To, POV, Behind the Scene, Founder, Statistic, Influencer Endorsement, Collage, Static-to-Video Hybrid, Expert Explained. Spend-use leaders: Letter (~1.7), Unconventional Text Placement (~1.5), Post-It (~1.3). — [Motion/Runneth: Top visual formats](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-visual-formats); [Motion/Runneth formats library](https://runneth.com/library/formats/)
- **Motion asset types, ranked by hit rate:** Text only, Product image with text, Lifestyle-product image, UGC, High production, GIF, Illustration, UGC mashup, Lifestyle-product image with text, Lifestyle image with text, Lifestyle image, Hybrid, Product image, Animation, Carousel (band ~4-12%). "Animation and Carousel under-capture spend relative to their volume share." — [Motion/Runneth: Top asset types](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types)
- **Motion by vertical:** Health & Wellness top hit-rate formats: Stitch, Reaction video, Unboxing, Celebrity, Founder, Letter, Stop motion, Influencer endorsement, POV, Transformation. Its top spend-use formats: Social post mockup, Letter, Celebrity, Case study, Offer-first banner, Behind the scene, UGC overlay, Founder, Transformation, Billboard. Fashion & Apparel top hit-rate formats: Post-it, Quiz, Stylized product shot, Meme, ASMR, Product shot, Social comment, Podcast, Product showcase, Unconventional text placement. Beauty & Personal Care has no public leaderboard. — [Motion/Runneth: Visual formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical)
- Motion's hook-tactic guide names confession, bold claim, relatability, contrast and curiosity as top tactics among $1M+/month spenders. In the 2026 leaderboard the "interrupt cluster" (Confession, Contrarian, Shocking Statement, Warning, Myth Busting, Bold Claim) has lower hit rates but higher variance than promotional hooks. — [Motion: top hook formats](https://motionapp.com/events/2026-creative-strategy-bootcamp/homebase/top-hook-formats); [Motion/Runneth: Top hook tactics](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-hook-tactics)
- Another site's format table puts Stitch (12.50%) and Reaction video (11.24%) as top hit-rate formats. These figures don't match Motion's own page. — search summary of [Motion/Runneth formats](https://runneth.com/library/formats/) and third-party tables (conflict noted)
- **UGC vs studio (vendor data, directional):** a dataset of 12,000+ ads reports UGC-style CTR of 1.88% vs 1.41% for polished production on Meta (+33%) and 1.02% vs 0.71% on TikTok (+44%), and UGC hook rate of 34% vs 26% (Meta) and 41% vs 29% (TikTok). Other sources give gaps of 20-64% CTR. Spark Ads are said to deliver +25% CTR / +24% CVR. Running 3+ UGC variants is said to give 40-60% lower CPA. Creator content loses power when brands add corporate intros or slick transitions. UGC lifespan on Meta is 3-6 weeks. — UGC stat roundups: [Mean CEO](https://blog.mean.ceo/ugc-ad-performance-statistics/), [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/tiktok-ugc-ads-performance-data), [Spray](https://www.spray.marketing/insights/the-rise-of-creator-led-ads-why-ugc-is-beating-brand-creative) (vendors; original datasets not verified)
- Meta: partnership (creator) ads cut CPA 19% on average. Gen Z responds to creator-led ads, younger shoppers to deals and value, older shoppers to reviews and demonstrations. — [Meta for Business, Nov 2024](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/three-steps-to-optimize-your-performance-with-creative-diversification)
- **Format economics on Meta (Confect client averages, Apr 28 2025; sample size not stated):** carousel +33% ROAS and -18% cost per purchase vs the Meta average; single image/video -15% ROAS and +30% cost per purchase; collection -11% ROAS and +4% cost per purchase; catalog vs manual single ads +86% ROAS. — [Confect](https://confect.io/blog/single-image-ads-and-single-video-ads-on-meta). This conflicts with Motion's ranking of carousel as the worst asset type by hit rate (different metrics: ROAS vs spend-based "winner").
- **TikTok's own research (older, Oct 2020):** over 63% of the highest-CTR videos show the key message or product in the first 3 s. Tracks above 120 BPM drive higher view-through. Vertical 9:16 gives 25% higher 6-s watch-through. 1 in 3 top-VTR ads address the viewer directly. 40% of top-VTR ads use text overlays. Almost half tell emotional stories. 61% of best-performing auction ads use half or more of these 9 tips. — [TikTok for Business: 9 creative tips](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/th/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance) (dated 2020; flag as older)
- FTC Endorsement Guides: "An endorsement must reflect the honest opinion of the endorser." If an endorser reports exceptional results, the ad must make clear "what the generally expected results" are. This bears on before/after and testimonial styles. — [FTC: Endorsement Guides FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)

### Inferences
Ranked e-commerce styles for the app (one uploaded product photo plus brand info):

1. **Offer-first / headline static** ("Product image with text"). 4:5 feed and 1:1, plus 9:16 story. Big headline or discount, product hero, minimal design. Evidence: Motion's #1 format by spend share; "text only" and "product image with text" are the top asset types. Cheapest to generate and the most reliable starting template.
2. **Product demo** (product in use, how it works). 9:16 video, 10-20 s. Motion hit rate 8.1%. Needs believable motion of the real product; AI hands can drift (keep the label legible).
3. **Unboxing / package reveal.** 9:16, 8-15 s. Motion's highest hit rate (9.8%), but low volume, so less competition in the auction.
4. **Before / after (transformation).** Split frame or swipe transition, as static, 9:16 video, or a 2-card carousel. In Motion's 5-9% band and in Health & Wellness's top 10. Two meanings for the app: (a) "plain photo → polished ad" (a meta-style showing the glow-up) and (b) the product's result. (b) needs typical-results support under FTC rules; never fabricate skin or fitness results.
5. **Testimonial / review card.** Static quote card from real reviews, or a 9:16 UGC-style talking head. Motion hit rate 6.5%. An AI talking head must be disclosed and must not pose as a real customer (FTC rule; NY synthetic-performer law; Brandon's own CLAUDE.md rule).
6. **Us vs Them / split-screen comparison.** Static or 9:16. In the 5-9% band.
7. **Listicle / "3 reasons why" / feature-benefit callouts.** Static, video with text stickers, or carousel. In Motion's band and on Meta's own suggested list.
8. **Post-it note / letter / "unconventional text placement" / social-post or text-message mockup.** Native-looking statics with high spend-use ratios (1.3-1.7x). Very cheap to generate and they look "un-ad-like", so they are less likely to read as AI.
9. **Founder story / letter.** Video or static letter. Ranks high in Health & Wellness. Needs a real founder (voice or photo).
10. **Stitch / reaction / green-screen.** Tops Health & Wellness by hit rate. Hard to do credibly with AI people. Better as a template where the brand records itself over AI-generated product B-roll.
11. **Cinematic B-roll / "hyper-motion" product video.** In the band, and premium-looking. Fits Higgsfield. Risk: the "overly polished" look reads as AI (Q5).

- No reliable data on green-screen specifically.

## 4. Best-practice specs for 2026: aspect ratios, lengths, hooks, captions, safe zones, sound and music licensing, number of variations

### Takeaway
Make a 9:16 master (1080x1920, or 1440x2560 for Meta), keep text and logos inside the safe zone (Meta: 14% top, 35% bottom, 6% sides clear), and also produce 4:5 (feed) and 1:1 versions. The product or food and the key message go in the first 2-3 seconds. Add captions and on-screen text because many people watch muted, and end with a direct CTA. Use only commercially cleared music (TikTok Commercial Music Library; Meta Sound Collection), not trending sounds. Variation counts: about 20 live on Meta Advantage+ sales, 3-5 per TikTok ad group, at least 6 for TikTok Smart+.

### Cited Findings
- Meta Reels/Stories/9:16 feed safe zone: 14% top, 35% bottom, 6% each side (about 269 px / 672 px / 65 px on 1080x1920). Meta's recommended upload is 1440x2560. TikTok margins conflict between guides (one measured TikTok's file at 240 px top / 660 px bottom / 120 px sides). YouTube Shorts: one source cites Google's universal vertical template at 288 px top / 672 px bottom / 48 px left / 192 px right. Common ground across guides: 9:16 master, MP4/MOV H.264, text in the central area. — [Vidova](https://vidova.ai/blog/video-ad-specs-for-meta-and-tiktok); [AdsUploader: Meta safe zones](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones); [Reloop](https://reloop.so/blog/article/social-media-safe-zones/); [Blitzcut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/social-video-specs-2026) (secondary; Meta and TikTok templates not opened)
- TikTok official (Jun 2025): vertical 9:16, at least 720p, "use sound or music", keep content in the UI safe zone. First 3 s: introduce your proposition. First 6 s: prioritize the hook (suspense, surprise, emotion). Use captions or text overlays (the page says "5-10 words per second"; the wording is odd, so verify). Use transitions, stickers and graphics. End with a strong CTA. Feature creators, employees or customers. DIY, not overly polished. 3-5 creatives per ad group. — [TikTok Ads: Creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en)
- TikTok (2020): 63% of top-CTR ads show the key message or product in the first 3 s, and >120 BPM tracks lift view-through. — [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/th/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance)
- The widely repeated "TikTok ads of 21-34 s convert best / +280%" claim could not be traced to an official TikTok document. — [Mobile Marketing Reads](https://mobilemarketingreads.com/?p=20237); [Adcreate](https://adcreate.com/blog/video-ad-length-format-guide) (unverified attribution)
- Restaurant Reels: 10-20 s, food in the first 2 s, captions because most watch muted, ambient kitchen audio. — [adlibrary](https://adlibrary.com/posts/meta-ads-for-restaurants)
- Instagram Reel watch time rose from 4 s to 8.5 s (Metricool, 2025→2026), and 65% of Reel views arrive in the first three days. — [Showfolk summary of Metricool, Jun 2026](https://www.showfolk.ai/blog/instagram-carousels-vs-reels-reach)
- Music: businesses can't legally use TikTok's general trending sounds in ads or on business profiles. They must use the Commercial Music Library (CML), whose size is variously reported at 150K to 1M tracks. CML licences cover TikTok only, so the same audio on Meta, YouTube or the web needs separate rights. The Meta Sound Collection is cleared for "commercial or non-commercial purposes", including paid ads on Facebook and Instagram. TikTok's CML is also available in Adobe Express. — [Fohr](https://www.fohr.co/blog/how-to-find-royalty-free-sounds-music-for-sponsored-posts-on-tiktok); [Third Chair](https://usethirdchair.com/blog/tiktok-commercial-library-what-brands-can-really-use); [People First](https://join.peoplefirst.cc/tag/sounds); [TikTok Business blog: CML in Adobe Express](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/es/blog/adobe-express-creative-assistant) (mostly secondary)
- Variation counts (see Q1): Meta at least 20 diversified ads in Advantage+ sales, 20-30% of budget on testing, weekly launches ([Meta](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/three-steps-to-optimize-your-performance-with-creative-diversification)). TikTok 3-5 per ad group ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en)), at least 6 for Smart+ Web ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/best-practices-for-smart-plus-web-campaigns)).
- Meta's own Advantage+ tools can expand images to other aspect ratios and animate statics into video. Meta's Canva integration includes "Meta Design Check" with a safe-zone preview. — [Meta for Business: Creative differentiation](https://www.facebook.com/business/m/small-business/creative-differentiation)

### Inferences
- Default export pack per style: 9:16 video (6-15 s for food, 10-25 s for e-commerce demo or UGC), 9:16 static (story), 4:5 static (feed), 1:1 (marketplace and carousel cards). All text should sit inside the Meta 14/35/6 safe zone, which is roughly the strictest common denominator.
- Burn in captions, and keep audio swappable: ship with Meta Sound Collection- or CML-cleared tracks, or with licence-free ambient or ASMR sound. Never auto-attach "trending audio". Higgsfield's `tiktok_music_trending` / `tiktok_music_tune` tools should be checked for whether their tracks are CML-cleared before use in paid ads.

## 5. AI-generated ads: performance vs human-made, viewer reactions, and labeling/disclosure rules (Meta, TikTok, YouTube/Google, FTC, state laws)

### Takeaway
On performance, AI-made ads match or beat human ads on clicks, as long as they don't look AI-made. Meta's own data shows image generation lifting CTR 11% and conversion rate 7.6%. A large 2026 academic study on Taboola found AI and human ads statistically equivalent in matched tests, with ads that are perceived as AI underperforming whatever made them. Disclosing AI cut CTR about 31% in one field study, and US consumers are increasingly negative, especially toward AI-generated people (62% unacceptable even when disclosed). On rules: Meta auto-labels ("AI info") ads made with its gen-AI tools or carrying C2PA metadata. TikTok's Jul 2026 ad policy requires labeling realistic AI imagery, video, voices and avatars. The FTC's 2024 rule bans fake testimonials, explicitly including AI-generated ones, with civil penalties. New York (from Jun 9, 2026) requires conspicuous disclosure of "synthetic performers" in ads.

### Cited Findings

**Performance**
- Meta (Apr 2025; repeated Dec 2025): campaigns using Advantage+ creative image generation see on average 11% higher CTR and 7.6% higher conversion rate than campaigns that don't. Text generation gives +3% CTR. Ben & Jerry's got +7% link clicks from AI background generation in catalog ads. — [Meta for Business, Apr 2025](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/the-creative-advantage-unlocking-the-power-of-diversification-with-meta-andromeda); [Meta for Business, Dec 2025](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/demystifying-creative-diversification)
- Meta case studies (Nov 2024): Tinuiti for Unilever, text and image generation, +18% website purchase volume. ObjectsHQ, text generation, +60% ROAS. — [Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/three-steps-to-optimize-your-performance-with-creative-diversification)
- Zuckerberg on the Q3 2024 earnings call: businesses using image generation see about a 7% increase in conversions. One report on Q1 2026 says advertisers using video generation saw about a 3% higher conversion rate. — [MarketBeat transcript, Oct 30 2024](https://www.marketbeat.com/earnings/reports/2024-10-30-facebook-inc-stock/); [PPC Land, Q1 2026](https://ppc.land/meta-q1-2026-56-3b-revenue-as-ai-tools-double-advertiser-adoption/) (secondary)
- Columbia/Harvard/TUM/CMU with Taboola (announced Jan 28, 2026; 2025 live ads, 500M+ impressions, 3M clicks): raw CTR 0.76% for AI ads vs 0.65% for human ads, but "statistically equivalent" in matched "sibling ad" comparisons. Ads that did not look AI-made significantly outperformed both human ads and ads perceived as AI, and ads perceived as AI underperformed whether or not AI made them. About half of AI ads were perceived as human-made. AI "tells": overly polished visuals, heavy saturation, strong symmetry. Large, clear human faces signalled human-made and drove engagement. No evidence of lower downstream conversion. Food and drink performed strongly. Caveats: the data came from Taboola, which sells AI ad tools, and AI ads used faces more often. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-ads-perform-as-well-as-humans-if-they-dont-look-ai-made/); [Mi-3](https://www.mi-3.com.au/30-01-2026/ai-generated-ads-outperform-human-ads-click-through-rates-study-finds)
- NYU/Emory (Lee et al., reported Dec 20, 2025; Google Display Network field study): fully AI-generated ads had 19% higher CTR than human controls. AI-modified human designs (e.g., inpainted backgrounds or faces) showed no gain and sometimes did worse. Labeling an ad as AI-generated or AI-edited cut CTR by about 31.5% vs unlabeled human ads. The secondary report gives no sample sizes. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/telling-consumers-an-ad-is-ai-generated-cuts-clicks-by-31-percent-study-finds/)
- TUM working paper "AI in disguise" (Feb 2025): AI ads perceived as human-made got significantly higher CTR than both human ads and AI ads that looked artificial. About 59% of AI ads were perceived as potentially human-made. — [TUM](https://www.msl.mgt.tum.de/en/dm/news/article/new-working-paper-ai-in-disguise/)
- Kantar (Link AI for Digital): of 9 AI or partly-AI ads tested, 6 beat average on Demand Power contribution and 2 of 3 fully AI ads were in the top 30%. The spread was wide, with no pattern by degree of AI. Kantar calls it "efficiency without effectiveness?". — [Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/north-america/inspiration/analytics/the-future-of-generative-ai-in-advertising-efficiency-without-effectiveness)
- A claim that Nielsen 2025 found human creative +28% completion and +19% CTR for videos over 15 s could not be traced to Nielsen. — [Agency Reporter](https://agencyreporter.com/ai-generated-creatives-vs-human-ads-ab-testing/) (unverified)
- AdBeacon argues AI creative "wins clicks, loses conversions", and a trade claim says conversion dropped 8% for AOV over $100. Neither is verified against a primary study. — [AdBeacon](https://www.adbeacon.com/ai-creative-wins-clicks-loses-conversions/)

**Viewer and consumer reactions**
- Bentley-Gallup (n=3,270 US adults, May 4-11, 2026, published Aug 19, 2026): 79% saw ads in the past 30 days that looked AI-made. 49% view businesses' use of AI in ads negatively and 19% positively. 75% accept AI for brainstorming or early drafts if disclosed. 53% accept AI-created final text, images or video. 62% say AI-created people or voices in ads are unacceptable even when clearly disclosed (73% among ages 18-29). 66% of 18-29s are negative. — [Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/713222/americans-aren-sold-businesses-using-advertising.aspx)
- Harris Poll / 4As / Infillion (Jun 24, 2026): 78% say AI makes ads "feel less authentic", 63% would be "less likely to purchase from a brand that uses AI-generated ads", and 73% would be "less likely to trust an ad" they suspected was AI. No sample size was given. — [Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/harris-poll-ai-fatigue-less-trust-ai-generated-ads-cannes-lions)
- Clutch (Jun 2026, n=408): 33% say AI worsens their perception of a brand and 16% say it improves it. Gartner (Mar 2026, n=1,539): 50% prefer brands that don't use gen AI in consumer-facing content. — [Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/ai-in-branding); Gartner figure via search summary of the same result set (not opened)

**Platform labeling rules**
- Meta: ads created or significantly edited with Meta's gen-AI tools (Background Generation, Image Generation, Add Animation) are automatically labeled. Ads made with third-party tools are labeled when detected via C2PA metadata (e.g., Photoshop, DALL-E). The disclosure sits under "About this ad" in the three-dot menu. — [Marketing Dive (article dated Jul 9, 2026 per fetch)](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/sociable-meta-adds-updated-disclosure-tags-for-ai-generated-ads/824833/)
- Meta's mandatory advertiser self-disclosure applies to social issue, electoral and political ads with digitally created or altered media. Claims that Meta requires AI disclosure on all ads are contradicted by Meta's help pages, per one guide. Cropping, resizing and colour correction are exempt. One guide says photorealistic AI humans get a more prominent label beside "Sponsored" (unverified). — [Mockingbird](https://www.mockingbirdhouse.com/learning/ai-ad-creative/does-meta-allow-ai-generated-ads); [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-content-labeling-rules-advertisers-2026-reference); [AdGenz](https://www.adgenz.ai/blog/meta-ai-ad-disclosure-label-2026-guide) (secondary)
- TikTok (ad policy update Jul 21, 2026, per CTC): paid ads must label realistic AI-generated images or video, synthetic voices that could pass as human, "AI-generated product imagery presented as real photography", and digitally altered avatars in realistic human contexts. Disclose with the "AI-generated content" toggle in Ads Manager, or a visible text note or watermark where unavailable. Exempt: colour grading, basic audio mixing, non-AI VFX, AI-written scripts and captions. TikTok reads C2PA to detect AI. If it finds undisclosed AI the advertiser gets 24 h to respond, after which reach is limited, and repeat violations can lead to removal or an account ban. Political AI ads are banned outright. — [Common Thread Collective](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/tiktok-ai-ad-disclosure-rules-ecommerce-2026). Other guides describe an April 2026 "misleading and false content" ad policy requiring an AIGC label or a clear disclaimer, and disagree on whether non-realistic AI content counts. — [UGCVids](https://ugcvids.ai/blog/tiktok-ai-content-disclosure-rules-2026); [CineRads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ad-disclosure-requirements)
- YouTube (creator content): creators must disclose realistic AI-generated or meaningfully altered content with the "AI use" setting in YouTube Studio. Non-realistic or animated content, beauty filters, colour fixes and AI-written scripts are exempt. Labels may be auto-applied from C2PA. The page doesn't address ads. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- Google Ads: election ads must disclose synthetic or altered content, via an "Altered or synthetic content" checkbox since 2024. Reports of a 2026 extension to all commercial ads ("How this ad was made" in the Ad Transparency panel) are from vendor blogs and unconfirmed. — [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ads-altered-or-synthetic-content-37656.html); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-disclosure-rules-synthetic-content-political-ads-443868); [TryVizup](https://www.tryvizup.com/blog/google-ai-ad-disclosures-what-marketers-need-to-know) (unverified)

**Law (US)**
- FTC Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (announced Aug 2024; effective Oct 21, 2024): bans reviews and testimonials that misrepresent the author as someone who doesn't exist, "including AI-generated fake reviews", or that misrepresent the author's experience. Bans buying, procuring from insiders, or spreading such reviews when the business "knew or should have known". Bans sentiment-conditioned incentives, undisclosed insider reviews, fake "independent" review sites, review suppression, and buying fake followers or views. The FTC can "seek civil penalties against knowing violators", at up to $51,744 per violation at the time, possibly counted per fake review. — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials); [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://dwt.com/insights/2024/08/ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-consumer-reviews); [Hunton](https://hunton.com/hunton-retail-law-resource/ftc-issues-final-rule-targeting-fake-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials-including-those-generated-by-artificial-intelligence-ai-and-online-bots)
- The FTC's 2024 Rytr consent order (against an AI tool that generated "testimonials and reviews") was set aside on Dec 22, 2025 under Chairman Ferguson. The FTC said future AI enforcement will focus on deception, and the theory that fabricated reviews are deceptive has not been withdrawn. — [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/ftc-reverses-rytr-consent-order-amid-9123405/); [Hunton](https://hunton.com/hunton-retail-law-resource/ftc-brings-case-against-ai-generated-reviews-company-over-strenuous-dissent)
- FTC Endorsement Guides: endorsements must reflect honest opinions of people who actually used the product. Atypical results require disclosure of generally expected results. — [FTC FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)
- New York synthetic-performer law (S.8420-A/A.8887-B, signed Dec 11, 2025; Gen. Bus. Law § 396-b; effective Jun 9, 2026): any ad using a "synthetic performer" (digitally created media that appears to be a real person) must conspicuously disclose it. Wording and format are not specified. Audio-only ads, AI used only to translate a human performer, and ads for expressive works are exempt. Penalties are $1,000 for a first violation and $5,000 for later ones. — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/new-york-enacts-law-regulating-the-use-of-ai-generated-synthetic-performers-in-advertising); [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-29-new-york-enacts-synthetic-performer-disclosure-law-for-advertisements-including-those-using-generative-ai); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ny-synthetic-performer-law-goes-into-effect)
- State AI laws remain enforceable even where they conflict with the federal executive order on AI. — [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/ftc-reverses-rytr-consent-order-amid-9123405/)

### Inferences
- Best AI approach for the app: build from the business's real product or food photo (image-to-ad, background and scene generation, animation of the real item). Avoid fully synthetic people. Meta's strongest official gains come from image and background generation. Gallup's 62% "AI people unacceptable" and NY's synthetic-performer law make AI humans the riskiest element. The Taboola study's warning signs (over-polish, saturation, symmetry) argue for a "natural, phone-shot" rendering option, especially for food.
- One conflict to note: NYU/Emory found AI-edited human designs didn't help, while Meta reports gains from AI background generation (Ben & Jerry's +7%). Before/after "photo → polished ad" falls somewhere between "edited" and "generated", so it needs testing.
- Compliance features to build in: keep C2PA metadata (Meta and TikTok auto-detect it, and one guide warns that stripping it can be a violation); a one-tap "AI-generated" disclosure reminder per platform (TikTok toggle); an automatic "AI-generated person" disclosure overlay when a synthetic human appears (NY law); block any "customer testimonial" template unless the quote is pasted from a real review with its source; and before/after for health or beauty results only with substantiation.
- These all agree with Brandon's existing rule in CLAUDE.md (tell businesses samples are AI-generated; never present AI people as real customer testimonials).

### Gaps
- No platform-published head-to-head of AI video ads vs human video ads on Meta or TikTok. Meta's video-generation figure (+3% conversion rate) is secondhand.
- Effect of Meta's "AI info" label on performance: unknown. The 31.5% CTR drop is from an explicit label in a display-ad experiment, not Meta's menu-hidden label.
- IAB/Sonata (Jan 2026) reportedly found Gen Z negativity toward AI ads rose from 21% (2024) to 39%. I saw it only in secondary roundups and did not verify it.
- Florida-specific AI advertising laws, California's AI disclosure laws, and the EU AI Act Article 50 timing were not researched.
- The current inflation-adjusted FTC civil-penalty amount was not confirmed (the $51,744 figure is from 2024).
- TikTok's own policy page for the Jul 21, 2026 update was not opened (via CTC).
- FULLBEAUTY (AI backgrounds, reportedly +45% ROAS) and the "22% ROAS" Advantage+ figure are Meta-reported or secondhand. The 22% is for Advantage+ sales campaigns, not creative alone.

## 6. Carousels vs static vs video (Reels) on Instagram in 2025-2026, organic and paid

### Takeaway
Organic: Reels still reach more non-followers, but carousels now match or beat Reels on engagement and saves, and on Socialinsider's reach-per-follower measure, while single images have declined sharply. Paid: the evidence conflicts. Confect's client data shows carousels with the best ROAS and lowest cost per purchase, while Motion's spend-based "winner" analysis ranks carousel (and animation) last and text-forward statics first. For an app, the takeaway is to offer all three and let the algorithm choose, which CTC recommends for Meta's 2026 ranking system.

### Cited Findings
- Socialinsider: Instagram engagement rate by followers in 2025 was carousel 0.55%, Reels 0.52%, single image 0.37% (35M posts). The Q2 2026 update has 0.50% / 0.48% / 0.33%. Reach rate (reach ÷ followers; 872,075 posts, Jan 2025-Aug 2026; published Sep 3, 2026): carousel 4.50%, Reels 4.10%, image 4.00%. Reels still lead reach for accounts under 50K followers. Median saves were 37 (carousel) vs 35 (Reel) vs 10 (image), comments 25 vs 33, shares 3 vs 5. — [Showfolk summary of Socialinsider](https://www.showfolk.ai/blog/instagram-carousels-vs-reels-reach); [CreatorFlow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-content-formats-guide/)
- Metricool (Jun 2026; 24.4M posts, Jan-Feb 2025 vs 2026): Reels reach 29% more people than carousels, carousels get 20% more views per post than Reels, and carousel saves are 9x single images. Single-image reach fell 21.96% and engagement fell 45.98% year over year. Reel engagement rose about 25% and shares 67%. Average Reel watch time went from 4 s to 8.5 s. — [Showfolk summary of Metricool](https://www.showfolk.ai/blog/instagram-carousels-vs-reels-reach)
- Buffer (Mar 5, 2026; 52M posts): median engagement as a share of reach was carousel 6.90%, image 4.44%, Reels 3.31%. Buffer (Oct 2024; 2022-2024 data): Reels reached 1.36x carousels and 2.25x single photos (older). — [Showfolk summary of Buffer](https://www.showfolk.ai/blog/instagram-carousels-vs-reels-reach)
- Adam Mosseri (reported Oct 17, 2024): carousels often get more reach than photos partly because an unswiped carousel is shown again with its next slide. (May 2023): most Reels views come from accounts users don't follow, and sharing and full watch-through are key Reels signals. — [Showfolk](https://www.showfolk.ai/blog/instagram-carousels-vs-reels-reach)
- Paid (Confect client averages, Apr 2025): carousel +33% ROAS and -18% cost per purchase vs the Meta average; single image/video -15% ROAS and +30% cost per purchase. — [Confect](https://confect.io/blog/single-image-ads-and-single-video-ads-on-meta). Other blogs claim carousels are 20-50% cheaper per conversion than single image, possibly all tracing to one origin. — [Lebesgue](https://lebesgue.io/?p=2976); [AdStellar](https://www.adstellar.ai/blog/carousel-vs-single-image-ad)
- Paid (Motion 2026, Meta winners by spend): carousel ranks last among asset types by hit rate and by spend use ratio. Text-only and product-image-with-text statics rank first. — [Motion/Runneth: Top asset types](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types)
- Restaurant paid (unsourced agency ranges, 2026): Reels CTR 1.6-2.8% vs carousel 0.9-2.0% vs single image 0.8-1.9%, but delivery carousels had the lowest cost per order. — [adlibrary](https://adlibrary.com/posts/best-restaurant-meta-ads-examples)
- Statics are 55.6% of all Meta ads and 64.8% of DTC ads (Howland via Sicard). — [Taylor Sicard](https://taylorsicard.com/blog/meta-ads-creative-benchmarks-2026)
- CTC (Jul 2026): for Meta's Generative Recommender, mix video, carousel and image, since single-image statics give the system less signal. — [CTC](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/meta-generative-recommender-q2-2026-ecommerce)

### Inferences
- Organic Instagram for restaurants: Reels to reach new local people, carousels (menu, "3 things to order") for saves and engagement with followers. Single photos are now the weakest organic format.
- Paid: offer and headline statics are the cheapest reliable winners. Carousels suit menus and catalogs (delivery, multiple SKUs). Video suits awareness and "crave". An app that outputs one concept in all three formats lets the algorithm decide.
- The carousel conflict (Confect vs Motion) comes from different metrics: ROAS vs "share of spend". It is worth flagging rather than resolving.

### Gaps
- Most organic figures came via one aggregator (Showfolk). The primary Socialinsider, Metricool and Buffer pages were not opened.
- No 2025-2026 paid-media data isolates carousel vs video vs static for restaurants specifically.
- TikTok photo-mode carousels and YouTube Shorts formats were not compared.
