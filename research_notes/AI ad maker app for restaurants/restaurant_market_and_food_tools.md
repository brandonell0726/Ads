# Restaurant & Food-Truck Market for an AI Ad-Making App, and Restaurant-Specific AI Marketing Tools (as of Nov 2026)

## Q1. How big is the market (US, Florida, Central Florida), and what do restaurants earn and spend on marketing?

### Takeaway
The US has more than 1 million restaurant and foodservice outlets. Florida has about 48,900 eating-and-drinking locations (2025 data), and food trucks number somewhere between ~58,000 and ~92,000 depending on the IBISWorld edition. Margins are very thin: median 2.8% (full-service) and 4% (limited-service) in 2024, and 42–45% of operators were unprofitable in 2025. The usual marketing rule of thumb is 3–6% of sales, with independents nearer 2–3%.

### Cited Findings
**US restaurant industry (NRA, Feb 2026)**
- The NRA projects $1.55 trillion in 2026 restaurant and foodservice sales, with 1.3% real growth. It counts more than 1 million restaurant and foodservice outlets and projects 15.8M jobs in 2026. — [NRA press release, Feb 12 2026](https://www.restaurant.org/research-and-media/media/press-releases/persistent-cost-increases-and-enduring-demand-will-shape-the-restaurant-industry-in-2026); [Franchise Times](https://www.franchisetimes.com/franchise_news/national-restaurant-association-estimates-1-5-trillion-in-sales-for-2026/article_9b26d7d3-f9eb-40d3-b2ce-d12abcf56352.html)
- 42% of operators said their restaurant was not profitable in 2025, and 60% reported softer traffic. More than 9 in 10 cite food, labor, insurance, energy and swipe fees as significant challenges. — [NRA press release](https://www.restaurant.org/research-and-media/media/press-releases/persistent-cost-increases-and-enduring-demand-will-shape-the-restaurant-industry-in-2026); [WTOP](https://wtop.com/business-finance/2026/02/chief-economist-says-42-of-restaurants-did-not-turn-a-profit-last-year)
  - Internal inconsistency: the foreword of the same 2026 State of the Industry (SOI) report says "45% of operators... were not profitable in 2025", while its findings pages say 42%. — [NRA SOI 2026 PDF (watermarked copy hosted by WTOP)](https://wtop.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/SOI-2026-Report-Watermarked.pdf)
- 2024 median profit margins were 2.8% for full-service restaurants (down from 4% in 2019) and 4% for limited-service (down from 6%). Food and labor costs are up at least 35% since the pandemic, and menu prices are up 32% (citing the NRA 2025 Operations Data Abstract). — [NRA SOI 2026 PDF](https://wtop.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/SOI-2026-Report-Watermarked.pdf)
- "9 in 10 restaurants are small businesses." — [NRA Florida fact sheet (2025 data)](https://www.restaurant.org/research-and-media/research/industry-statistics/florida-state-fact-sheet)

**Florida and Central Florida**
- Florida had 48,907 restaurant (eating and drinking place) locations, $105.4B in restaurant and foodservice sales, 1,132,100 restaurant and foodservice jobs (855,000 of them at eating and drinking places), and the industry is the state's 3rd-largest private employer (2025 data, BLS/Census). — [NRA Florida fact sheet](https://www.restaurant.org/research-and-media/research/industry-statistics/florida-state-fact-sheet)
- Establishment counts for Central Florida congressional districts on the same sheet: FL-9 (Soto): 1,782 establishments / 41,217 employees; FL-10 (Frost): 2,237 / 47,945; FL-11 (Webster): 1,557 / 45,466; FL-18 (Franklin): 1,241 / 19,068; FL-7 (Mills): 1,600 / 25,293. — [NRA Florida fact sheet](https://www.restaurant.org/research-and-media/research/industry-statistics/florida-state-fact-sheet)
- Older or alternative Florida counts: 43,116 eating and drinking locations in 2023 (WifiTalents, secondary), and 66,017 "restaurants" from a Google Maps-based count that includes bars and coffee shops (not comparable). — [WifiTalents](https://wifitalents.com/florida-restaurant-industry-statistics/); [Snappy](https://gosnappy.io/blog/how-many-restaurants-in-florida/)

**Food trucks**
- IBISWorld counts are inconsistent across its free pages:
  - 92,257 food-truck businesses in 2025, after a 23.8% CAGR over 2020–25 — [IBISWorld number of businesses](https://img3.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/food-trucks/4322/)
  - Another snapshot shows 64,495 for 2025 — [IBISWorld](https://script.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/food-trucks/4322)
  - Restaurant HQ cites IBISWorld for 58,064 trucks in 2024 — [Restaurant HQ](https://www.therestauranthq.com/trends/food-truck-industry-statistics/)
- IBISWorld puts industry revenue at about $2.8B in 2025 (-0.2%) and $2.9B in 2026. Another edition shows $2.4B. — [IBISWorld market size](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-size/food-trucks/4322/)
- Other per-truck economics come from secondary sources and are untraced:
  - Average revenue of about $346,000 per truck (2024, attributed to foodtruckprofit.com)
  - Owner earnings of $24k–$153k/yr (FLIP Report 2024)
  - Profit margin of 6.2% (legionfoodtrucks.com)
  - About 1.2 employees per truck
  - 91% of trucks independently owned; no company holds more than 3% share
  - New truck with kitchen: $125k–$200k
  — [Restaurant HQ](https://www.therestauranthq.com/trends/food-truck-industry-statistics/)
- Groupon cites a 2–6% average food-truck margin, and one guide gives $20k–$42k in monthly revenue. Source quality is mixed, and much of it is vendor content. — [Groupon Merchant](https://www.groupon.com/merchant/managing-your-business/accounting-legal/food-truck-profit-margin); [CloudWaitress](https://www.cloudwaitress.com/restaurant-management/how-much-do-food-trucks-make-2025/)

DBPR's Division of Hotels and Restaurants issues the Mobile Food Dispensing Vehicle (MFDV) license, and F.S. 509.102 preempts local licensing, permits and fees, so one DBPR license covers the whole state. — [Fla. Stat. 509.102](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/509.102); [City of Palm Bay](https://www.palmbayfl.gov/government/city-departments-f-to-z/growth-management/land-development-planning/mobile-food-vending); [7shifts guide](https://www.7shifts.com/blog/food-truck-license-florida/)

**Marketing budgets**
- The common rule of thumb is 3–6% of sales on marketing, rising to 7–10% for new restaurants or growth pushes. Smaller independents typically sit at 2–3%, "some as high as six percent". One blog cites an NRA figure of about 3% of monthly sales. For a $1.5M/yr restaurant, 3–6% is about $45k–$90k/yr, and 60–80% of spend now goes to digital. These are vendor and agency blogs, not surveys. — [UpMenu](https://www.upmenu.com/blog/restaurant-marketing-budget); [FSR Magazine](https://www.fsrmagazine.com/expert-takes/how-much-money-should-you-spend-marketing-your-restaurant); [Sauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/2025/04/02); [DoorDash Merchants](https://merchants.doordash.com/en-us/blog/restaurant-marketing-budget)
- Restaurant social media agency pricing (agency and vendor sources, 2025):
  - Basic (1–2 platforms): $500–$1,500/mo
  - Standard (2–3 platforms, consistent posting): $1,500–$3,500/mo
  - Comprehensive (original content, ads, influencers): $3,500–$7,000+/mo
  — [PricingLink guide](https://pricinglink-static2.pages.dev/knowledge-base/restaurant-social-media-marketing/how-much-charge-restaurant-social-media)
  - Restaurant social agencies range from $500 to $10,000/mo — [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/agency/social-media-marketing/restaurants?page=35)
  - Low-end packaged offer: $500/mo for restaurants (Canada) — [Digital Main Street](https://digitalmainstreet.ca/product/500-month-social-media-management-for-restaurants/)

### Inferences
- The IBISWorld revenue and count figures don't reconcile with the "$346k average truck" figure. $2.8B ÷ 92,257 is about $30k revenue per business, roughly a tenth of $346k. Either the business count includes many tiny or part-time operators, or the $346k figure is inflated. Plan for a long tail of very small trucks with little cash.
- Implied marketing budget for a typical food truck: 2–3% of $250k–$350k is about $5k–$10k a year (~$400–$875/mo). With 2–6% net margins, the share available for a software subscription (as opposed to paid ads) is likely well under $100/mo for most trucks. This is an estimate, not sourced.
- In the five Central Florida districts listed (FL-7, 9, 10, 11, 18), the fact sheet shows about 8,400 establishments. These districts roughly cover Orlando, Kissimmee/Osceola, Lake and parts of Polk, but I didn't verify exactly which towns each district contains, so treat this only as a rough sense of local scale.

### Gaps
- No verified count of food trucks in Florida or Central Florida. The DBPR MFDV license list would give one, but I couldn't query it here.
- No authoritative per-restaurant "$ per month on social media" survey for small independents. The figures above are rules of thumb.
- No national count of eating-and-drinking establishments only (the NRA's "1M+ outlets" includes all foodservice).
- Toast/Square location counts were not retrieved.

## Q2. How do restaurant owners use and feel about AI for marketing, and what are their marketing pain points?

### Takeaway
Independent data (NRA 2026) shows modest real usage: 26% of operators use any AI-based tools, and 17% use AI for marketing (19% full-service, 15% limited-service). Marketing is the top AI use case. Vendor surveys report much higher numbers (Popmenu: 55% use AI to write marketing content; Toast: nearly 9 in 10 "experimenting"). The main pain point vendors document is the time it takes to create content. I found no survey giving a ChatGPT-specific share for owners.

### Cited Findings
**NRA SOI 2026 (independent)**
- 26% of operators use tools or technologies that incorporate AI. Marketing is the top AI area: 17% of all operators (19% full-service, 15% limited-service) use AI-driven tools in marketing. AI for administrative tasks is 10% and for menu optimization 7%. — [NRA SOI 2026 PDF](https://wtop.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/SOI-2026-Report-Watermarked.pdf)
- 56% of full-service and 64% of limited-service operators say technology had a significant impact on their marketing/advertising over the past 2–3 years. 8 in 10 say tech provides a competitive advantage, and 6 in 10 plan tech investments in 2026. — [NRA SOI 2026 PDF](https://wtop.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/SOI-2026-Report-Watermarked.pdf)
- Nearly two-thirds of operators say technology improves hospitality, but only 41% of consumers agree. — [NRA SOI 2026 PDF](https://wtop.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/SOI-2026-Report-Watermarked.pdf)

**Popmenu (vendor surveys)**
- 2026 survey (328 US operators, Jan 8–31 2026, plus 1,000 consumers): 44% already use AI and 25% plan to start this year ("69% adopting"). 55% use AI to create marketing content, and 87% use tech to automatically tailor marketing messages. — [Restaurant Technology News, Feb 2026](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/02/research-69-of-restaurants-are-adopting-ai-while-81-increase-digital-marketing-investment/)
  - Caveat: an aggregator flags the 55% as coming from "digitally active" operators. — [Duck-Hub stats roundup](https://www.duck-hub.com/blog/ai-in-restaurants-statistics)
- Earlier survey (359 operators, Nov 2024–Jan 2025): 34% had adopted AI and 48% planned to in 2025. — [Restaurant Technology News, Feb 2025](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2025/02/market-research-one-third-of-restaurants-have-already-adopted-ai-technology-48-plan-to-do-so-this-year/)
- 2024 survey (secondary citation): 58% of operators named creating marketing content among their most time-consuming tasks, and about 74% were open to using AI for it. — [Duck-Hub roundup](https://www.duck-hub.com/blog/ai-in-restaurants-statistics); [FSR Magazine sponsored (Popmenu)](https://www.fsrmagazine.com/sponsored_content/artificial-intelligence-is-getting-real-results-for-restaurant-marketers)

**Toast (vendor surveys)**
- 2025 survey (712 US operators, Apr–May 2025): 28% use AI to automate marketing, 27% for real-time insights, 26% to optimize menus. — [Toast "AI in restaurants"](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/data/ai-in-restaurants)
- 2026 survey (676 operators, Apr 2026): nearly 9 in 10 are experimenting with AI, 87% are comfortable with AI, 85% will use it more, and 42% experiment with AI only through their vendors. — [Toast restaurant AI statistics](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/data/restaurant-ai-statistics)
- Q1 2026 use of Toast's AI assistant by topic: sales/revenue 47%, menu/inventory 34%, guests and marketing 32%, ops/reporting 29%. — [Toast Q1 2026 AI/POS trends](https://pos.toasttab.com/zh-us/blog/data/q1-2026-restaurant-ai-pos-trends)

**Other surveys**
- TouchBistro 2025: 89% of US independent operators feel positive about using AI. — [TouchBistro via Silicon UK](https://www.silicon.co.uk/press-release/touchbistros-2025-state-of-restaurants-report-reveals-89-per-cent-of-u-s-independent-operators-feel-positive-about-the-use-of-ai)
- Square 2025 Future of Restaurants: 85% of restaurant leaders plan to invest in tech such as AI and automation. 81% of Canadian leaders see benefits in areas including marketing. — [Square press](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/future-of-commerce-us); [Square report page](https://squareup.com/us/en/townsquare/future-of-restaurants)

**Consumer side**
- 20% of consumers use AI tools (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity) to find restaurants, and 85% still use search engines. — [Popmenu 2026 via RTN](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/02/research-69-of-restaurants-are-adopting-ai-while-81-increase-digital-marketing-investment/)
- Reputation (Oct 2025) also found about 1 in 5 consumers use AI tools for venue discovery. — [Reputation survey](https://am590theanswer.com/technology/new-reputation-survey-reveals-how-ai-and-economic-pressures-are-rewriting-the-ru/67fd623dca9f4752a60de0405a587065)

**Owner sentiment**
- Owners fall into three camps: those who find AI "so easy to use that it's worth any backlash", those who don't realise it's turning customers off, and those who avoid it entirely. — [The Star, Oct 24 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/10/24/restaurants-let039s-talk-about-those-ai-menus-and-marketing-materials)

### Inferences
- The gap between NRA (17% use AI for marketing) and Popmenu/Toast (55% / "9 in 10") reflects sampling. Vendor surveys skew toward digitally engaged customers. For a cold-outreach food-truck audience, assume the NRA level of usage: a minority use AI, though most are open to it.
- "42% experiment with AI only through their vendors" (Toast) suggests many operators prefer AI built into tools they already pay for (POS, delivery app) over a new standalone app. That is a distribution headwind for an indie app.
- Time to create content is the pain point most consistently documented (Popmenu's 58%). It fits Brandon's observation that owners are "too busy to talk", and it argues for very low-effort input (one photo, one tap).

### Gaps
- I found no survey reporting the share of restaurant owners who use ChatGPT specifically.
- I couldn't retrieve SpotOn, Owner.com or Square survey details on marketing pain points.
- No owner surveys specific to food trucks on AI or marketing.
- Reddit owner sentiment was not retrievable (search returned vendor pages, not threads).

## Q3. How do restaurants buy SaaS (price tolerance, churn, seasonality, failure rates, done-for-you vs DIY, payments, language)?

### Takeaway
Established restaurant marketing platforms charge about $179–$499/mo per location, often with contracts, onboarding fees or minimum terms. Toast's newest product bundles AI with a human marketer, which signals that operators want marketing done for them. Restaurant-specific churn data isn't public. Generic SMB SaaS churn is high, and many small restaurants and trucks close. Florida's restaurant owners are heavily Hispanic: 34% of restaurant firms, double the national share.

### Cited Findings
**Price points of incumbent platforms**
- Popmenu:
  - Starter $179/mo, Essentials $299, Premier $499, Enterprise undisclosed — [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/popmenu)
  - A Popmenu page shows $179 ($159/mo prepaid annually) and $499 ($449 annually) — [Popmenu page](https://get.popmenu.com/ads-2/lorem-ipsum)
  - The AI Marketing add-on is about $300/mo/location on top of the base plan, plus about $1,300+ one-time onboarding, and there's no free trial (third-party estimate) — [Restolabs](https://www.restolabs.com/blog/popmenu-pricing)
- Owner.com: Flex $249/mo plus a 5% per-order fee, or Flat-Rate $499/mo. Both are month-to-month with a 5% guest fee. — [Sauce blog (competitor), Jan 2026](https://www.getsauce.com/post/owner-com-pricing-fees); [Owner pricing](https://www.owner.com/pricing?utm_category=sales)
- Toast IQ Grow (launched May 5, 2026) costs $499/mo. It bundles growth tools including social media marketing, an AI Marketing Agent in beta that designs campaigns with email, SMS and organic social assets, and a dedicated human marketer. Toast says restaurants working with a Marketing Success Manager grew sales more than 8% vs similar Toast restaurants (pilot, vendor claim). — [Toast newsroom](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/toast-debuts-toast-iq-grow-spring-release-2026)
- Marqii: full suite at $180/location/mo on an annual contract, including social management (Google Posts, Apple Showcase). Other listings show $74.99–$149.99 tiers. — [Marqii pricing](https://marqii.com/pricing); [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/compare-products/marqii)
- Square's referral page offers Popmenu AI Marketing with 6 months free, but it requires a 6-month purchase at the monthly rate. — [Square x Popmenu referral](https://squareup.com/us/en/campaign/popmenu-referral)

**Done-for-you vs DIY**
- Toast pairs AI with a human Marketing Success Manager. — [Toast newsroom](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/toast-debuts-toast-iq-grow-spring-release-2026)
- MenuPhotoAI offers both self-serve and "done-for-you": email your photos and the team handles it. — [G2 listing](https://ai.g2.com/marketplace/tools/menuphotoai)
- Popmenu's AI Marketing "automatically creates a full month of emails, texts, and social posts". — [Popmenu/Square partnership](https://contentenginellc.com/?p=462007)

**Churn and failure rates**
- No restaurant-SaaS churn data was found. Generic SMB SaaS benchmarks conflict:
  - One cites 3–7% monthly churn (30–58% annually)
  - Others cite ProfitWell/ChartMogul medians of about 6–10% annual gross revenue churn and 10–14% annual logo churn for SMB
  - Page-level attribution came from search snippets and wasn't verified
  — [UserJot benchmarks](https://userjot.com/blog/saas-churn-rate-benchmarks); [Focus Digital](https://focus-digital.co/average-churn-rate-by-industry-saas/); [Kalungi](https://kalungi.com/blog/saas-churn-rate-benchmarks)
- Food-truck failure rate: no reliable figure exists.
  - Toast says it couldn't find reliable data behind the oft-quoted 50–60% — [Toast blog](https://pos.toasttab.com/es-us/blog/on-the-line/food-truck-failure-rate)
  - Restaurant HQ cites "only 40% remain after three years" (foodparks.io, unverified) — [Restaurant HQ](https://www.therestauranthq.com/trends/food-truck-industry-statistics/)
  - Eposnow notes most sources borrow the restaurant figure (60% closed within 3 years) — [Epos Now](https://eposnow.com/ca/resources/what-is-the-average-food-truck-success-rate)

**Owner demographics and language (NRA data brief, Apr 2026, 2023 Census data)**
- Nationally, 49% of restaurant firms are minority-owned: 18% Hispanic-owned, 18% Asian-owned, 16% Black-owned. — [NRA Owner Demographics brief](https://www.restaurant.org/NRA/media/Research/Indicators/2026/NRA-Data-Brief-Restaurant-Owner-Demographics-April-2026.pdf)
  - An earlier NRA brief gave 16% Hispanic — [NRA demographics page](https://restaurant.org/research-and-media/u-s-restaurant-owner-demographics)
- Florida: 62% of restaurant firms are minority-owned, 34% Hispanic-owned, 23% Black-owned and 12% Asian-owned. 51% are at least 50% women-owned. — [NRA Owner Demographics brief](https://www.restaurant.org/NRA/media/Research/Indicators/2026/NRA-Data-Brief-Restaurant-Owner-Demographics-April-2026.pdf)
- 36% of US restaurant owners were born outside the US (vs 20% of all business owners), including 63% of Hispanic restaurant owners and 88% of Asian ones. 29% of restaurant owners are under 45, rising to 33% of Hispanic owners. — [NRA Owner Demographics brief](https://www.restaurant.org/NRA/media/Research/Indicators/2026/NRA-Data-Brief-Restaurant-Owner-Demographics-April-2026.pdf)
- Incumbents localize: Toast publishes Spanish (es-us), Vietnamese, Korean, Chinese and Thai versions of its US blog, and DoorDash has es-us merchant help pages. — [Toast es-us](https://pos.toasttab.com/es-us/blog/data/ai-in-restaurants); [DoorDash es-us help](https://help.doordash.com/es-us/merchants/article/menu-photos-with-smart-photo-tools)

### Inferences
- Willingness to pay has three levels in practice:
  - Free or near-free AI built into delivery apps and POS
  - $9–$29/mo self-serve photo tools
  - $179–$499/mo all-in-one platforms or $500+/mo agencies
  An app priced at ~$29–$99/mo would undercut platforms and agencies, but it must clearly beat the free incumbent tools.
- With high closure rates and thin margins, expect high logo churn. Month-to-month pricing (as Owner.com uses) with credit packs for slow months may suit food trucks better than annual contracts. This is an inference, not sourced.
- Bilingual English/Spanish is a meaningful differentiator in Florida, where 34% of restaurant firms are Hispanic-owned and many owners are foreign-born. The incumbents' Spanish support appears to be mostly help content, not Spanish-first ad creation. That last point is inferred and wasn't verified.

### Gaps
- No data on restaurant SaaS churn specifically (Toast/Popmenu don't disclose it in anything I found).
- No data on Florida restaurant seasonality or how tourism seasons in Orlando/Kissimmee affect marketing spend.
- No data on preferred payment methods (card vs ACH vs app-store billing) for restaurant SaaS.
- No survey quantifying done-for-you vs DIY preference.

## Q4. Which restaurant- and food-specific AI photo, video and marketing tools already exist, and what do they cost?

### Takeaway
There is a crowded tier of cheap AI food-photo enhancers ($5–$29/mo or credit packs: FoodShot, MenuPhotoAI, YumMate, PlatePose, Beautiful Food, DishPhoto and others), and some show tiny verified revenue. DoorDash and Uber Eats now give merchants AI photo retouching for free. DoorDash added a free merchant Video Library in May 2026. The all-in-one platforms (Popmenu, Owner, Toast IQ Grow, Marqii) focus on websites, email/SMS, listings and social scheduling rather than finished video ads. Video-specific "dish photo to video ad" tools are mostly general AI video tools with restaurant landing pages.

### Cited Findings
**AI food-photo enhancers and generators**
- **FoodShot AI**: upload a photo, pick a style, get commercial-use images. Third-party listings disagree on limits:
  - Starter $15/mo or $9/mo billed annually — [Capterra CA](https://www.capterra.ca/software/1077967/FoodShot-AI)
  - Up to 100 generations — [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/product/530147-FoodShot-AI)
  - 25 generations — [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/all-software/a/foodshot-ai/)
  - The vendor's own blog cites $15–$99/mo — [FoodShot blog](https://foodshot-studio.pages.dev/blog/food-photography-for-restaurants)
- **MenuPhotoAI**: 5 free edits, credit packs from $27 or subscriptions from $29/mo, commercial license, and a done-for-you option. It targets restaurants, cafes, bakeries, food trucks, ghost kitchens and caterers. — [Slashdot](https://slashdot.org/software/p/MenuPhotoAI/); [G2](https://ai.g2.com/marketplace/tools/menuphotoai); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/menuphotoai/about/)
- **YumMate**: from €29/mo, "Daily Menu" €59/mo, "Gourmet Menu" €99/mo. — [G2 listing](https://ai.g2.com/marketplace/tools/yummate-app)
- **PlatePose**: per-dish credit packages. — [WebCatalog](https://next.webcatalog.io/en/apps/platepose)
- **Snappy Menu** (iOS): free with in-app purchases. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6746658961)
- **Snappr AI for Menus**: generates images for menu items that lack photos, available by demo or API. Snappr claims it has worked with more than 138,000 restaurants. No public pricing. — [Snappr](https://snappr.com/ai/menus)
- Verified traction for two small entrants (via TrustMRR, Stripe-verified, as of Nov 4 2026):
  - **Beautiful Food** (US, founded Oct 2025): $160 all-time revenue, no active subscriptions. Plans run from $9.99 for 10 photos to $249.99 for 500. — [TrustMRR Beautiful Food](https://trustmrr.com/startup/beautiful-food)
  - **DishPhoto** (Netherlands, Dec 2025): $12 all-time revenue. Credits run from €4.99 for 20 to €49.99 for 400. — [TrustMRR DishPhoto](https://trustmrr.com/startup/dishphoto)
- Other launches seen with no details gathered: YummyPic (dish photo to menu image "in 60 seconds"), and "Restaurant Photos AI" (Nov 2025, photos and videos). Otter also maintains a list of AI food-photo tools. — [YummyPic via Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/35708009/yummypic-launches-ai-food-photography-platform-that-turns-basic-dish-photos-into-professional-menu-images-in-60-seconds); [Restaurant Photos AI release](https://business.minstercommunitypost.com/minstercommunitypost/article/abnewswire-2025-11-24-restaurant-photos-ai-launches-to-replace-costly-food-photo-shoots-with-professional-ai-photos-and-videos); [Otter list](https://tryotter.com/blog/restaurant-tips/best-ai-tools-for-food-photography)

**Delivery platforms (free to merchants)**
- **DoorDash, April 9 2025**: AI background enhancement "without changing the food itself", an AI camera in the Business Manager app giving framing and lighting feedback, and AI moderation that approves qualifying photos in about a minute. DoorDash claims menus with item photos see 44% higher monthly sales (vendor claim). — [DoorDash newsroom, Apr 2025](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/doordash-unveils-ai-powered-tools-to-enhance-online-menus-and-streamline-merchant-operations)
- **DoorDash, May 4 2026**:
  - AI Retouch (background, sharpness, lighting; preserves food "exactly as it appears")
  - AI Replate (adds dinnerware; doesn't change "the food item, quantity, or portion")
  - Match the Style (applies a reference image's look)
  - Edited images can be downloaded for other channels
  - A Video Library at no additional cost: merchants upload videos, tag menu items so customers can order from them, and track views and video-driven sales

## Q5. What are the legal and ethical risks of AI food imagery that doesn't match the real dish?

### Takeaway
The real exposure is false advertising plus platform rejection plus customer backlash. In US case law, food ads that overstate portions survive motions to dismiss (Burger King, S.D. Fla., 2025). DoorDash's photo rules reject AI-generated or "non-representative" images. Grubhub says it removes AI images. Delivery apps in other countries (Zomato, Just Eat) have pulled AI dish images after complaints and refunds. Lawyers say an "AI-generated" label doesn't cure a misleading image. Safe practice is to enhance or animate the real dish and never generate the food.

### Cited Findings
**US case law**
- Coleman v. Burger King (S.D. Fla., filed 2022). In May 2025, Judge Altman denied dismissal, finding it "plausible... some reasonable consumers could be deceived". Plaintiffs allege the ads show Whoppers about 35% larger with more than double the meat. In Nov 2025, nationwide class certification was denied (claims too disparate), so the case continues with individual plaintiffs. — [PetaPixel, May 2025](https://petapixel.com/2025/05/12/judge-says-most-burger-king-customers-could-be-deceived-by-its-ad-photos); [Insurance Journal, Dec 2025](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2025/12/01/849291.htm); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/burger-king-avoids-class-certification-in-suit-over-whopper-size)

**Legal commentary**
- Loeb & Loeb's Libby O'Neill (quoted in Fortune, Sept 2026):
  - Images convey claims about size, quantity, ingredients and quality even when nothing is stated
  - An "AI-generated" label "explains how the image was made; it does not correct a false impression about what is actually being sold"
  - Her advice: "Sell the product you depict, and depict the product you sell."
  — [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/newsevents/news/2026/09/restaurants-have-been-serving-up-fake-food-ads-for-decades-ai-slop)
- Coverage of the same story says the UK ASA treats AI images under existing rules and that an AI label doesn't remedy a misleading claim. It also cites the EU food-information regulation (1169/2011, Art. 7) and the FTC v. Colgate-Palmolive (1965) mock-up precedent. This is secondary coverage, and its description of the Colgate case looks garbled (see Inferences). — [Pasquale Pillitteri](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/18582/ai-slop-menu-ai-generated-food-photos-us-restaurants)

**Platform policies**
- DoorDash's "Non-representative" photo rejection covers images that look artificial, were AI-generated, or were heavily AI-modified so they're unlike the actual item. — [DoorDash photo rejection guide](https://get.doordash.com/en-ca/learning-center/photo-rejection)
  - The consumer FAQ says AI enhancements may re-plate or swap backgrounds but avoid altering food, ingredients or portion size — [DoorDash Photos FAQ](https://help.doordash.com/es-us/consumers/article/photos-faqs)
- Enforcement is inconsistent: 404 Media found AI food images live on DoorDash, while Grubhub said it removes AI images it finds (article date unclear). — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/email/57421524-fd79-4073-b6f2-e7fb1755dbe3/)
- Uber Eats has reportedly asked photo contributors not to submit AI-generated or heavily edited images (per TODAY, cited secondhand). — [Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/ai-video-for-restaurants-2026)
  - In 2023 Uber Eats was accused of AI-generating mismatched dish images, and Uber said it "does not use AI to add images to the app" — [PetaPixel, Dec 2023](https://www.petapixel.com/2023/12/05/uber-eats-is-ai-generating-images-of-food-and-gettting-them-wrong)

**Backlash cases**
- Zomato (India, Aug 2024) removed AI dish images. The CEO said they caused a "breach of trust", more refunds and lower ratings. — [Business Today](https://businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/zomato-to-ditch-ai-generated-food-images-after-customer-complaints-442158-2024-08-20)
- Just Eat (Oct 2026) apologized and paused AI-generated menu photos after errors such as bacon floating in a soda. — [PetaPixel, Oct 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/10/21/just-eat-food-delivery-app-apologizes-for-ai-generated-menu-photo-showing-bacon-floating-in-soda/)
- A San Francisco restaurant was defaced in July 2026 after displaying AI-generated food photos. — [PetaPixel, Jul 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/07/22/san-francisco-restaurant-defaced-over-ai-generated-food-photos/)
- A Japanese food truck ("kitchen car") apologized after AI signboard imagery implied cheese that the dish didn't contain (secondary source). — [Toy People](https://www.toy-people.com/en/?p=113343)
- US local coverage of the pushback: Columbia, SC restaurants weighing AI images amid backlash; Baltimore restaurants using ChatGPT for menu photos; Scripps News on customer pushback. — [Post and Courier](https://www.postandcourier.com/epaper/page-c3/page_f0ca58e6-8755-5327-aa7c-c07afcf87495.html); [The Banner](https://www.thebanner.com/culture/food-drink/chatgpt-ai-restaurants-food-photography-DRANOQEY2BCJRFY2FTMUOMFDG4/); [Scripps News](https://www.scrippsnews.com/science-and-tech/artificial-intelligence/customers-push-back-as-restaurants-use-ai-generated-content-to-promote-products)

**How tools handle "enhance" vs "generate"**
- DoorDash markets all three of its modes as food-preserving. — [DoorDash, May 2026](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/ai-powered-merchant-tools)
- Snappr openly generates images for items without photos, positioned as a supplement to photography. — [Snappr](https://snappr.com/ai/menus)
- Flowjam's rule: "The food must come from your kitchen. Everything around the food can come from AI." — [Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/ai-video-for-restaurants-2026)

### Inferences
- An app should hard-code "enhance or animate the uploaded real dish" and keep portion size, ingredients and garnish unchanged. It should flag or block generation of food that wasn't photographed. It should also offer a verification step ("does this match what you serve?"). These controls reduce the restaurant's false-advertising risk and the app's reputational risk.
- Because Coleman v. Burger King is in Florida federal court and is about burger size, a "new burger" ad that makes the patty look bigger is the exact pattern plaintiffs target. This is directly relevant to Brandon's example use case.
- From background knowledge, not verified this session: FTC v. Colgate-Palmolive (1965) concerned a simulated "sandpaper" shaving demo, not food. The FTC's classic food mock-up case is Campbell Soup (1970, marbles in soup to push solids up). The secondary source's description looks garbled, so verify before citing.
- AI video in which a dish visibly morphs or warps (cheese pulls, pours) is both a quality risk and a misrepresentation risk. Low-motion templates are safer.

### Gaps
- No FTC guidance specific to AI food imagery was found. The FTC's general Section 5 deception framework applies, but I didn't retrieve it here.
- The findings of the Oxford study on the appeal of real vs AI-generated food images weren't retrieved. — [Oxford Neuroscience listing](https://www.neuroscience.ox.ac.uk/publications/1859171)
- No Florida-specific statute or case on restaurant ad imagery (beyond Coleman, which is in Florida federal court) was researched.

## Q6. What ad content actually drives restaurant sales?

### Takeaway
Consumers overwhelmingly discover restaurants through social media (78% of adults, per the NRA 2026 report). TikTok-style short video drives visits and higher spend in agency surveys, and appetizing-looking food is the stated reason. Delivery platforms report that dish photos lift sales, and DoorDash now supports shoppable merchant video. Operators plan more limited-time menus (41%). Hard, independent case studies with sales numbers are scarce. Most figures are vendor claims.

### Cited Findings
- **Social discovery (NRA 2026)**: 78% of adults say social media is a good way to learn about local restaurants (Gen Z 82%, millennials 81%, Gen X 79%, boomers 75%). 73% say it's a good way to learn about foods they haven't tried (Gen Z 82%, boomers 58%). — [NRA SOI 2026 PDF](https://wtop.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/SOI-2026-Report-Watermarked.pdf)
- **TikTok (MGH agency surveys)**:
  - Nov 2024 survey of 1,142 US adult TikTok viewers: 58% visited a restaurant after seeing it on TikTok (38% in 2022), 64% learned about a restaurant there, and 52% spent more than usual after seeing a restaurant on TikTok — [MGH](https://mghus.com/insights/survey-tiktok-drives-restaurant-sales-2/); [MGH blog](https://www.mghus.com/blog/how-tiktok-influences-restaurant-dining-behavior)
  - In 2022, 72% said appetizing-looking food was the main driver — [MGH blog](https://www.mghus.com/blog/tiktoks-major-influence-on-todays-dining-behavior)
  - In 2021, 36% of TikTok users had visited or ordered after seeing a restaurant — [Restaurant Dive](https://restaurantdive.com/news/survey-36-of-tiktok-users-order-from-restaurants-after-seeing-them-on-the/607523)
  - MGH sells TikTok marketing, so these figures are promotional.
- **Photos on delivery apps**: DoorDash claims menus with item photos see 44% higher monthly sales (vendor claim). DoorDash's May 2026 Video Library lets merchants tag menu items in videos so customers order from what they see, and reports video-driven sales. — [DoorDash, Apr 2025](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/doordash-unveils-ai-powered-tools-to-enhance-online-menus-and-streamline-merchant-operations); [DoorDash, May 2026](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/ai-powered-merchant-tools)
- **LTOs and new items**: 41% of operators plan to introduce more limited-time menus in 2026, and 31% are considering variable pricing (up from 22%). 48% of consumers want to hear from restaurants at least weekly. 68% of consumers say they're cutting back on dining out, and average weekly restaurant spend fell to about $90 in Feb 2026, down $25 from June 2025. — [Popmenu 2026 via RTN](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/02/research-69-of-restaurants-are-adopting-ai-while-81-increase-digital-marketing-investment/)
- **Beverages**: 87% of full-service and 80% of limited-service operators say beverages can drive visits. The NRA notes that "food may still grab the spotlight on social media, but beverages are increasingly generating buzz". — [NRA SOI 2026 PDF](https://wtop.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/SOI-2026-Report-Watermarked.pdf)
- **Human-led marketing**: Toast's pilot of a human Marketing Success Manager reports +8% sales vs comparable restaurants (vendor). — [Toast newsroom](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/toast-debuts-toast-iq-grow-spring-release-2026)
- **Vendor video claims**: blogs claim video listings get 25–40% more orders. These come from tool sellers, not independent studies. — [Dreamina](https://dreamina.capcut.com/ai-video/ai-video-ads-for-restaurants); [Ponpon](https://ponpon.ai/blog/ai-video-for-restaurants)

### Inferences
- The strongest evidence-backed hook is the food itself looking appetizing in short vertical video, plus frequent "what's new" posts. An LTO or new-item ad style ("New burger, this week only") fits both operator plans (41% more LTOs) and consumer behavior (48% want weekly updates).
- Given the cost-squeezed diners in Popmenu 2026, value and deal formats (BOGO, combo price) are probably worth one of the ~5 styles. Toast's AI agent explicitly looks for "BOGO or holiday promotions".
- For food trucks, location and schedule posts ("Where we are today") are a plausible must-have format. No source quantified this.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) case study with before/after sales numbers for short-form video ads by a small restaurant or food truck was found.
- No data on paid local-targeted social ads (Meta/TikTok geo ads) ROI for restaurants.
- No food-truck-specific evidence on which content formats drive visits.
- No UGC or influencer ROI data specific to restaurants beyond the MGH TikTok surveys.

## Q7. Where are the gaps for an indie entrant?

### Takeaway
The open space is a low-cost, video-first, bilingual, food-truck-friendly ad generator that enhances or animates the real dish and never fakes it. It would sit between free delivery-app photo tools and cheap still-photo enhancers on one side, and $179–$499/mo platforms and $500+/mo agencies on the other. The main risks are commoditization by free incumbent AI, weak monetization in the photo-only niche, and high churn among small operators.

### Cited Findings (evidence supporting each gap)
- **Price gap**:
  - Cheap still-photo tools cost $9–$29/mo — [Capterra CA](https://www.capterra.ca/software/1077967/FoodShot-AI); [Slashdot](https://slashdot.org/software/p/MenuPhotoAI/)
  - Platforms cost $179–$499/mo plus add-ons — [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/popmenu); [Toast newsroom](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/toast-debuts-toast-iq-grow-spring-release-2026); [Sauce on Owner.com](https://www.getsauce.com/post/owner-com-pricing-fees)
  - Agencies start around $500/mo — [PricingLink](https://pricinglink-static2.pages.dev/knowledge-base/restaurant-social-media-marketing/how-much-charge-restaurant-social-media)
- **Video gap**:
  - Incumbent AI focuses on photos, email/SMS, websites and listings — [DoorDash, May 2026](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/ai-powered-merchant-tools); [Marqii](https://www.marqii.com/press/marqii-launches-social-management); [Popmenu/Square](https://contentenginellc.com/?p=462007)
  - DoorDash now hosts merchant video but expects merchants to supply it — [DoorDash, May 2026](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/ai-powered-merchant-tools)
- **Food-truck gap**:
  - 91% of trucks are independent, with about 1.2 employees each — [Restaurant HQ](https://www.therestauranthq.com/trends/food-truck-industry-statistics/)
  - Of the tools found, only MenuPhotoAI explicitly lists food trucks — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/menuphotoai/about/)
  - The platforms are built around websites and online ordering — [Sauce on Owner.com](https://www.getsauce.com/post/owner-com-pricing-fees)
- **Bilingual gap (Florida)**: 34% of Florida restaurant firms are Hispanic-owned, and 63% of Hispanic restaurant owners nationally are foreign-born. — [NRA Owner Demographics brief](https://www.restaurant.org/NRA/media/Research/Indicators/2026/NRA-Data-Brief-Restaurant-Owner-Demographics-April-2026.pdf)
- **Done-with-you demand**: Toast bundles a human marketer, and its pilot reports +8% sales. MenuPhotoAI offers done-for-you. — [Toast newsroom](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/toast-debuts-toast-iq-grow-spring-release-2026); [G2 MenuPhotoAI](https://ai.g2.com/marketplace/tools/menuphotoai)
- **Trust and compliance gap**: platform rules and backlash reward "real dish only" tools. — [DoorDash photo rejection](https://get.doordash.com/en-ca/learning-center/photo-rejection); [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/newsevents/news/2026/09/restaurants-have-been-serving-up-fake-food-ads-for-decades-ai-slop)
- **Monetization risk**: two small indie AI food-photo apps show $160 and $12 in all-time verified revenue. — [TrustMRR Beautiful Food](https://trustmrr.com/startup/beautiful-food); [TrustMRR DishPhoto](https://trustmrr.com/startup/dishphoto)
- **Distribution risk**: 42% of operators experiment with AI only through their existing vendors. — [Toast AI statistics](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/data/restaurant-ai-statistics)

### Inferences
- Positioning that fits the evidence: "Upload your real dish → get a week of vertical videos, carousels and captions, in English and Spanish, that look like your food on its best day. We never invent food." Optionally add a done-with-you tier, where Brandon reviews and adjusts the output or shoots local footage for Central Florida clients. This uses his local presence as a moat that national SaaS can't copy.
- Price hypothesis to test: about $29–$79/mo, or credit packs for seasonal or part-time trucks. That is under platform and agency pricing but above the photo-enhancer commodity tier, justified by video and by being finished and post-ready. No source validates a specific price point. It is an inference from the price ladder above.
- Selling self-serve alone to food trucks is likely hard: they're time-poor, have cash-thin margins and churn a lot. A hybrid (Brandon onboards in person or by WhatsApp, then the app handles weekly output) may convert better than cold self-serve. This fits Toast's human-plus-AI bet and MenuPhotoAI's done-for-you tier.
- Export presets that comply with DoorDash and Uber photo and video rules (no altered portions, landscape stills for menus, vertical for social) could be a concrete differentiator, since owners also list on delivery apps.

### Gaps
- Research limits: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. Lumy, BentoBox, ChowNow, Slang, MenuSpring, SpotOn, Yelp, Grubhub tools, Reddit owner threads, Florida food-truck counts, seasonality, payment preferences and Spanish-language competitor tools weren't researched. Several primary pages (Toast newsroom and AI stats, PetaPixel) returned 403 when fetched, so some details rely on search-result summaries of those pages.
- No direct evidence on food trucks' willingness to pay for a recurring ad subscription vs one-off purchases.
- No competitor found that is specifically bilingual (EN/ES) and video-first for restaurants. Absence of evidence isn't proof such a tool doesn't exist.
