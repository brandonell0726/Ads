# The "operator" model: what's true and what to expect

Checked 2026-11-12, for Brandon (elier.visuals). The model: find Instagram creators with an engaged audience and no product, build a digital product with them using Monetise's tools, sell it on Whop and split the revenue. Every point below has a source. Points that come from the program's own pages or affiliate reviews are marked as such. Dollar estimates are ours and show their inputs.

## Bottom line

- **The model is real, and some people make money with it.** The tools exist and Whop can split revenue automatically.
- **Building the product is the easy part.** Getting a creator to say yes, and then getting their audience to buy, is where most people stall.
- **Early money is usually modest.** Expect hundreds of dollars per launch at first, not thousands. Getting back the $1,995 will probably take several launches or several creators.
- **The operators who win often charge an upfront fee** on top of a 20–30% share.
- **Protect yourself with a written agreement**, so the creator can't cut you out after you've built the product.

## What the program actually gives you

- **The tools:** Monetise's official page lists Synthesise (product ideas, structure and pricing), Flozy (a CRM for creators and prospects), GhostWriter OS (AI agents for launch copy) and a "Shadow Operator Playbook" for launching creator-led products. ([consulting.com/monetise](https://consulting.com/monetise))
- **Creator mode:** Synthesise can take one Instagram handle, score product ideas and generate an ebook PDF. ListKit is automated creator outreach, preloaded with a "Handpicked 100" list. These details come from a reviewer who bought the program; that site earns affiliate commission. ([sidehustlesummitreviews.com](https://sidehustlesummitreviews.com/what-is-monetise/))
- **The AI does about half the work.** One long-term member says the output needs real editing before it can be sold. ([same review](https://sidehustlesummitreviews.com/what-is-monetise/))
- **Tool access lasts 12 months.** GhostWriter OS then renews at **$495 a year, non-refundable**. "Lifetime access" covers the course videos only. ([Terms](https://consulting.com/policy/terms))
- **Your earnings can be shown publicly.** If you join a Monetise leaderboard or competition, your earnings may be displayed unless you opt out in settings (Terms §8.11). Because the sales are partly the creator's, check that setting. ([Terms](https://consulting.com/policy/terms))
- **Expect an upsell.** "UpLevel" is offered to buyers at $3,800 or $5,400, with a "100 Pre-Qualified Creators List" and "44 spots" scarcity. The page doesn't say whether that list is shared between buyers. ([UpLevel page](https://consulting.com/uplevel/4db/offer-3800-kre))
- **Refund timing:** the 7-day refund runs from the exact date and time of purchase. Accepting any giveaway prize (such as the spin-to-win wheel) cancels your refund right. ([Terms](https://consulting.com/policy/terms))

## The good

- **Whop pays your share automatically.** Under Dashboard → Marketing → Affiliates → "Share your revenue", the store owner adds you and sets a percentage. Whop works it out after its fees and pays it into your Whop balance. ([Whop docs](https://docs.whop.com/affiliates/setup-rev-share))
- **Whop's fees are low:** 2.7% + $0.30 per US card sale, with no separate platform fee. On a $27 ebook that leaves about $25.87. ([Whop fees](https://docs.whop.com/fees))
- **Real examples exist.** These are picked by the seller or come from friendly reviews, so they're not typical:
  - Mishaal, the program's best-known operator, made about $35K in **gross** sales from ebooks for creators, keeping 20–25%. That's roughly $7–9K for him. His first ebook sold about $9K, of which $2K was his. ([ippei.com](https://ippei.com/monetise/))
  - Another operator got his first creator within a week. It then took about a month to the first sale, and his first profit was $450. He says he now makes about $4,500 a month. Some creators agreed, then changed their minds or blocked him. ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/drewlyy-lovable-app))
- **Your video skills are a real edge.** Most operators send a text DM. You can show up with a ready-made launch Reel.

## The bad, and what to expect

- **Selling is harder than building.** The most common complaint in reviews is "creating a product is simple, but selling it is not as easy as they say". One Trustpilot reviewer wrote: "They expected me to find my customers or pay for ads." ([ippei.com](https://ippei.com/monetise/), [Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/monetise.com))
- **You're not the only one pitching these creators.** The community is about 14,000 members, ListKit automates outreach, creator lists are sold, and copycat "shadow operating" courses are on Whop. Whop's own guide says popular creators are "flooded with DMs". Nobody has measured how crowded it is. ([Whop guide](https://whop.com/blog/what-is-growth-operating/))
- **40% is a high ask.** Gadzhi's free videos frame the operator's share as 20–30%. His own upsell page also shows deals at 30% and 50%, so 40% isn't impossible, but expect pushback. ([marksinsights.com](https://marksinsights.com/ai-shadow-operating/), [UpLevel page](https://consulting.com/uplevel/4db/offer-3800-kre))
- **The winners usually charge upfront too.** Examples from the program's own testimonials: "$6k up front + 30% rev share", "$3k + 20% rev share", a "$3,000 retainer". ([UpLevel page](https://consulting.com/uplevel/4db/offer-3800-kre))
- **The program's headline numbers can't be checked.** "$155M+ tracked" and the leaderboard totals are measured on Whop, which Gadzhi co-owns. They have no published method, and no typical result.
- **The 4.85-star rating is mostly first impressions.** Most reviews are written within days of buying, not after making money. ([Whop reviews](https://whop.com/monetise/reviews/))

### Realistic money (our estimate)

No platform publishes how many followers actually buy, so here is the per-sale math and an anchor:

- **You get about $10 per sale.** A $27 ebook leaves about $25.87 after Whop's fees, and 40% of that is about **$10.35**.
- **Typical creators in this size range sell modestly.** Stan's data on 80,000+ creators puts the 10K–100K follower tier at an **average of about $666 a month** in total sales. ([Stan 2026 report](https://stan.store/blog/state-of-the-creator-economy/))
- **One launch from a 50K creator:** using real Instagram reach numbers, that's anywhere from a handful to a few hundred sales.

| Sales in a launch | Your share at 40% |
|---|---|
| 10 | about $100 |
| 60 | about $620 |
| 200 | about $2,070 |

So getting back $1,995 will most likely take several launches or several creators. The fastest ways to raise the number are a higher price, an upsell, an upfront fee and more than one creator.

**On reach:** Stories reach only about **0.3%** of followers for 50–100K accounts. Feed posts, Reels and carousels reach about 3–6%. A launch that relies on a Story link sticker alone will reach very few people. ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram-stories-benchmarks), [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/Instagram-reels-stats))

## Protect yourself

1. **Get a written agreement before you build anything.** It should cover:
   - the split, and whether it's taken from gross sales or after fees
   - whose Whop store the product lives in, and who owns the files and the buyer list
   - who handles refunds, support and chargebacks
   - payment day, and read-only access to the sales numbers
   - a minimum amount of promotion from the creator
   - the term, how either side exits, and a "no cutting me out" clause with a tail payment
2. **Watch out for Florida's partnership rule.** In Florida, sharing **profits** can make you legal partners whether you mean to or not, and partners are each liable for the whole business's debts. Sharing **gross revenue** doesn't, by itself, create a partnership. So write your cut as a share of gross sales or as a fee for your services, and consider a Florida LLC ($125 to form). Have a lawyer look at your template once. ([Fla. Stat. 620.8202](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/620.8202), [620.8306](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/620.8306))
3. **Get the creator's written OK to use their name and face** on the sales page and ads. Florida requires consent for that. ([Fla. Stat. 540.08](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/540.08))
4. **Know who carries the risk on Whop.** The store owner controls payouts and is responsible for refunds, chargebacks and any negative balance. New accounts can have part of each sale held back as a reserve. New accounts are also capped at **$2,500 per checkout link** until Whop approves higher-ticket sales. ([Whop roles](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/team-management/manage-team-roles), [reserves](https://docs.whop.com/trust-and-safety/account-health/reserves), [higher checkout links](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/payment-processing/access-higher-checkout-links))
5. **Set Whop's marketplace affiliate rate on purpose.** If the product is listed on Whop's marketplace, any Whop affiliate can promote it at a **default 30%** commission unless you change it. ([Whop docs](https://docs.whop.com/affiliates/setup-global))
6. **Follow cold-email law.** A partnership pitch is still a commercial email under US law. Every one needs:
   - an honest sender and subject line
   - a line saying it's a business pitch
   - **your postal address** (a P.O. box works)
   - an opt-out you honor within 10 business days

   Penalties go up to $53,088 per email. So say plainly that it's a business proposal instead of "this isn't a sales pitch". Being upfront also builds trust. ([FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business))
7. **Instagram DMs:** someone who doesn't follow you can get only **one text-only message request** until they accept, so the first message has to stand on its own. Automated DMs and scraping break Instagram's terms. ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagram-fights-dm-spam-with-limits-on-text-and-photos-165013992.html), [Instagram terms](https://help.instagram.com/581066165581870))
8. **Never promise income.** Don't tell a creator "you'll make $X", and keep income promises off sales pages. The FTC goes after unproven earnings claims, and Whop requires them to be backed up. ([FTC notice](https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2021/10/ftc-puts-businesses-notice-false-money-making-claims-could-lead-big-penalties))
9. **No fake or AI testimonials.** The FTC's 2024 rule bans fake reviews, including AI-made ones. A Higgsfield AI person can't pose as a real buyer.
10. **Health and food safety:** Whop bans unproven health claims, supplements and therapy. Test recipes, add allergy notes and have a human check everything the AI writes. AI meal tools have produced dangerous recipes before. ([Whop rules](https://docs.whop.com/trust-and-safety/trust-safety-overview/what-is-not-allowed-on-whop))
11. **Copyright won't protect an AI-written book.** AI output alone, and plain ingredient lists, aren't protected, so your contract has to do that job. Building the product from the creator's own recipes, photos and voice also makes it worth paying for. ([Copyright Office report summary](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/us-copyright-office-issues-report-on-copyrightability-of-ai-assisted-and-generated-works))
12. **Taxes:**
    - Self-employment tax is 15.3% on top of income tax, paid quarterly.
    - Polk County requires a local business tax receipt (about $39 a year).
    - Trading as "elier.visuals" without an LLC needs a $50 fictitious-name registration.
    - Ask a Florida tax pro about sales tax on ebooks.

    ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes), [Polk County](https://www.polktaxes.com/services/local-business-taxes/), [Fla. Stat. 865.09](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/865.09))

## Tips that raise your odds

- **Pitch one specific idea**, drawn from the creator's own posts and comments: "your followers keep asking for your bread recipes, so here's a 20-recipe sourdough guide." Show a cover, a table of contents and a short Reel.
- **Pre-sell before you build.** A waitlist page or pre-orders show whether people will pay before you spend the time.
- **Make the launch content yourself.** Reels and carousels, not just Stories. This is your edge.
- **Collect buyers' emails** and plan an upsell or bundle, so one launch keeps paying.
- **Ask for a small upfront fee**, or start at 30% with a bump once sales pass a target.
- **Look like a real business:** a business email, a simple portfolio and a decent Instagram profile. Trust is the biggest barrier.
